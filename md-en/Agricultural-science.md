**Agricultural science** (or **agriscience** for short[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-1)) is a broad multidisciplinary field of [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology "Biology") that encompasses the parts of exact, natural, economic and [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science "Social science") that are used in the practice and understanding of [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture"). Professionals of the agricultural science are called agricultural scientists or [agriculturists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculturists "Agriculturists").

## History

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Agricultural_science\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

Main article: [History of agricultural science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_agricultural_science "History of agricultural science")

In the 18th century, [Johann Friedrich Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Friedrich_Mayer_\(agriculturist\) "Johann Friedrich Mayer (agriculturist)") conducted experiments on the use of [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum "Gypsum") (hydrated [calcium sulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_sulfate "Calcium sulfate")) as a [fertilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilizer "Fertilizer").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-JB_1840-2)

In 1843, [John Bennet Lawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lawes "John Lawes") and [Joseph Henry Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Henry_Gilbert "Joseph Henry Gilbert") began a set of long-term field experiments at [Rothamsted Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Research_Station "Rothamsted Research Station") in England, some of which are still running as of 2018.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-5)

In the United States, a scientific revolution in agriculture began with the [Hatch Act of 1887](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatch_Act_of_1887 "Hatch Act of 1887"), which used the term "agricultural science".[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7) The Hatch Act was driven by farmers' interest in knowing the constituents of early artificial fertilizer. The [Smith–Hughes Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith%E2%80%93Hughes_Act "Smith–Hughes Act") of 1917 shifted agricultural education back to its vocational roots, but the scientific foundation had been built.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-8) For the next 44 years after 1906, federal expenditures on agricultural research in the United States outpaced private expenditures.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ScienceForAg-9): xxi

## Scope

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Agricultural_science\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Scope")]

Agriculture, agricultural science, and agronomy are closely related. However, they cover different concepts:

- Agriculture is the set of activities that transform the environment for the production of animals and plants for human use. Agriculture concerns techniques, including the application of agronomic research.
- [Agronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agronomy "Agronomy") is [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development "Research and development") related to studying and improving plant-based crops.
- Geoponics is the science of cultivating the earth.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10)
- [Hydroponics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroponics "Hydroponics") involves growing plants without soil, by using water-based mineral nutrient solutions in an artificial environment.

## Research topics

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Agricultural_science\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Research topics")]

Agricultural sciences include research and development on:[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Bosso_2015-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Boucher_2018-12)

- Improving [agricultural productivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_productivity "Agricultural productivity") in terms of quantity and quality (e.g., selection of [drought-resistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drought_tolerance "Drought tolerance") crops and animals, development of new [pesticides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesticide "Pesticide"), yield-sensing technologies, simulation models of crop growth, in-vitro [cell culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_culture "Cell culture") techniques)
- Minimizing the effects of pests ([weeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weed "Weed"), [insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect "Insect"), [pathogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathogen "Pathogen"), [mollusks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusk "Mollusk"), [nematodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nematode "Nematode")) on crop or animal production systems.
- Transformation of primary products into end-consumer products (e.g., production, preservation, and packaging of [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_product "Dairy product"))
- Prevention and correction of adverse environmental effects (e.g., [soil degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_retrogression_and_degradation "Soil retrogression and degradation"), [waste management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_management "Waste management"), [bioremediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioremediation "Bioremediation"))
- [Theoretical production ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_production_ecology "Theoretical production ecology"), relating to crop production modeling
- Traditional agricultural systems, sometimes termed [subsistence agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_agriculture "Subsistence agriculture"), which feed most of the poorest people in the world. These systems are of interest as they sometimes retain a level of integration with natural ecological systems greater than that of [industrial agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_agriculture "Industrial agriculture"), which may be more sustainable than some modern agricultural systems.
- Food production and demand globally, with particular attention paid to the primary producers, such as China, India, Brazil, the US, and the EU.
- Various sciences relating to agricultural resources and the environment (e.g. soil science, agroclimatology); biology of agricultural crops and animals (e.g. crop science, animal science and their included sciences, e.g. ruminant nutrition, farm animal welfare); such fields as agricultural economics and rural sociology; various disciplines encompassed in [agricultural engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_engineering "Agricultural engineering").
