**Albert Sidney Johnston** (February 2, 1803 – April 6, 1862) was an American military officer who served as a [general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_officer "General officer") in three different armies: the [Texian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texian_Army "Texian Army"), the [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army"), and the [Confederate States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_Army "Confederate States Army"). He saw extensive combat during his 34-year military career, fighting actions in the [Black Hawk War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hawk_War "Black Hawk War"), the [Texas-Indian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas-Indian_Wars "Texas-Indian Wars"), the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War "Mexican–American War"), the [Utah War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_War "Utah War"), and the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"), where he died on the battlefield.

Considered by Confederate States President [Jefferson Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Davis "Jefferson Davis") to be the finest general officer in the Confederacy before the later emergence of [Robert E. Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_E._Lee "Robert E. Lee"), he was killed early in the Civil War at the [Battle of Shiloh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shiloh "Battle of Shiloh") on April 6, 1862. Johnston was the highest-ranking officer on either side killed during the war. Davis believed the loss of General Johnston "was the turning point of our fate."

Johnston was unrelated to Confederate general [Joseph E. Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_E._Johnston "Joseph E. Johnston").

## Early life and education

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")]

Johnston was born in [Washington, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_Kentucky "Washington, Kentucky"), the youngest son of Dr. John and Abigail (Harris) Johnston. His father was a native of [Salisbury, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury,_Connecticut "Salisbury, Connecticut"). Although Albert Johnston was born in Kentucky, he lived much of his life in [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas"), which he considered his home.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-1) He was first educated at [Transylvania University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transylvania_University "Transylvania University") in [Lexington, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Kentucky "Lexington, Kentucky"), where he met fellow student [Jefferson Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Davis "Jefferson Davis"). Both were appointed to the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy "United States Military Academy") at [West Point, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Point,_New_York "West Point, New York"), Davis two years behind Johnston.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth46-2) In 1826,[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB1911-3) Johnston graduated eighth of 41 cadets in his class from West Point with a commission as a [brevet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brevet_\(military\) "Brevet (military)") [second lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Lieutenant#United_States "Second Lieutenant") in the [2nd U.S. Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Infantry_Regiment_\(United_States\) "2nd Infantry Regiment (United States)").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Eicher322-4)

Johnston was assigned to New York and [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri "Missouri") posts. In August 1827, he participated in the expedition to capture [Red Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bird "Red Bird"), the rebellious Winnebago chief. Johnston later wrote: "I must confess that I consider Red Bird one of the noblest and most dignified men I ever saw\... He said: 'I have offended. I sacrifice myself to save my country.'"[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-:1-5) Johnston served in the brief [Black Hawk War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hawk_War "Black Hawk War") of 1832 as chief of staff to Brevet Brigadier General [Henry Atkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Atkinson_\(soldier\) "Henry Atkinson (soldier)"). The commander praised Johnston for "talents of the first order, a gallant soldier by profession and education and a gentleman of high standing and integrity."[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-:0-6)

## Marriage and family

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Marriage and family")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/China_Grove_Plantation.jpg/220px-China_Grove_Plantation.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:China_Grove_Plantation.jpg)

China Grove Plantation, Johnston's Texas home

In 1829, he married Henrietta Preston, sister of Kentucky politician and future Civil War general [William Preston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Preston_\(Kentucky_soldier\) "William Preston (Kentucky soldier)"). They had three children, of whom two survived to adulthood. Their son, [William Preston Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Preston_Johnston "William Preston Johnston"), became a colonel in the Confederate States Army.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7) The senior Johnston resigned his commission in 1834 to care for his dying wife in Kentucky, who succumbed two years later to [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth46-2)

After serving as Secretary of War for the Republic of Texas in 1838–40, Johnston resigned and went back to Kentucky.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB1911-3) In 1843, he married [Eliza Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliza_Griffin_Johnston "Eliza Griffin Johnston"), his late wife's first cousin. The couple moved to Texas, where they settled on a large [plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantations_in_the_American_South "Plantations in the American South") in [Brazoria County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazoria_County,_Texas "Brazoria County, Texas"). Johnston named the property "China Grove". Here they raised Johnston's two children from his first marriage and the first three children born to Eliza and him. A sixth child was born when the family lived in Los Angeles, where they had permanently settled.

## Texian Army

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=3 "Edit section: Texian Army")]

Johnston moved to Texas in 1836 and[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB1911-3) enlisted as a [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_\(rank\) "Private (rank)") in the Texian Army[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB1911-3) after the [Texas War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_War_of_Independence "Texas War of Independence") from the Republic of Mexico. He was named [Adjutant General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjutant_General_of_Texas "Adjutant General of Texas") as a [colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_\(United_States\) "Colonel (United States)") in the Republic of Texas Army on August 5, 1836. On January 31, 1837, he became senior [brigadier general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_general "Brigadier general") in command of the Texas Army.

On February 5, 1837, Johnston fought in a duel with Texas Brigadier General [Felix Huston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Huston "Felix Huston"), who was angered and offended by Johnston's promotion. Huston had been the acting commander of the army and perceived Johnston's appointment as a slight from the Texas government. Johnston was shot through the hip and severely wounded, requiring him to relinquish his post during his recovery.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-8)

Afterward, Johnston said he fought Huston "as a public duty... he had little respect for the practice of dueling." He believed that the "safety of the republic depended upon the efficiency of the army... and upon the good discipline and subordination of the troops, which could only be secured by their obedience to their legal commander. General Huston embodied the lawless spirit in the army, which had to be met and controlled at whatever personal peril."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-9)

Many years later, Huston said that the duel was "a shameful piece of business, and I wouldn't do it again under any circumstances... Why, when I reflect upon the circumstances, I hate myself... that one act blackened all the good ones of my life. But I couldn't challenge Congress; and President [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Houston "Sam Houston"), although a duelist, was too far above me in rank. Well, thank God I didn't kill him."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10)

On December 22, 1838, [Mirabeau B. Lamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirabeau_B._Lamar "Mirabeau B. Lamar"), the second president of the [Republic of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Texas "Republic of Texas"), appointed Johnston as Secretary of War. He defended the Texas border against Mexican attempts to recover the state in rebellion. In 1839, he campaigned against Native Americans in northern Texas during the Cherokee War of 1838–39. At the [Battle of the Neches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Neches "Battle of the Neches"), Johnston and Vice President [David G. Burnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_G._Burnet "David G. Burnet") were both cited in the commander's report "for active exertions on the field" and "having behaved in such a manner as reflects great credit upon themselves."[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-11) In February 1840, he resigned and returned to Kentucky.

## United States Army

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: United States Army")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/AlbertSJohnston.jpg/220px-AlbertSJohnston.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:AlbertSJohnston.jpg)

Johnston as commander of the Department of Utah. Portrait taken by [Samuel C. Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_C._Mills "Samuel C. Mills") at [Camp Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Floyd "Camp Floyd"), Utah Territory, winter of 1858–59

When the United States declared war on Mexico in May 1846, Johnston rode 400 miles from his home in [Galveston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galveston,_Texas "Galveston, Texas") to [Port Isabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Isabel,_Texas "Port Isabel, Texas") to volunteer for service in Brigadier General [Zachary Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachary_Taylor "Zachary Taylor")'s Army of Occupation. Johnston was elected as [colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_\(United_States\) "Colonel (United States)") of the 1st Texas Rifle Volunteers but the enlistments of his soldiers ran out just before the army's advance on [Monterrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey "Monterrey"), so Taylor appointed him as the inspector general of Brigadier General [William O. Butler's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Orlando_Butler "William Orlando Butler") division of volunteers. Johnston convinced a few volunteers of his former regiment to stay on and fight.

During the [Battle of Monterrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Monterrey "Battle of Monterrey"), Butler was wounded and carried to the rear, and Johnston assumed an active leadership role in the division. Future U.S. general, [Joseph Hooker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Hooker "Joseph Hooker"), was with Johnston at Monterrey and wrote: "It was through \[Johnston's] agency, mainly, that our division was saved from a cruel slaughter... The coolness and magnificent presence \[that he] displayed on this field... left an impression on my mind that I have never forgotten."[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-:0-6) General Taylor considered Johnston "the best soldier he had ever commanded."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-12)

Johnston resigned from the army just after the battle of Monterrey in October 1846. He had promised his wife, Eliza, that he would only volunteer for six months' service. In addition, President [James K. Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_K._Polk "James K. Polk")'s administration's preference for officers associated with the Democratic Party prevented the promotion of those, such as Johnston, who were perceived as Whigs:

> Authorized to appoint a large number of officers in the increased military force, raised directly by the United States, an unjust discrimination was made in favor of Democrats... Not one Whig was included, and not one of the Democratic appointees had seen service in the field, or possessed the slightest pretension to military education. Such able graduates of West Point as [Henry Clay, jun.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Clay_Jr. "Henry Clay Jr."), and William R. McKee, were compelled to seek service through State appointments in volunteer regiments, while Albert Sidney Johnston, subsequently proved to be one of the ablest commanders ever sent from the Military Academy, could not obtain a commission from the General Government. In the war between Mexico and Texas, by which the latter had secured its independence, Johnston had held high command, and was perhaps the best equipped soldier, both by education and service, to be found in the entire country outside the regular army at the time of the Mexican war. General Taylor urged the President to give Johnston command of one of the ten new regiments. Johnston took no part in politics; but his eminent brother, [Josiah Stoddard Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josiah_S._Johnston "Josiah S. Johnston"), long a senator from Louisiana, was Mr. Clay's most intimate friend in public life, and General Taylor's letter was not even answered.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Blaine-13)

He remained on his plantation after the war until he was appointed by later 12th president [Zachary Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachary_Taylor "Zachary Taylor") to the [U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army") as a [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_\(United_States\) "Major (United States)") and was made a paymaster in December 1849 for a district of Texas encompassing the military posts from the upper Colorado River to the upper Trinity River.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB1911-3) He served in that role for more than five years, making six tours and traveling more than 4,000 miles (6,400 km) annually on the Indian frontier of Texas. He served on the Texas frontier at [Fort Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Mason_\(Texas\) "Fort Mason (Texas)") and elsewhere in the western United States.

In 1855, 14th president [Franklin Pierce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Pierce "Franklin Pierce") appointed him colonel of the new 2nd U.S. Cavalry (the unit that preceded the modern [5th U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Cavalry_Regiment_\(United_States\) "5th Cavalry Regiment (United States)")), a new regiment, which he organized, his lieutenant colonel being Robert E. Lee, and his majors [William J. Hardee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_J._Hardee "William J. Hardee") and [George H. Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Henry_Thomas "George Henry Thomas").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB1911-3) Other subordinates in this unit included [Earl Van Dorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Van_Dorn "Earl Van Dorn"), [Edmund Kirby Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Kirby_Smith "Edmund Kirby Smith"), [Nathan G. Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_George_Evans "Nathan George Evans"), [Innis N. Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innis_N._Palmer "Innis N. Palmer"), [George Stoneman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Stoneman "George Stoneman"), R.W. Johnson, [John B. Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bell_Hood "John Bell Hood"), and [Charles W. Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_W._Field "Charles W. Field"), all future Civil War generals.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-14) On March 31, 1856, Johnston received a promotion to temporary command of the entire Department of Texas. He campaigned aggressively against the [Comanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comanche "Comanche"), writing to his daughter that "the Indians harass our frontiers and the 2nd Cavalry and other troops thrash them wherever they catch them."[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-15) In March 1857, Brigadier General [David E. Twiggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_E._Twiggs "David E. Twiggs") was appointed permanent commander of the department and Johnston returned to his position as colonel of the 2nd Cavalry.

## Utah War

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Utah War")]

As a key figure in the [Utah War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_War "Utah War"), Johnston took command of the U.S. forces dispatched to crush the [Latter Day Saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormons "Mormons") rebellion in November 1857. Their objective was to install [Alfred Cumming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Cumming_\(governor\) "Alfred Cumming (governor)") as governor of the Utah Territory, replacing [Brigham Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young "Brigham Young"), and restore U.S. legal authority in the region. As Johnston had replaced Brigadier General [William S. Harney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Harney "William S. Harney") in command, he only joined the army after it had already departed for Utah. Johnston's adjutant general, and future U.S. general in the Civil War, Major [Fitz John Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitz_John_Porter "Fitz John Porter") wrote: "Experienced on the Plains and of established reputation for energy, courage, and resources, \[Johnston's] presence restored confidence at all points, and encouraged the weak-hearted and panic-stricken multitude. The long chain of wagons, kinked, tangled, and hard to move, uncoiled and went forward smoothly."[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-16)

Johnston worked tirelessly over the next few months to maintain the effectiveness of his army in the harsh winter environment at Fort Bridger, Wyoming. Major Porter wrote to an associate: "Col. Johnston has done everything to add to the efficiency of the command – and put it in a condition to sustain the dignity and honor of the country – More he cannot do… Don't let any one come here over Col. Johnston – It would be much against the wishes and hopes of everyone here – who would gladly see him a Brigadier General."[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-17) Even the Mormons commended Johnston's actions, with the Salt Lake City *Deseret News* reporting that "It takes a cool brain and good judgment to maintain a contented army and healthy camp through a stormy winter in the Wasatch Mountains."[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-18)

Johnston and his troops hoped for war. They had learned of the [Mountain Meadows Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Meadows_Massacre "Mountain Meadows Massacre") and wanted revenge against the Mormons. However, a peaceful resolution was reached after the army had endured the harsh winter at Fort Bridger. In late June 1858, Johnston led the army through Salt Lake City without incident to establish [Camp Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Floyd "Camp Floyd") some 50 miles distant. In a report to the War Department, Johnston reported that "horrible crimes… have been perpetrated in this territory, crimes of a magnitude and of an apparently studied refinement in atrocity, hardly to be conceived of, and which have gone unwhipped of justice."[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-19) Nevertheless, Johnston's army peacefully occupied the Utah Territory. U.S. Army Commander-in-Chief, Major General [Winfield Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winfield_Scott "Winfield Scott"), was delighted with Johnston's performance during the campaign and recommended his promotion to brevet [brigadier general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_general_\(United_States\) "Brigadier general (United States)"): "Colonel Johns\[t]on is more than a good officer – he is a God send to the country thro' the army."[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-20) The Senate confirmed Johnston's promotion on March 24, 1858.

With regard to the relations established by Johnston with the Native American tribes of the area, Major Porter reported that "Colonel Johnston took every occasion to bring the Indians within knowledge and influence of the army, and induced numerous chiefs to come to his camp... Colonel Johnston was ever kind, but firm, and dignified to them... The Utes, Pi-Utes, Bannocks, and other tribes, visited Colonel Johnston, and all went away expressing themselves pleased, assuring him that so long as he remained they would prove his friends, which the colonel told them would be best for them. Thus he effectively destroyed all influence of the Mormons over them, and insured friendly treatment to travelers to and from California and Oregon."[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-21)

In August 1859, parts of Johnston's Army of Utah were implicated as participants in an alleged massacre at [Spring Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahsahwahbee "Bahsahwahbee"), a retaliation against an Indian massacre of an emigrant train to California. There are conflicting reports of the event and Johnston only referenced it in a November 1859 report to Scott. He wrote: "I have ascertained that three \[emigrant] parties were robbed, and ten or twelve of their members, comprising men, women, and children, murdered... The perpetrators of the robbery of the first party were severely chastised by a detachment of dragoons, under the command of Lieutenant Gay. The troops failed to discover the robbers of the last two parties that were attacked. They are supposed to be vagabonds from the Shoshonee (sic) or Snake and Bannack (sic) Indians, whose chiefs deny any complicity with these predatory bands. There is abundant evidence to prove that these robber bands are accompanied by white men, and probably instigated and led by them. On that account I am inclined to believe the disclaimer of the Indians referred to, of having any knowledge of the robberies or any share in the plunder." The only evidence of the massacre is the account of [Elijah Nicholas Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elijah_Nicholas_Wilson "Elijah Nicholas Wilson") (written in 1910, about 51 years after the incident) and oral histories.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-22)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-23)

In late February 1860, Johnston received orders from the War Department recalling him to Washington D.C. to prepare for a new assignment. He spent 1860 in Kentucky until December 21, when he sailed for California to take command of the Department of the Pacific.

## Slavery

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=6 "Edit section: Slavery")]

Johnston was a slave owner and a strong supporter of slavery. By 1846, he owned four slaves in Texas.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-24) In 1855, having discovered that a slave was stealing from the Army payroll, Johnston refused to have him physically punished and instead sold him for $1,000 to recoup the losses. Johnston explained that "whipping will not restore what is lost and it will not benefit the \[culprit], whom a lifetime of kind treatment has failed to make honest."[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-25) In 1856, he called [abolitionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States "Abolitionism in the United States") "fanatical, idolatrous, negro worshipping" in a letter to his son, fearing that the abolitionists would incite a slave revolt in the Southern states.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-26) Upon moving to California, Johnston sold one slave to his son and freed another, Randolph Hughes, or "Ran", who agreed to accompany the family on the condition of a $12/month contract for five more years of servitude. Ran accompanied Johnston throughout the American Civil War until the latter's death.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-27)

## American Civil War

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=7 "Edit section: American Civil War")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Albert_S._Johnston%2C_head_and_shoulders_portrait_%28cropped%29.jpg/220px-Albert_S._Johnston%2C_head_and_shoulders_portrait_%28cropped%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Albert_S._Johnston,_head_and_shoulders_portrait_\(cropped\).jpg)

Albert S. Johnston in Confederate Army uniform wearing Three Gold Stars and Wreath on a General's Collar

At the outbreak of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"), Johnston was the commander of the U.S. Army [Department of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Pacific "Department of the Pacific")[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB1911-3) in [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_and_the_Civil_War "California and the Civil War"). Like many regular army officers from the Southern United States, he opposed secession. Nevertheless, Johnston resigned his commission soon after he heard of the Confederate states' declarations of secession. The War Department accepted it on May 6, 1861, effective May 3.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-28) On April 28, he moved to Los Angeles, the home of his wife's brother [John Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Strother_Griffin "John Strother Griffin"). Considering staying in California with his wife and five children, Johnston remained there until May. A sixth child was born in the family home in Los Angeles. His eldest son, Capt. Albert S. Johnston, Jr. was later killed in an [accidental explosion on a steamer ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Ada_Hancock "SS Ada Hancock") while on liberty in Los Angeles in 1863.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-29)

Soon, Johnston enlisted in the [Los Angeles Mounted Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Mounted_Rifles "Los Angeles Mounted Rifles") (a pro-Southern militia unit) as a private, leaving [Warner's Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner%27s_Ranch "Warner's Ranch") on May 27.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-30) He participated in their trek across the Southwestern deserts to Texas, crossing the [Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River "Colorado River") into the [Confederate Territory of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_Territory_of_Arizona "Confederate Territory of Arizona") on July 4, 1861. His escort was commanded by [Alonzo Ridley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonzo_Ridley "Alonzo Ridley"), Undersheriff of Los Angeles, who remained at Johnston's side until he was killed.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-31)

Early in the Civil War, Confederate President [Jefferson Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Davis "Jefferson Davis") decided that the Confederacy would attempt to hold as much territory as possible, distributing military forces around its borders and coasts.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-32) In the summer of 1861, Davis appointed several generals to defend Confederate lines from the Mississippi River east to the Allegheny Mountains.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-33) Aged 58 when the war began, Johnston was old by Army standards. He came east to offer his service for the Confederacy without having been promised anything, merely hoping for an assignment.

The most sensitive, and in many ways, the most crucial areas, along the Mississippi River and in western Tennessee along the [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_River "Tennessee River") and the [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_River "Cumberland River") rivers[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-34) were placed under the command of [Maj. Gen.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_General_\(CSA\) "Major General (CSA)") [Leonidas Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonidas_Polk "Leonidas Polk") and [Brig. Gen.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_General_\(CSA\) "Brigadier General (CSA)") [Gideon J. Pillow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gideon_J._Pillow "Gideon J. Pillow"). The latter had initially been in command in Tennessee as that State's top general.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-35) Their impolitic occupation of [Columbus, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Kentucky "Columbus, Kentucky"), on September 3, 1861, two days before Johnston arrived in the Confederacy's capital of [Richmond, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_in_the_American_Civil_War "Richmond in the American Civil War"), after his cross-country journey, drove Kentucky from its stated neutrality.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-36)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-37) The majority of Kentuckians allied with the U.S. camp.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-38) Polk and Pillow's action gave U.S. [Brig. Gen.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_general_\(United_States\) "Brigadier general (United States)") [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant "Ulysses S. Grant") an excuse to take control of the strategically located town of [Paducah, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paducah,_Kentucky "Paducah, Kentucky"), without raising the ire of most Kentuckians and the pro-U.S. majority in the State legislature.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-39)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-40)

### Confederate command in Western Theater

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=8 "Edit section: Confederate command in Western Theater")]

On September 10, 1861, Johnston was assigned to command the huge area of the Confederacy west of the Allegheny Mountains, except for coastal areas. He became commander of the Confederacy's western armies in the area often called the [Western Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Theater_of_the_American_Civil_War "Western Theater of the American Civil War") or Western Military Department.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth51-41)[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Long,_p._116-42) Johnston's appointment as a [full general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_\(CSA\) "General (CSA)") by his friend and admirer Jefferson Davis had already been confirmed by the Confederate Senate on August 31, 1861. The appointment had been backdated to rank from May 30, 1861, making him the second-highest-ranking general in the Confederate States Army. Only Adjutant General and Inspector General [Samuel Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Cooper_\(general\) "Samuel Cooper (general)") ranked ahead of him.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-43) After his appointment, Johnston immediately headed for his new territory.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth52-44) He was permitted to call on Arkansas, Tennessee, and Mississippi governors for new troops. However, politics largely stifled this authority, especially concerning Mississippi.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth51-41) On September 13, 1861, Johnston ordered Brig. Gen. [Felix Zollicoffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Zollicoffer "Felix Zollicoffer") with 4,000 men to occupy [Cumberland Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_Gap "Cumberland Gap") in Kentucky to block U.S. troops from coming into eastern Tennessee. The Kentucky legislature had voted to side with the United States after the occupation of Columbus by Polk.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth52-44) By September 18, Johnston had Brig. Gen. [Simon Bolivar Buckner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Bolivar_Buckner "Simon Bolivar Buckner") with another 4,000 men blocking the railroad route to Tennessee at [Bowling Green, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Green,_Kentucky "Bowling Green, Kentucky").[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth52-44)[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-45)

Johnston had fewer than 40,000 men spread throughout Kentucky, Tennessee, Arkansas, and Missouri.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth53-46) Of these, 10,000 were in Missouri under [Missouri State Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_State_Guard "Missouri State Guard") Maj. Gen. [Sterling Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_Price "Sterling Price").[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth53-46) Johnston did not quickly gain many recruits when he first requested them from the governors, but his more serious problem was lacking sufficient arms and ammunition for the troops he already had.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth53-46) As the Confederate government concentrated efforts on the units in the East, they gave Johnston small numbers of reinforcements and minimal amounts of arms and material.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth55-47) Johnston maintained his defense by conducting raids and other measures to make it appear he had larger forces than he did, a strategy that worked for several months.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth55-47) Johnston's tactics had so annoyed and confused U.S. Brig. Gen. [William Tecumseh Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tecumseh_Sherman "William Tecumseh Sherman") in Kentucky that he became paranoid and mentally unstable. Sherman overestimated Johnston's forces and was relieved by Brig. Gen. [Don Carlos Buell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Carlos_Buell "Don Carlos Buell") on November 9, 1861. However, in his *Memoirs*, Sherman strongly rebutted this account.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-48)[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-49)[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-50)[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-51)

### Battle of Mill Springs

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=9 "Edit section: Battle of Mill Springs")]

[East Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tennessee "East Tennessee") (a heavily [pro-union region of the southern U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Unionist "Southern Unionist") during the Civil War) was occupied for the Confederacy by two unimpressive brigadier generals appointed by Jefferson Davis: [Felix Zollicoffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Zollicoffer "Felix Zollicoffer"), a brave but untrained and inexperienced officer, and soon-to-be Maj. Gen. [George B. Crittenden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_B._Crittenden "George B. Crittenden"), a former U.S. Army officer with apparent alcohol problems.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-52) While Crittenden was away in Richmond, Zollicoffer moved his forces to the north bank of the upper Cumberland River near Mill Springs (now [Nancy, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy,_Kentucky "Nancy, Kentucky")), putting the river to his back and his forces into a trap.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-53)[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-54) Zollicoffer decided it was impossible to obey orders to return to the other side of the river because of the scarcity of transport and proximity of U.S. troops.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth,_p._66-55) When U.S. Brig. Gen. [George H. Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._Thomas "George H. Thomas") moved against the Confederates, Crittenden decided to attack one of the two parts of Thomas's command at Logan's Cross Roads near Mill Springs before the U.S. forces could unite.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth,_p._66-55) At the [Battle of Mill Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mill_Springs "Battle of Mill Springs") on January 19, 1862, the ill-prepared Confederates, after a night march in the rain, attacked the U.S. soldiers with some initial success.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-56) As the battle progressed, Zollicoffer was killed and the Confederates were turned back and routed by a U.S. bayonet charge, their force of 4,000 suffering 533 casualties, while Crittenden's conduct in the battle was so inept that subordinates accused him of being drunk.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-57)[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-58) The Confederate troops who escaped were assigned to other units as General Crittenden faced an investigation of his conduct.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-59)

After the Confederate defeat at Mill Springs, Davis sent Johnston a brigade and a few other scattered reinforcements. He also assigned him Gen. [P. G. T. Beauregard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._G._T._Beauregard "P. G. T. Beauregard"), who was supposed to attract recruits because of his victories early in the war and act as a competent subordinate for Johnston.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-60) The brigade was led by Brig. Gen. [John B. Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_B._Floyd "John B. Floyd"), considered incompetent. He took command at [Fort Donelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Donelson "Fort Donelson") as the senior general present just before U.S. Brig. Gen. [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant "Ulysses S. Grant") attacked the fort.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-61) Historians believe the assignment of Beauregard to the west stimulated U.S. commanders to attack the forts before Beauregard could make a difference in the theater. U.S. Army officers heard that he was bringing 15 regiments with him, but this was an exaggeration of his forces.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth7278-62)

### Fort Henry, Fort Donelson, Nashville

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=10 "Edit section: Fort Henry, Fort Donelson, Nashville")]

Based on the assumption that Kentucky neutrality would act as a shield against a direct invasion from the north, circumstances that no longer applied in September 1861, Tennessee initially had sent men to Virginia and concentrated defenses in the Mississippi Valley.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-63)[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-64) Even before Johnston arrived in Tennessee, construction of two forts had been started to defend the [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_River "Tennessee River") and the [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_River "Cumberland River") rivers, which provided avenues into the State from the north.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth56-65) Both forts were located in Tennessee to respect Kentucky neutrality, but these were not in ideal locations.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth56-65)[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Long142-66)[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-67)[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-68) [Fort Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fort_Henry "Battle of Fort Henry") on the Tennessee River was in an unfavorable low-lying location, commanded by hills on the Kentucky side of the river.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth56-65) [Fort Donelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Donelson "Fort Donelson") on the Cumberland River, although in a better location, had a vulnerable land side and did not have enough heavy artillery to defend against gunboats.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth56-65)

Maj. Gen. Polk ignored the problems of the forts when he took command. After Johnston took command, Polk at first refused to comply with Johnston's order to send an engineer, Lt. Joseph K. Dixon, to inspect the forts.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth57-69) After Johnston asserted his authority, Polk had to allow Dixon to proceed. Dixon recommended that the forts be maintained and strengthened, although they were not in ideal locations, because much work had been done on them, and the Confederates might not have time to build new ones. Johnston accepted his recommendations.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth57-69) Johnston wanted Major [Alexander P. Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_P._Stewart "Alexander P. Stewart") to command the forts, but President Davis appointed Brig. Gen. [Lloyd Tilghman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Tilghman "Lloyd Tilghman") as commander.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Long142-66)[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth57-69)

To prevent Polk from dissipating his forces by allowing some men to join a partisan group, Johnston ordered him to send Brig. Gen. Gideon Pillow and 5,000 men to Fort Donelson.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth58-70) Pillow took up a position at nearby [Clarksville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarksville,_Tennessee "Clarksville, Tennessee"), and did not move into the fort until February 7, 1862.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-71)[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-72) Alerted by a U.S. reconnaissance on January 14, 1862, Johnston ordered Tilghman to fortify the high ground opposite Fort Henry, which Polk had failed to do despite Johnston's orders.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth71-73) Tilghman failed to act decisively on these orders, which were too late to be adequately carried out in any event.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth71-73)[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-74)[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-75)

Gen. Beauregard arrived at Johnston's headquarters at Bowling Green on February 4, 1862, and was given overall command of Polk's force at the western end of Johnston's line at Columbus, Kentucky.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-76)[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-77) On February 6, 1862, U.S. gunboats quickly reduced the defenses of ill-sited Fort Henry, inflicting 21 casualties on the small remaining Confederate force.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth7879-78)[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Long167-79) Brig. Gen. Lloyd Tilghman surrendered the 94 remaining officers and men of his approximately 3,000-man force, which had not been sent to Fort Donelson, before Grant's U.S. forces could even take up their positions.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth7879-78)[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-80)[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-81) Johnston knew he could be trapped at Bowling Green if Fort Donelson fell, so he moved his force to [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee "Nashville, Tennessee"), the capital of Tennessee and an increasingly important Confederate industrial center, beginning on February 11, 1862.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth79-82)[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-83)

Johnston also reinforced Fort Donelson with 12,000 more men, including those under Floyd and Pillow, a curious decision given his thought that the U.S. gunboats alone could take the fort.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth79-82) He ordered the fort commanders to evacuate the troops if the fort could not be held.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth80-84) The senior generals sent to the fort to command the enlarged garrison, Gideon J. Pillow and John B. Floyd, squandered their chance to avoid having to surrender most of the garrison[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-85) and on February 16, 1862, Brig. Gen. Simon Buckner, having been abandoned by Floyd[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-86) and Pillow, surrendered Fort Donelson.[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-87) Colonel [Nathan Bedford Forrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Bedford_Forrest "Nathan Bedford Forrest") escaped with his cavalry force of about 700 men before the surrender.[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-88)[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-89)[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-90) The Confederates suffered about 1,500 casualties, with an estimated 12,000 to 14,000 taken prisoner.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-91)[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Weigley111-92) U.S. casualties were 500 killed, 2,108 wounded, and 224 missing.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Weigley111-92)

Johnston, who had little choice in allowing Floyd and Pillow to take charge at Fort Donelson based on seniority after he ordered them to add their forces to the garrison, took the blame and suffered calls for his removal because a full explanation to the press and public would have exposed the weakness of the Confederate position.[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-93) His passive defensive performance while positioning himself in a forward position at Bowling Green, spreading his forces too thinly, not concentrating his forces in the face of U.S. advances, and appointing or relying upon inadequate or incompetent subordinates subjected him to criticism at the time and by later historians.[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-94)[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-95)[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-96) The fall of the forts exposed Nashville to an imminent attack, and it fell without resistance to U.S. forces under Brig. Gen. Buell on February 25, 1862, two days after Johnston had to pull his forces out to avoid having them captured as well.[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth86-97)[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-98)[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-99)

### Concentration at Corinth

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=11 "Edit section: Concentration at Corinth")]

Johnston was in a perilous situation after the fall of Ft. Donelson and Henry; with barely 17,000 men to face an overwhelming concentration of Union force, he hastily fled south into Mississippi by way of Nashville and then into northern Alabama.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-100) Johnston himself retreated with the force under his personal command, the [Army of Central Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Central_Kentucky "Army of Central Kentucky"), from the vicinity of Nashville.[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth86-97) With Beauregard's help,[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-McPherson,_p._406-101) Johnston decided to concentrate forces with those formerly under Polk and now already under Beauregard's command at the strategically located railroad crossroads of [Corinth, Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinth,_Mississippi "Corinth, Mississippi"), which he reached by a circuitous route.[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-102) Johnston kept the U.S. forces, now under the overall command of Maj. Gen. [Henry Halleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Halleck "Henry Halleck"), confused and hesitant to move, allowing Johnston to reach his objective undetected.[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-103) He scraped together reinforcements from Louisiana, as well as part of Polk's force at Island No. 10, and 10,000 additional troops under [Braxton Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braxton_Bragg "Braxton Bragg") brought up from Mobile.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-104) Bragg at least calmed the nerves of Beauregard and Polk, who had become agitated by their apparent dire situation in the face of numerically superior forces, before Johnston's arrival on March 24, 1862.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth95-105)[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-106)

Johnston's army of 17,000 men gave the Confederates a combined force of about 40,000 to 44,669 men at Corinth.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth95-105)[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-McPherson,_p._406-101)[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-107) On March 29, 1862, Johnston officially took command of this combined force, which continued to use the Army of the Mississippi name under which Beauregard had organized it on March 5.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-108)[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-109)

Johnston's only hope was to crush Grant before Buell and others could reinforce him.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woodworth95-105) He started his army in motion on April 3, intent on surprising Grant's force as soon as the next day. It was not an easy undertaking; his army had been hastily thrown together, two-thirds of the soldiers had never fired a shot in battle, and drill, discipline, and staff work were so poor that the different divisions kept stumbling into each other on the march.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-110)[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-111) Beauregard felt that this offensive was a mistake and could not possibly succeed, but Johnston replied "I would fight them if they were a million" as he drove his army on to Pittsburg Landing.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-112) His army was finally in position within a mile or two of Grant's force, undetected, by the evening of April 5, 1862.[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-113)[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-114)[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-115)[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-116)[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-117)

### Battle of Shiloh and death

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=12 "Edit section: Battle of Shiloh and death")]

Johnston launched a massive surprise attack with his concentrated forces against Grant at the [Battle of Shiloh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shiloh "Battle of Shiloh") on April 6, 1862.[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-118) As the Confederate forces overran the U.S. camps, Johnston personally rallied troops up and down the line on his horse. One of his famous moments in the battle occurred when he witnessed some of his soldiers breaking from the ranks to pillage and loot the U.S. camps and was outraged to see a young lieutenant among them. "None of that, sir", Johnston roared at the officer, "we are not here for plunder." Then, realizing he had embarrassed the man, he picked up a tin cup from a table and announced, "Let this be my share of the spoils today", before directing his army onward.[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-119)

At about 2:30 pm, while leading one of those charges against a U.S. camp near the "Peach Orchard", he was wounded, taking a bullet behind his right knee. The bullet clipped a part of his [popliteal artery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popliteal_artery "Popliteal artery"), and his boot filled up with blood. No medical personnel were on the scene since Johnston had sent his personal surgeon to care for the wounded Confederate troops and U.S. prisoners earlier in the battle.[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-civilwarlandscapes.org-120)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Mosler_sketch%2C_Death_of_Gen._Albert_Sidney_Johnston.jpg/220px-Mosler_sketch%2C_Death_of_Gen._Albert_Sidney_Johnston.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mosler_sketch,_Death_of_Gen._Albert_Sidney_Johnston.jpg)

[Henry Mosler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Mosler "Henry Mosler")'s drawing of the death of General Johnston

Within a few minutes, Johnston was observed by his staff to be nearly fainting. Among his staff was [Isham G. Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isham_G._Harris "Isham G. Harris"), the [Governor of Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Tennessee "Governor of Tennessee"), who had ceased to make any real effort to function as governor after learning that [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln "Abraham Lincoln") had appointed [Andrew Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Johnson "Andrew Johnson") as military governor of Tennessee. Seeing Johnston slumping in his saddle and his face turning deathly pale, Harris asked: "General, are you wounded?" Johnston glanced down at his leg wound, then faced Harris and said his last words in a weak voice: "Yes... and I fear seriously." Harris and other staff officers removed Johnston from his horse, carried him to a small ravine near the "Hornets Nest", and desperately tried to aid the general, who had lost consciousness. Harris then sent an aide to fetch Johnston's surgeon but did not apply a [tourniquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourniquet "Tourniquet") to Johnston's wounded leg. A few minutes later, Johnston died from blood loss before a doctor could be found. It is believed that Johnston may have lived for as long as one hour after receiving his fatal wound. It was later discovered that Johnston had a tourniquet in his pocket when he died.[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-civilwarlandscapes.org-120)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Johnston_Shiloh_Monument.jpg/220px-Johnston_Shiloh_Monument.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Johnston_Shiloh_Monument.jpg)

Monument to Johnston at [Shiloh National Military Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiloh_National_Military_Park "Shiloh National Military Park")

Harris and the other officers wrapped General Johnston's body in a blanket to not damage the troops' morale with the sight of the dead general. Johnston and his wounded horse, Fire Eater, were taken to his field headquarters on the Corinth road, where his body remained in his tent for the remainder of the battle. P. G. T. Beauregard assumed command of the army. He resumed leading the Confederate assault, which continued advancing and pushed the U.S. forces back to a final defensive line near the Tennessee river. With his army exhausted and daylight almost gone, Beauregard called off the final Confederate attack around 1900 hours, figuring he could finish off the U.S. army the following morning. However, Grant was reinforced by 20,000 fresh troops from [Don Carlos Buell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Carlos_Buell "Don Carlos Buell")'s [Army of the Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Ohio "Army of the Ohio") during the night and led a successful counter-attack the following day, driving the Confederates from the field and winning the battle. As the Confederate army retreated to Corinth, Johnston's body was taken to the home of Colonel William Inge, which had been his headquarters in Corinth. It was covered in the Confederate flag and lay in state for several hours.[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-121)

It is possible that a Confederate soldier fired the fatal round, as many Confederates were firing at the U.S. lines while Johnston charged well in advance of his soldiers.[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-122) [Alonzo Ridley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonzo_Ridley "Alonzo Ridley") of Los Angeles commanded the bodyguard "the Guides" of Gen. A. S. Johnston and was by his side when he fell.[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-123)

Johnston was the highest-ranking fatality of the war on either side and his death was a strong blow to the morale of the Confederacy. At the time, Davis considered him the best general in the country.[\[124\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-124)

## Legacy and honors

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albert_Sidney_Johnston\&action=edit\&section=13 "Edit section: Legacy and honors")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Albert_Sidney_Johnston_Tomb.jpg/220px-Albert_Sidney_Johnston_Tomb.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Albert_Sidney_Johnston_Tomb.jpg)

Johnston's [tomb and statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Albert_Sidney_Johnston_\(Texas_State_Cemetery\) "Statue of Albert Sidney Johnston (Texas State Cemetery)") by [Elisabet Ney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabet_Ney "Elisabet Ney") in the [Texas State Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Cemetery "Texas State Cemetery") in [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas")

Johnston was survived by his wife, Eliza, and six children. His wife and five younger children, including one born after he went to war, chose to live out their days at home in Los Angeles with Eliza's brother, Dr. [John Strother Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Strother_Griffin "John Strother Griffin").[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-125) Johnston's eldest son, Albert Sidney Jr. (born in Texas), had already followed him into the Confederate States Army. In 1863, Albert Jr. was on his way out of [San Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro,_California "San Pedro, California") harbor on a ferry after taking home leave in Los Angeles. While a steamer was taking on passengers from the ferry, a wave swamped the smaller boat, causing its boilers to explode. Albert Jr. was killed in the accident.[\[126\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-126)

Upon his passing, General Johnston received the highest praise ever given by the Confederate government: accounts were published on December 20, 1862, and after that, in the Los Angeles *Star* of his family's hometown.[\[127\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-127) Johnston Street, Hancock Street, and Griffin Avenue, each in [northeast Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Heights,_Los_Angeles "Lincoln Heights, Los Angeles"), are named after the general and his family, who lived in the neighborhood.

Johnston was initially buried in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans"). In 1866, a joint resolution of the [Texas Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Legislature "Texas Legislature") was passed to have his body moved and reinterred at the [Texas State Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Cemetery "Texas State Cemetery") in [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas"). The re-interment occurred in 1867.[\[128\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Monthly-128) Forty years later, the state appointed [Elisabet Ney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabet_Ney "Elisabet Ney") to design [a monument and sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Albert_Sidney_Johnston_\(Texas_State_Cemetery\) "Statue of Albert Sidney Johnston (Texas State Cemetery)") of him to be erected at the grave site, installed in 1905.[\[129\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-TSC-129)

The [Texas Historical Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Historical_Commission "Texas Historical Commission") has erected a historical marker near the entrance of what was once Johnston's [plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantations_in_the_American_South "Plantations in the American South"). An adjacent marker was erected by the San Jacinto Chapter of the [Daughters of The Republic of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daughters_of_The_Republic_of_Texas "Daughters of The Republic of Texas") and the Lee, Roberts, and Davis Chapter of the [United Daughters of the Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Daughters_of_the_Confederacy "United Daughters of the Confederacy").

In 1916, the University of Texas at Austin recognized several confederate veterans (including Johnston) with statues on its South Mall. On August 21, 2017, as part of the wave of [confederate monument removals in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Removal_of_Confederate_monuments_and_memorials "Removal of Confederate monuments and memorials"), [Johnston's statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Albert_Sidney_Johnston_\(University_of_Texas_at_Austin\) "Statue of Albert Sidney Johnston (University of Texas at Austin)") was taken down. Plans were announced to add it to the [Briscoe Center for American History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolph_Briscoe_Center_for_American_History "Dolph Briscoe Center for American History") on the east side of the university campus.[\[130\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-130)

Johnston was inducted to the [Texas Military Hall of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_of_Honor_\(Texas_Military\) "Hall of Honor (Texas Military)") in 1980.[\[131\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-131)

In the fall of 2018, A. S. Johnston Elementary School in Dallas, Texas, was renamed Cedar Crest Elementary. Johnston Middle School in Houston, Texas, was also renamed Meyerland Middle School. Three other elementary schools named for Confederate veterans were renamed simultaneously.[\[132\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-132)
