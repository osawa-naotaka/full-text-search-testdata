The **aardwolf** (***Proteles cristatus***[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-3)) is an [insectivorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insectivore "Insectivore") [hyaenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyena "Hyena") species, native to [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa "East Africa") and [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa "Southern Africa"). Its name means "earth-wolf" in [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans "Afrikaans") and [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language "Dutch language").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-5) It is also called the **maanhaar-jackal**[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-OED-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7) (Afrikaans for "mane-jackal"), **termite-eating hyena**[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-8) and **civet hyena**, based on its habit of secreting substances from its [anal gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anal_gland "Anal gland"), a characteristic shared with the [African civet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_civet "African civet").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ingo-9)

Unlike many of its relatives in the order [Carnivora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnivora "Carnivora"), the aardwolf does not hunt large [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal"). It eats [insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect "Insect") and their [larvae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larva "Larva"),[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-wff-10) mainly [termites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termite "Termite"); one aardwolf can lap up as many as 300,000 termites during a single night[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKoepfliJenksEizirikZahirpour2006-11) using its long, sticky tongue. The aardwolf's tongue has adapted to be tough enough to withstand the strong bite of termites.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh71-12)

The aardwolf lives in the [shrublands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrubland "Shrubland") of eastern and southern Africa – open lands covered with stunted trees and shrubs. It is [nocturnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnal "Nocturnal"), resting in [burrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrow "Burrow") during the day and emerging at night to seek food.

## Taxonomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Aardwolf\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: Taxonomy")]

The aardwolf is generally classified as part of the hyena family [Hyaenidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyaenidae "Hyaenidae"). However, it was formerly placed in its own family Protelidae.[\[nb 1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-14) Early on, scientists felt that it was merely mimicking the [striped hyena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striped_hyena "Striped hyena"), which subsequently led to the creation of Protelidae.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-15) Recent studies have suggested that the aardwolf probably diverged from other hyaenids early on; how early is still unclear, as the fossil record and genetic studies disagree by 10 million years.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-koe-16)[\[nb 2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-17)

The aardwolf is the only surviving species in the [subfamily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subfamily "Subfamily") Protelinae. There is disagreement as to whether the species is [monotypic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotypic_taxon "Monotypic taxon"),[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Woz-18) or can be divided into [subspecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspecies "Subspecies"). A 2021 study found the genetic differences in eastern and southern aardwolves may be pronounced enough to categorize them as species.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-19)

## Etymology

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Aardwolf\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Etymology")]

The [generic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus "Genus") name *proteles* comes from two words both of [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") origin, *protos* and *teleos* which combined means "complete in front" based on the fact that they have five toes on their front feet and four on the rear.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ingo-9) The specific name, *cristatus*, comes from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") and means "provided with a comb", relating to their mane.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ingo-9)

## Description

Aardwolf with tongue exposed. Note the prominent [papillae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingual_papillae "Lingual papillae")

The aardwolf resembles a much smaller and thinner [striped hyena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striped_hyena "Striped hyena"), with a more slender [muzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snout "Snout"), black vertical stripes on a coat of yellowish fur, and a long, distinct [mane](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mane "wikt:mane") down the midline of the neck and back. It also has one or two diagonal stripes down the fore and hindquarters and several stripes on its legs.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh33-20) The mane is raised during confrontations to make the aardwolf appear larger. It is missing the throat spot that others in the family have.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ingo-9) Its lower leg (from the knee down) is all black, and its tail is bushy with a black tip.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-w1-13)

The aardwolf is about 55 to 80 cm (22 to 31 in) long, excluding its bushy tail, which is about 20–30 cm (7.9–11.8 in) long,[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB-4)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-w1-13) and stands about 40 to 50 cm (16 to 20 in) tall at the shoulders.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Colliers-21) An adult aardwolf weighs approximately 7–10 kg (15–22 lb), sometimes reaching 15 kg (33 lb).[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ingo-9) The aardwolves in the south of the continent tend to be smaller (about 10 kg (22 lb)) than the eastern version (around 14 kg (31 lb)). This makes the aardwolf the smallest extant member of the Hyaenidae family.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh33-20) The front feet have five toes each, unlike the four-toed hyena.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB-4)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-22) The skull is similar in shape to those of other hyenas, though much smaller,[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Colliers-21) and its cheek teeth are specialised for eating insects.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB-4) It still has canines, but unlike other hyenas, these teeth are used primarily for fighting and defense.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-w1-13) Its ears, which are large,[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-w1-13) are very similar to those of the striped hyena.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ingo-9)

As an aardwolf ages, it will typically lose some of its teeth, though this has little impact on its feeding habits due to the softness of the insects that it eats.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-wff-10)

## Distribution and habitat

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Aardwolf\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Distribution and habitat")]

Aardwolves live in open, dry plains and [bushland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushland "Bushland"), avoiding mountainous areas.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-w1-13) Due to their specific food requirements, they are found only in regions where termites of the family [Hodotermitidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodotermitidae "Hodotermitidae") occur. Termites of this family depend on dead and withered grass and are most populous in heavily grazed [grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland "Grassland") and [savannahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah "Savannah"), including [farmland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land "Arable land"). For most of the year, aardwolves spend time in shared territories consisting of up to a dozen dens, which are occupied for six weeks at a time.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-wff-10)

There are two distinct populations: one in Southern Africa, and another in East and Northeast Africa. The species does not occur in the intermediary [miombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miombo "Miombo") forests.

An adult pair, along with their most-recent offspring, occupies a territory of 1–4 km2 (0.39–1.54 sq mi).[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23)

## Behavior and ecology

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Aardwolf\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Behavior and ecology")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Aardwolf.jpg/220px-Aardwolf.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Aardwolf.jpg)

Aardwolf at the [San Antonio Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Zoo_and_Aquarium "San Antonio Zoo and Aquarium")

Aardwolves are shy and [nocturnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnal_animal "Nocturnal animal"), sleeping in burrows by day.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB-4) They will, on occasion during the winter, become diurnal feeders. This happens during the coldest periods as they then stay in at night to conserve heat.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-br30-24)

They are primarily solitary animals, though during mating season they form [monogamous pairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogamous_pairing_in_animals "Monogamous pairing in animals") which occupy a territory with their young.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Richardson-25)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-koel-26) If their territory is infringed upon by another aardwolf, they will chase the intruder away for up to 400 m (1,300 ft)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) or to the border.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23) If the intruder is caught, which rarely happens,[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23) a fight will occur, which is accompanied by soft clucking,[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-br31-28) hoarse barking, and a type of roar.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) The majority of incursions occur during mating season, when they can occur once or twice per week.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) When food is scarce, the stringent territorial system may be abandoned and as many as three pairs may occupy a single territory.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27)

The territory is marked by both sexes, as they both have developed anal glands from which they extrude a black substance that is smeared on rocks or grass stalks in 5-millimetre (0.20 in)-long streaks.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) Aardwolves also have scent glands on the forefoot and penile pad.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-29) They often mark near termite mounds within their territory every 20 minutes or so. If they are patrolling their territorial boundaries, the marking frequency increases drastically, to once every 50 m (160 ft). At this rate, an individual may mark 60 marks per hour,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) and upwards of 200 per night.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23)

An aardwolf pair's territory may have up to 10 dens, and numerous [middens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midden#Other_definitions "Midden") where they dig small holes and bury their feces with sand.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-30) Their dens are usually abandoned [aardvark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aardvark "Aardvark"), [springhare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springhare "Springhare"), or [porcupine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World_porcupine "Old World porcupine") dens,[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-br31-28) or on occasion they are crevices in rocks. They will also dig their own dens, or enlarge dens started by [springhares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springhare "Springhare").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) They typically will only use one or two dens at a time, rotating through all of their dens every six months. During the summer, they may rest outside their den during the night and sleep underground during the heat of the day.

Aardwolves are not fast runners nor are they particularly adept at fighting off predators. Therefore, when threatened, the aardwolf may attempt to mislead its foe by doubling back on its tracks. If confronted, it may raise its mane in an attempt to appear more menacing. It also emits a foul-smelling liquid from its anal glands.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Colliers-21)

### Feeding

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Aardwolf\&action=edit\&section=6 "Edit section: Feeding")]

The aardwolf feeds primarily on termites and more specifically on *[Trinervitermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinervitermes "Trinervitermes")*.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh71-12) This genus of termites has different species throughout the aardwolf's range. In East Africa, they eat *[Trinervitermes bettonianus](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Trinervitermes_bettonianus\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Trinervitermes bettonianus (page does not exist)")*, in central Africa, they eat *[Trinervitermes rhodesiensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Trinervitermes_rhodesiensis\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Trinervitermes rhodesiensis (page does not exist)")*, and in southern Africa, they eat *[T. trinervoides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinervitermes_trinervoides "Trinervitermes trinervoides")*.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB-4)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh71-12)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) Their technique consists of licking them off the ground as opposed to the [aardvark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aardvark "Aardvark"), which digs into the mound.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-br30-24) They locate their food by sound and also from the scent secreted by the soldier termites.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) An aardwolf may consume up to 250,000 [termites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termites "Termites") per night using its long, broad, sticky tongue.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh71-12)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-wff-10)

They do not destroy the termite mound or consume the entire colony, thus ensuring that the termites can rebuild and provide a continuous supply of food. They often memorize the location of such nests and return to them every few months.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-br31-28) During certain seasonal events, such as the onset of the rainy season and the cold of midwinter, the primary termites become scarce, so the need for other foods becomes pronounced. During these times, the southern aardwolf will seek out *[Hodotermes mossambicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hodotermes_mossambicus\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Hodotermes mossambicus (page does not exist)")*, a type of [harvester termite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvester_termite "Harvester termite")[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) active in the afternoon, which explains some of their diurnal behavior in the winter.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh71-12) The eastern aardwolf, during the rainy season, subsists on termites from the genera *[Odontotermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odontotermes "Odontotermes")* and *[Macrotermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrotermes "Macrotermes")*.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh71-12) They are also known to feed on other insects and larvae, and, some sources mention, occasionally eggs, small mammals and birds, but these constitute a very small percentage of their total diet.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) They use their wide tongues to lap surface foraging termites off of the ground and consume large quantities of sand in the process, which aids in digestion in the absence of teeth to break down their food.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Anderson1992-31)

Unlike other [hyenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyena "Hyena"), aardwolves do not scavenge or kill larger animals.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-w1-13)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-br31-28) Contrary to popular myths, aardwolves do not eat carrion, and if they are seen eating while hunched over a dead carcass, they are actually eating larvae and beetles.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-w1-13) Also, contrary to some sources, they do not like meat, unless it is finely ground or cooked for them.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-w1-13) The adult aardwolf was formerly assumed to forage in small groups,[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Colliers-21) but more recent research has shown that they are primarily solitary foragers,[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-koel-26) necessary because of the scarcity of their insect prey. Their primary source, *Trinervitermes*, forages in small but dense patches of 25–100 cm (9.8–39.4 in).[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) While foraging, the aardwolf can cover about 1 km (0.62 mi) per hour, which translates to 8–12 km (5.0–7.5 mi) per summer night and 3–8 km (1.9–5.0 mi) per winter night.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-w1-13)

### Breeding

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Aardwolf\&action=edit\&section=7 "Edit section: Breeding")]

The [breeding season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breeding_season "Breeding season") varies depending on location, but normally takes place during autumn or spring. In South Africa, breeding occurs in early July.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23) During the breeding season, unpaired male aardwolves search their own territory, as well as others, for a female to mate with. Dominant males also mate opportunistically with the females of less dominant neighboring aardwolves,[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23) which can result in conflict between rival males.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ingo-9) Dominant males even go a step further and as the breeding season approaches, they make increasingly greater and greater incursions onto weaker males' territories. As the female comes into [oestrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oestrus "Oestrus"), they add pasting to their tricks inside of the other territories, sometimes doing so more in rivals' territories than their own.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23) Females will also, when given the opportunity, mate with the dominant male, which increases the chances of the dominant male guarding "his" cubs with her.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23) [Copulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copulation_\(zoology\) "Copulation (zoology)") lasts between 1 and 4.5 hours.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Richardson-25)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-32)

[Gestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestation "Gestation") lasts between 89 and 92 days,[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ingo-9)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23) producing two to five [cubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_animal_names "List of animal names") (most often two or three) during the rainy season (October–December),[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Colliers-21) when termites are more active.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-EB-4) They are born with their eyes open, but initially are helpless,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) and weigh around 200–350 g (7.1–12.3 oz).[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ingo-9) The first six to eight weeks are spent in the [den](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrow "Burrow") with their parents.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-br31-28) The male may spend up to six hours a night watching over the cubs while the mother is out looking for food.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) After three months, they begin supervised foraging, and by four months are normally independent, though they often share a den with their mother until the next breeding season.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-br31-28) By the time the next set of cubs is born, the older cubs have moved on.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-mh108-23) Aardwolves generally achieve [sexual maturity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_maturity "Sexual maturity") at one and a half to two years of age.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ingo-9)

## Conservation

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Aardwolf\&action=edit\&section=8 "Edit section: Conservation")]

The aardwolf has not seen decreasing numbers and is relatively widespread throughout eastern Africa. They are not common throughout their range, as they maintain a density of no more than 1 per square kilometer, if food is abundant. Because of these factors, the IUCN has rated the aardwolf as [least concern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_concern "Least concern").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-iucn-1) In some areas, they are persecuted because of the mistaken belief that they prey on livestock; however, they are actually beneficial to the farmers because they eat termites that are detrimental.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PKR-27) In other areas, the farmers have recognized this, but they are still killed, on occasion, for their fur. Dogs and insecticides[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-iucn-1) are also common killers of the aardwolf.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-br31-28)
