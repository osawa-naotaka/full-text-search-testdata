**Anatomy** (from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language "Ancient Greek language") **[ἀνατομή](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%80%CE%BD%CE%B1%CF%84%CE%BF%CE%BC%CE%AE#Ancient_Greek "wikt:ἀνατομή")*&#x20;(*anatomḗ*)* '[dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection "Dissection")') is the branch of [morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_\(biology\) "Morphology (biology)") concerned with the study of the internal structure of [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisms "Organisms") and their parts.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-1) Anatomy is a branch of [natural science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science "Natural science") that deals with the structural organization of living things. It is an old science, having its beginnings in prehistoric times.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-2) Anatomy is inherently tied to [developmental biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_biology "Developmental biology"), [embryology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryology "Embryology"), [comparative anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_anatomy "Comparative anatomy"), [evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology "Evolutionary biology"), and [phylogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogeny "Phylogeny"),[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-intro_HGray-3) as these are the processes by which anatomy is generated, both over immediate and long-term timescales. Anatomy and [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology"), which study the structure and [function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_\(biology\) "Function (biology)") of organisms and their parts respectively, make a natural pair of related disciplines, and are often studied together. Human anatomy is one of the essential [basic sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_sciences "Basic sciences") that are applied in medicine, and is often studied alongside [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-4)

Anatomy is a complex and dynamic field that is constantly evolving as discoveries are made. In recent years, there has been a significant increase in the use of advanced imaging techniques, such as [MRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRI "MRI") and [CT scans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan "CT scan"), which allow for more detailed and accurate visualizations of the body's structures.

The discipline of anatomy is divided into [macroscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroscopic "Macroscopic") and [microscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscopic "Microscopic") parts. [Macroscopic anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroscopic_anatomy "Macroscopic anatomy"), or gross anatomy, is the examination of an animal's body parts using unaided eyesight. Gross anatomy also includes the branch of [superficial anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superficial_anatomy "Superficial anatomy"). Microscopic anatomy involves the use of optical instruments in the study of the [tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_\(biology\) "Tissue (biology)") of various structures, known as [histology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histology "Histology"), and also in the study of [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_biology "Cell biology").

The [history of anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_anatomy "History of anatomy") is characterized by a progressive understanding of the functions of the organs and structures of the human body. Methods have also improved dramatically, advancing from the examination of animals by [dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection "Dissection") of carcasses and [cadavers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadaver "Cadaver") (corpses)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-:0-5) to 20th-century [medical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging "Medical imaging") techniques, including [X-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiography "Radiography"), [ultrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasound_imaging "Ultrasound imaging"), and [magnetic resonance imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging "Magnetic resonance imaging").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-:1-6)

## Etymology and definition

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: Etymology and definition")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/An_%C3%A9corch%C3%A9_figure_%28life-size%29%2C_lying_prone_on_a_table_Wellcome_L0020561.jpg/220px-An_%C3%A9corch%C3%A9_figure_%28life-size%29%2C_lying_prone_on_a_table_Wellcome_L0020561.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:An_%C3%A9corch%C3%A9_figure_\(life-size\),_lying_prone_on_a_table_Wellcome_L0020561.jpg)

A dissected body, lying prone on a table, by [Charles Landseer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Landseer "Charles Landseer")

Derived from the Greek ἀνατομή *anatomē* "dissection" (from ἀνατέμνω *anatémnō* "I cut up, cut open" from ἀνά *aná* "up", and τέμνω *témnō* "I cut"),[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7) anatomy is the scientific study of the structure of [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism "Organism") including their systems, [organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organs "Organs") and [tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_\(biology\) "Tissue (biology)"). It includes the appearance and position of the various parts, the materials from which they are composed, and their relationships with other parts. Anatomy is quite distinct from [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") and [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry"), which deal respectively with the functions of those parts and the chemical processes involved. For example, an anatomist is concerned with the shape, size, position, structure, blood supply and innervation of an organ such as the liver; while a physiologist is interested in the production of [bile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bile "Bile"), the role of the liver in nutrition and the regulation of bodily functions.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Everyman-8)

The discipline of anatomy can be subdivided into a number of branches, including gross or [macroscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroscopic "Macroscopic") anatomy and [microscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscopic "Microscopic") anatomy.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-9) [Gross anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_anatomy "Gross anatomy") is the study of structures large enough to be seen with the naked eye, and also includes [superficial anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superficial_anatomy "Superficial anatomy") or surface anatomy, the study by sight of the external body features. [Microscopic anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscopic_anatomy "Microscopic anatomy") is the study of structures on a microscopic scale, along with [histology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histology "Histology") (the study of tissues), and [embryology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryology "Embryology") (the study of an organism in its immature condition).[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-intro_HGray-3) Regional anatomy is the study of the interrelationships of all of the structures in a specific body region, such as the abdomen. In contrast, systemic anatomy is the study of the structures that make up a discrete body system—that is, a group of structures that work together to perform a unique body function, such as the digestive system.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-openstax-10)

Anatomy can be studied using both invasive and non-invasive methods with the goal of obtaining information about the structure and organization of organs and systems.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-intro_HGray-3) Methods used include [dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection "Dissection"), in which a body is opened and its organs studied, and [endoscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoscopy "Endoscopy"), in which a [video camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_camera "Video camera")-equipped instrument is inserted through a small incision in the body wall and used to explore the internal organs and other structures. [Angiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiography "Angiography") using [X-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray "X-ray") or [magnetic resonance angiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_angiography "Magnetic resonance angiography") are methods to visualize blood vessels.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-12)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-13)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-14)

The term "anatomy" is commonly taken to refer to [human anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_anatomy "Human anatomy"). However, substantially similar structures and tissues are found throughout the rest of the animal kingdom, and the term also includes the anatomy of other animals. The term *zootomy* is also sometimes used to specifically refer to non-human animals. The structure and tissues of plants are of a dissimilar nature and they are studied in [plant anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_anatomy "Plant anatomy").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Everyman-8)

## Animal tissues

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Animal tissues")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Anima_cell_notext.svg/220px-Anima_cell_notext.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Anima_cell_notext.svg)

Stylized cutaway diagram of an animal cell (with flagella)

The [kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_\(biology\) "Kingdom (biology)") [Animalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animalia "Animalia") contains [multicellular organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular_organism "Multicellular organism") that are [heterotrophic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotroph "Heterotroph") and [motile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motile "Motile") (although some have secondarily adopted a [sessile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sessility_\(zoology\) "Sessility (zoology)") lifestyle). Most animals have bodies differentiated into separate [tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_\(biology\) "Tissue (biology)") and these animals are also known as [eumetazoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eumetazoa "Eumetazoa"). They have an internal [digestive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestion "Digestion") chamber, with one or two openings; the [gametes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete "Gamete") are produced in multicellular sex organs, and the [zygotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygote "Zygote") include a [blastula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blastula "Blastula") stage in their [embryonic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryogenesis "Embryogenesis"). Metazoans do not include the [sponges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge "Sponge"), which have undifferentiated cells.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit549-15)

Unlike [plant cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_cell "Plant cell"), [animal cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_cells "Animal cells") have neither a cell wall nor [chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast"). Vacuoles, when present, are more in number and much smaller than those in the plant cell. The body tissues are composed of numerous types of cells, including those found in muscles, nerves and skin. Each typically has a cell membrane formed of [phospholipids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phospholipid "Phospholipid"), [cytoplasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoplasm "Cytoplasm") and a [nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus"). All of the different cells of an animal are derived from the embryonic [germ layers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_layer "Germ layer"). Those simpler invertebrates which are formed from two germ layers of ectoderm and endoderm are called [diploblastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diploblastic "Diploblastic") and the more developed animals whose structures and organs are formed from three germ layers are called [triploblastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triploblastic "Triploblastic").[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Ruppert60-16) All of a triploblastic animal's tissues and organs are derived from the three germ layers of the embryo, the [ectoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectoderm "Ectoderm"), [mesoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoderm "Mesoderm") and [endoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoderm "Endoderm").

Animal tissues can be grouped into four basic types: [connective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connective_tissue "Connective tissue"), [epithelial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithelial "Epithelial"), [muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle "Muscle") and [nervous tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_tissue "Nervous tissue").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Cartilage_polarised.jpg/220px-Cartilage_polarised.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cartilage_polarised.jpg)

Hyaline cartilage at high magnification ([H\&E stain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%26E_stain "H\&E stain"))

### Connective tissue

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=3 "Edit section: Connective tissue")]

[Connective tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connective_tissue "Connective tissue") are fibrous and made up of cells scattered among inorganic material called the [extracellular matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracellular_matrix "Extracellular matrix"). Often called [fascia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascia "Fascia") (from the Latin "fascia," meaning "band" or "bandage"), connective tissues give shape to organs and holds them in place. The main types are loose connective tissue, [adipose tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adipose_tissue "Adipose tissue"), fibrous connective tissue, [cartilage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartilage "Cartilage") and bone. The extracellular matrix contains [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"), the chief and most abundant of which is [collagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collagen "Collagen"). Collagen plays a major part in organizing and maintaining tissues. The matrix can be modified to form a skeleton to support or protect the body. An [exoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoskeleton "Exoskeleton") is a thickened, rigid [cuticle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuticle "Cuticle") which is stiffened by [mineralization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineralisation_\(biology\) "Mineralisation (biology)"), as in [crustaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustacean "Crustacean") or by the cross-linking of its proteins as in [insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect "Insect"). An [endoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoskeleton "Endoskeleton") is internal and present in all developed animals, as well as in many of those less developed.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Ruppert60-16)

### Epithelium

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Epithelium")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Normal_gastric_mucosa_low_mag.jpg/220px-Normal_gastric_mucosa_low_mag.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Normal_gastric_mucosa_low_mag.jpg)

Gastric mucosa at low magnification ([H\&E stain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%26E_stain "H\&E stain"))

[Epithelial tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithelial_tissue "Epithelial tissue") is composed of closely packed cells, bound to each other by [cell adhesion molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_adhesion_molecule "Cell adhesion molecule"), with little intercellular space. Epithelial cells can be [squamous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squamous_epithelial_cell "Squamous epithelial cell") (flat), [cuboidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_cuboidal_epithelium "Simple cuboidal epithelium") or [columnar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columnar_epithelial_cell "Columnar epithelial cell") and rest on a [basal lamina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_lamina "Basal lamina"), the upper layer of the [basement membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basement_membrane "Basement membrane"),[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-17) the lower layer is the reticular lamina lying next to the connective tissue in the extracellular matrix secreted by the epithelial cells.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-18) There are many different types of epithelium, modified to suit a particular function. In the [respiratory tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_tract "Respiratory tract") there is a type of [ciliated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudostratified_ciliated_columnar_epithelium "Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium") epithelial lining; in the small intestine there are [microvilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microvilli "Microvilli") on the epithelial lining and in the large intestine there are [intestinal villi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestinal_villi "Intestinal villi"). Skin consists of an outer layer of [keratinized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keratin "Keratin") stratified squamous epithelium that covers the exterior of the vertebrate body. [Keratinocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keratinocyte "Keratinocyte") make up to 95% of the cells in the [skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidermis_\(skin\) "Epidermis (skin)").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-19) The epithelial cells on the external surface of the body typically secrete an extracellular matrix in the form of a [cuticle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuticle "Cuticle"). In simple animals this may just be a coat of [glycoproteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycoproteins "Glycoproteins").[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Ruppert60-16) In more advanced animals, many [glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gland "Gland") are formed of epithelial cells.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-20)

### Muscle tissue

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Muscle tissue")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Skeletal_muscle_-_cross_section%2C_nerve_bundle.jpg/220px-Skeletal_muscle_-_cross_section%2C_nerve_bundle.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Skeletal_muscle_-_cross_section,_nerve_bundle.jpg)

Cross section through [skeletal muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_muscle "Skeletal muscle") and a small [nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve "Nerve") at high magnification ([H\&E stain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%26E_stain "H\&E stain"))

[Muscle cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_cells "Muscle cells") (myocytes) form the active contractile tissue of the body. [Muscle tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_tissue "Muscle tissue") functions to produce force and cause motion, either locomotion or movement within internal organs. Muscle is formed of contractile [filaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myofibril "Myofibril") and is separated into three main types; [smooth muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_muscle "Smooth muscle"), [skeletal muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_muscle "Skeletal muscle") and [cardiac muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_muscle "Cardiac muscle"). Smooth muscle has no [striations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striated_muscle_tissue "Striated muscle tissue") when examined microscopically. It contracts slowly but maintains contractibility over a wide range of stretch lengths. It is found in such organs as [sea anemone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_anemone "Sea anemone") tentacles and the body wall of [sea cucumbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_cucumber "Sea cucumber"). Skeletal muscle contracts rapidly but has a limited range of extension. It is found in the movement of appendages and jaws. Obliquely striated muscle is intermediate between the other two. The filaments are staggered and this is the type of muscle found in [earthworms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthworm "Earthworm") that can extend slowly or make rapid contractions.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Ruppert103-21) In higher animals striated muscles occur in bundles attached to bone to provide movement and are often arranged in antagonistic sets. Smooth muscle is found in the walls of the [uterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterus "Uterus"), [bladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bladder "Bladder"), [intestines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestines "Intestines"), [stomach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach "Stomach"), [oesophagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oesophagus "Oesophagus"), [respiratory airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_airways "Respiratory airways"), and [blood vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_vessel "Blood vessel"). [Cardiac muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_muscle "Cardiac muscle") is found only in the [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart "Heart"), allowing it to contract and pump blood round the body.

### Nervous tissue

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=6 "Edit section: Nervous tissue")]

See also: [Neuroanatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroanatomy "Neuroanatomy")

[Nervous tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_tissue "Nervous tissue") is composed of many nerve cells known as [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron "Neuron") which transmit information. In some slow-moving [radially symmetrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radially_symmetrical "Radially symmetrical") marine animals such as [ctenophores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctenophore "Ctenophore") and [cnidarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cnidarian "Cnidarian") (including [sea anemones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_anemone "Sea anemone") and [jellyfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jellyfish "Jellyfish")), the nerves form a [nerve net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_net "Nerve net"), but in most animals they are organized longitudinally into bundles. In simple animals, receptor neurons in the body wall cause a local reaction to a stimulus. In more complex animals, specialized receptor cells such as [chemoreceptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemoreceptor "Chemoreceptor") and [photoreceptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoreceptor_cell "Photoreceptor cell") are found in groups and send messages along [neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_neural_network "Biological neural network") to other parts of the organism. Neurons can be connected together in [ganglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganglia "Ganglia").[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Ruppert104-22) In higher animals, specialized receptors are the basis of sense organs and there is a [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") (brain and spinal cord) and a [peripheral nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system"). The latter consists of [sensory nerves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron") that transmit information from sense organs and [motor nerves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_nerves "Motor nerves") that influence target organs.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-23)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Ruppert107-24) The peripheral nervous system is divided into the [somatic nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_nervous_system "Somatic nervous system") which conveys sensation and controls [voluntary muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_muscle "Voluntary muscle"), and the [autonomic nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") which involuntarily controls [smooth muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_muscle "Smooth muscle"), certain glands and internal organs, including the [stomach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach "Stomach").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-25)

## Vertebrate anatomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=7 "Edit section: Vertebrate anatomy")]

See also: [Vertebrate § Anatomy and morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate#Anatomy_and_morphology "Vertebrate"), and [Comparative anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_anatomy "Comparative anatomy")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/VolRenderShearWarp.gif/170px-VolRenderShearWarp.gif)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:VolRenderShearWarp.gif)

[Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouse "Mouse") skull. The neck and most of the forelimbs are also seen.

All [vertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate "Vertebrate") have a similar basic [body plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_plan "Body plan") and at some point in their lives, mostly in the [embryonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryogenesis "Embryogenesis") stage, share the major [chordate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chordate "Chordate") characteristics: a stiffening rod, the [notochord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notochord "Notochord"); a dorsal hollow tube of nervous material, the [neural tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_tube "Neural tube"); [pharyngeal arches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharyngeal_arch "Pharyngeal arch"); and a tail posterior to the anus. The [spinal cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_cord "Spinal cord") is protected by the [vertebral column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebral_column "Vertebral column") and is above the notochord, and the [gastrointestinal tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_tract "Gastrointestinal tract") is below it.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-26) Nervous tissue is derived from the [ectoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectoderm "Ectoderm"), connective tissues are derived from [mesoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoderm "Mesoderm"), and gut is derived from the [endoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoderm "Endoderm"). At the posterior end is a tail which continues the spinal cord and vertebrae but not the gut. The mouth is found at the anterior end of the animal, and the [anus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anus "Anus") at the base of the tail.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-27) The defining characteristic of a vertebrate is the [vertebral column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebral_column "Vertebral column"), formed in the development of the segmented series of [vertebrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebra "Vertebra"). In most vertebrates the notochord becomes the [nucleus pulposus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleus_pulposus "Nucleus pulposus") of the [intervertebral discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intervertebral_disc "Intervertebral disc"). However, a few vertebrates, such as the [sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgeon "Sturgeon") and the [coelacanth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coelacanth "Coelacanth"), retain the notochord into adulthood.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-28) [Jawed vertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawed_vertebrates "Jawed vertebrates") are typified by paired appendages, fins or legs, which may be secondarily lost. The limbs of vertebrates are considered to be [homologous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_\(biology\) "Homology (biology)") because the same underlying skeletal structure was inherited from their last common ancestor. This is one of the arguments put forward by [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin") to support his theory of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution "Evolution").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-29)

### Fish anatomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=8 "Edit section: Fish anatomy")]

Main article: [Fish anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_anatomy "Fish anatomy")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/Internal_anatomy_of_a_fish_%28cutaway_diagram%29.jpg/220px-Internal_anatomy_of_a_fish_%28cutaway_diagram%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Internal_anatomy_of_a_fish_\(cutaway_diagram\).jpg)

Cutaway diagram showing various organs of a fish

The body of a [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish "Fish") is divided into a head, trunk and tail, although the divisions between the three are not always externally visible. The skeleton, which forms the support structure inside the fish, is either made of cartilage, in [cartilaginous fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartilaginous_fish "Cartilaginous fish"), or bone in [bony fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bony_fish "Bony fish"). The main skeletal element is the vertebral column, composed of articulating [vertebrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebra "Vertebra") which are lightweight yet strong. The ribs attach to the spine and there are no [limbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limb_\(anatomy\) "Limb (anatomy)") or limb girdles. The main external features of the fish, the [fins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_fin "Fish fin"), are composed of either bony or soft spines called rays, which with the exception of the [caudal fins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudal_fin "Caudal fin"), have no direct connection with the spine. They are supported by the muscles which compose the main part of the trunk.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit818-30) The heart has two chambers and pumps the blood through the respiratory surfaces of the [gills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gill "Gill") and on round the body in a single circulatory loop.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-31) The eyes are adapted for seeing underwater and have only local vision. There is an inner ear but no external or [middle ear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_ear "Middle ear"). Low frequency vibrations are detected by the [lateral line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_line "Lateral line") system of sense organs that run along the length of the sides of fish, and these respond to nearby movements and to changes in water pressure.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit818-30)

Sharks and rays are [basal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_\(phylogenetics\) "Basal (phylogenetics)") fish with numerous [primitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_\(phylogenetics\) "Primitive (phylogenetics)") anatomical features similar to those of ancient fish, including skeletons composed of cartilage. Their bodies tend to be dorso-ventrally flattened, they usually have five pairs of gill slits and a large mouth set on the underside of the head. The dermis is covered with separate dermal [placoid scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placoid_scales "Placoid scales"). They have a [cloaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloaca "Cloaca") into which the urinary and genital passages open, but not a [swim bladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swim_bladder "Swim bladder"). Cartilaginous fish produce a small number of large, [yolky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_yolk "Egg yolk") eggs. Some species are [ovoviviparous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovoviviparous "Ovoviviparous") and the young develop internally but others are [oviparous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oviparous "Oviparous") and the larvae develop externally in egg cases.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Kotpal-32)

The bony fish lineage shows more [derived](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_trait "Derived trait") anatomical traits, often with major evolutionary changes from the features of ancient fish. They have a bony skeleton, are generally laterally flattened, have five pairs of gills protected by an [operculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operculum_\(fish\) "Operculum (fish)"), and a mouth at or near the tip of the snout. The dermis is covered with overlapping [scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_scale "Fish scale"). Bony fish have a swim bladder which helps them maintain a constant depth in the water column, but not a cloaca. They mostly [spawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spawn_\(biology\) "Spawn (biology)") a large number of small eggs with little yolk which they broadcast into the water column.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Kotpal-32)

### Amphibian anatomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=9 "Edit section: Amphibian anatomy")]

Main article: [Amphibian anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian_anatomy "Amphibian anatomy")

[![Frog skeleton](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Ceratophrys_cornuta_skeleton_front.jpg/220px-Ceratophrys_cornuta_skeleton_front.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ceratophrys_cornuta_skeleton_front.jpg)

Skeleton of [Surinam horned frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinam_horned_frog "Surinam horned frog") (*Ceratophrys cornuta*)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Frog_anatomy_tags.PNG/170px-Frog_anatomy_tags.PNG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Frog_anatomy_tags.PNG)

Plastic model of a frog

[Amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian "Amphibian") are a [class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_\(biology\) "Class (biology)") of animals comprising [frogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frog "Frog"), [salamanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salamander "Salamander") and [caecilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caecilian "Caecilian"). They are [tetrapods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrapod "Tetrapod"), but the caecilians and a few species of salamander have either no limbs or their limbs are much reduced in size. Their main bones are hollow and lightweight and are fully ossified and the vertebrae interlock with each other and have [articular processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articular_processes "Articular processes"). Their ribs are usually short and may be fused to the vertebrae. Their skulls are mostly broad and short, and are often incompletely ossified. Their skin contains little [keratin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keratin "Keratin") and lacks scales, but contains many [mucous glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucous_gland "Mucous gland") and in some species, poison glands. The hearts of amphibians have three chambers, two [atria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrium_\(heart\) "Atrium (heart)") and one [ventricle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventricle_\(heart\) "Ventricle (heart)"). They have a [urinary bladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urinary_bladder "Urinary bladder") and [nitrogenous waste products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic_waste#nitrogen_wastes "Metabolic waste") are excreted primarily as [urea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urea "Urea"). Amphibians breathe by means of [buccal pumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buccal_pumping "Buccal pumping"), a pump action in which air is first drawn into the [buccopharyngeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buccopharyngeal_membrane "Buccopharyngeal membrane") region through the nostrils. These are then closed and the air is forced into the lungs by contraction of the throat.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-33) They supplement this with [gas exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_exchange "Gas exchange") through the skin which needs to be kept moist.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit859-34)

In frogs the pelvic girdle is robust and the hind legs are much longer and stronger than the forelimbs. The feet have four or five digits and the toes are often webbed for swimming or have suction pads for climbing. Frogs have large eyes and no tail. Salamanders resemble lizards in appearance; their short legs project sideways, the belly is close to or in contact with the ground and they have a long tail. Caecilians superficially resemble [earthworms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthworm "Earthworm") and are limbless. They burrow by means of zones of muscle contractions which move along the body and they swim by undulating their body from side to side.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-35)

### Reptile anatomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=10 "Edit section: Reptile anatomy")]

Main article: [Reptile anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile_anatomy "Reptile anatomy")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Crotalus_atrox_-Museum_of_Osteology%2C_Oklahoma_City%2C_Oklahoma%2C_USA-29Aug2012.jpg/220px-Crotalus_atrox_-Museum_of_Osteology%2C_Oklahoma_City%2C_Oklahoma%2C_USA-29Aug2012.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Crotalus_atrox_-Museum_of_Osteology,_Oklahoma_City,_Oklahoma,_USA-29Aug2012.jpg)

Skeleton of a [western diamondback rattlesnake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_diamondback_rattlesnake "Western diamondback rattlesnake")

[Reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile "Reptile") are a class of [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal") comprising [turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtle "Turtle"), [tuataras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuatara "Tuatara"), [lizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizard "Lizard"), [snakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake "Snake") and [crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodile "Crocodile"). They are [tetrapods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrapod "Tetrapod"), but the snakes and a few species of lizard either have no limbs or their limbs are much reduced in size. Their bones are better ossified and their skeletons stronger than those of amphibians. The teeth are conical and mostly uniform in size. The surface cells of the epidermis are modified into horny scales which create a waterproof layer. Reptiles are unable to use their skin for respiration as do amphibians and have a more efficient respiratory system drawing air into their [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung "Lung") by expanding their chest walls. The heart resembles that of the amphibian but there is a septum which more completely separates the oxygenated and deoxygenated bloodstreams. The reproductive system has evolved for internal fertilization, with a [copulatory organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copulatory_organ "Copulatory organ") present in most species. The eggs are surrounded by [amniotic membranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amniote "Amniote") which prevents them from drying out and are laid on land, or [develop internally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovoviviparity "Ovoviviparity") in some species. The bladder is small as nitrogenous waste is excreted as [uric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uric_acid "Uric acid").[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit865-36)

Turtles are notable for their protective shells. They have an inflexible trunk encased in a horny [carapace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carapace "Carapace") above and a [plastron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastron "Plastron") below. These are formed from bony plates embedded in the dermis which are overlain by horny ones and are partially fused with the ribs and spine. The neck is long and flexible and the head and the legs can be drawn back inside the shell. Turtles are vegetarians and the typical reptile teeth have been replaced by sharp, horny plates. In aquatic species, the front legs are modified into flippers.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit868-37)

**Tuataras** superficially resemble lizards but the lineages diverged in the [Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic "Triassic") period. There is one living species, *[Sphenodon punctatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphenodon_punctatus "Sphenodon punctatus")*. The skull has two openings (fenestrae) on either side and the jaw is rigidly attached to the skull. There is one row of teeth in the lower jaw and this fits between the two rows in the upper jaw when the animal chews. The teeth are merely projections of bony material from the jaw and eventually wear down. The brain and heart are more primitive than those of other reptiles, and the lungs have a single chamber and lack [bronchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchi "Bronchi"). The tuatara has a well-developed [parietal eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parietal_eye "Parietal eye") on its forehead.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit868-37)

Lizards have skulls with only one [fenestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_fenestra "Nasal fenestra") on each side, the lower bar of bone below the second fenestra having been lost. This results in the jaws being less rigidly attached which allows the mouth to open wider. Lizards are mostly quadrupeds, with the trunk held off the ground by short, sideways-facing legs, but a few species have no limbs and resemble snakes. Lizards have moveable eyelids, eardrums are present and some species have a central parietal eye.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit868-37)

Snakes are closely related to lizards, having branched off from a common ancestral lineage during the [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous "Cretaceous") period, and they share many of the same features. The skeleton consists of a skull, a hyoid bone, spine and ribs though a few species retain a vestige of the pelvis and rear limbs in the form of [pelvic spurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvic_spur "Pelvic spur"). The bar under the second fenestra has also been lost and the jaws have extreme flexibility allowing the snake to swallow its prey whole. Snakes lack moveable eyelids, the eyes being covered by transparent "spectacle" scales. They do not have eardrums but can detect ground vibrations through the bones of their skull. Their forked tongues are used as organs of taste and smell and some species have sensory pits on their heads enabling them to locate warm-blooded prey.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit870-38)

[Crocodilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodilians "Crocodilians") are large, low-slung aquatic reptiles with long snouts and large numbers of teeth. The head and trunk are dorso-ventrally flattened and the tail is laterally compressed. It undulates from side to side to force the animal through the water when swimming. The tough keratinized scales provide body armour and some are fused to the skull. The nostrils, eyes and ears are elevated above the top of the flat head enabling them to remain above the surface of the water when the animal is floating. Valves seal the nostrils and ears when it is submerged. Unlike other reptiles, crocodilians have hearts with four chambers allowing complete separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit874-39)

### Bird anatomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=11 "Edit section: Bird anatomy")]

Main article: [Bird anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_anatomy "Bird anatomy")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Duerer_wing_of_a_blue_roller.jpg/220px-Duerer_wing_of_a_blue_roller.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Duerer_wing_of_a_blue_roller.jpg)

Part of a wing. [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer "Albrecht Dürer"), c. 1500–1512

[Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird "Bird") are [tetrapods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrapod "Tetrapod") but though their hind limbs are used for walking or hopping, their front limbs are [wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_wing "Bird wing") covered with [feathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feather "Feather") and adapted for flight. Birds are [endothermic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endotherm "Endotherm"), have a high [metabolic rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic_rate "Metabolic rate"), a light [skeletal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_system "Skeletal system") and powerful [muscles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle "Muscle"). The long bones are thin, hollow and very light. Air sac extensions from the lungs occupy the centre of some bones. The sternum is wide and usually has a keel and the caudal vertebrae are fused. There are no teeth and the narrow jaws are adapted into a horn-covered beak. The eyes are relatively large, particularly in nocturnal species such as owls. They face forwards in predators and sideways in ducks.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit895-40)

The feathers are outgrowths of the [epidermis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidermis_\(zoology\) "Epidermis (zoology)") and are found in localized bands from where they fan out over the skin. Large flight feathers are found on the wings and tail, contour feathers cover the bird's surface and fine down occurs on young birds and under the contour feathers of water birds. The only cutaneous gland is the single [uropygial gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uropygial_gland "Uropygial gland") near the base of the tail. This produces an oily secretion that waterproofs the feathers when the bird [preens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_grooming "Personal grooming"). There are scales on the legs, feet and claws on the tips of the toes.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit895-40)

### Mammal anatomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=12 "Edit section: Mammal anatomy")]

Main article: [Mammal anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal_anatomy "Mammal anatomy")

[Mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal "Mammal") are a diverse class of animals, mostly terrestrial but some are aquatic and others have evolved flapping or gliding flight. They mostly have four limbs, but some aquatic mammals have no limbs or limbs modified into fins, and the forelimbs of bats are modified into wings. The legs of most mammals are situated below the trunk, which is held well clear of the ground. The bones of mammals are well ossified and their teeth, which are usually differentiated, are coated in a layer of [prismatic enamel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tooth_enamel "Tooth enamel"). The teeth are shed once ([milk teeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_teeth "Milk teeth")) during the animal's lifetime or not at all, as is the case in [cetaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetacea "Cetacea"). Mammals have three bones in the middle ear and a [cochlea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochlea "Cochlea") in the [inner ear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_ear "Inner ear"). They are clothed in hair and their skin contains glands which secrete [sweat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweat_gland "Sweat gland"). Some of these glands are specialized as [mammary glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammary_gland "Mammary gland"), producing milk to feed the young. Mammals breathe with [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung "Lung") and have a muscular [diaphragm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_diaphragm "Thoracic diaphragm") separating the thorax from the abdomen which helps them draw air into the lungs. The mammalian heart has four chambers, and oxygenated and deoxygenated blood are kept entirely separate. Nitrogenous waste is excreted primarily as urea.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit914-41)

Mammals are [amniotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amniote "Amniote"), and most are [viviparous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viviparous "Viviparous"), giving birth to live young. Exceptions to this are the egg-laying [monotremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotreme "Monotreme"), the [platypus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platypus "Platypus") and the [echidnas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echidna "Echidna") of Australia. Most other mammals have a [placenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta "Placenta") through which the developing [foetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foetus "Foetus") obtains nourishment, but in [marsupials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsupial "Marsupial"), the foetal stage is very short and the immature young is born and finds its way to its mother's [pouch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pouch_\(marsupial\) "Pouch (marsupial)") where it latches on to a [teat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teat "Teat") and completes its development.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Dorit914-41)

#### Human anatomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=13 "Edit section: Human anatomy")]

Further information: [Human body § Anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_body#Anatomy "Human body"), and [Outline of human anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_human_anatomy "Outline of human anatomy")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Parasagittal_MRI_of_human_head_in_patient_with_benign_familial_macrocephaly_prior_to_brain_injury_%28ANIMATED%29.gif/220px-Parasagittal_MRI_of_human_head_in_patient_with_benign_familial_macrocephaly_prior_to_brain_injury_%28ANIMATED%29.gif)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Parasagittal_MRI_of_human_head_in_patient_with_benign_familial_macrocephaly_prior_to_brain_injury_\(ANIMATED\).gif)

Sagittal sections of the head as seen by a modern [MRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRI "MRI") scan

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/Violin_Scroll_%282769099541%29.jpg/170px-Violin_Scroll_%282769099541%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Violin_Scroll_\(2769099541\).jpg)

In humans, dexterous hand movements and increased brain size are likely to have evolved simultaneously.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-BritBrit-Hand-42)

[Humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human "Human") have the overall body plan of a mammal. Humans have a head, neck, [trunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trunk_\(anatomy\) "Trunk (anatomy)") (which includes the [thorax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorax "Thorax") and [abdomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdomen "Abdomen")), two arms and hands, and two legs and feet.

Generally, students of certain [biological sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_sciences "Biological sciences"), [paramedics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramedic "Paramedic"), prosthetists and orthotists, [physiotherapists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiotherapists "Physiotherapists"), [occupational therapists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_therapy "Occupational therapy"), [nurses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurses "Nurses"), [podiatrists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podiatry "Podiatry"), and [medical students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_school "Medical school") learn gross anatomy and microscopic anatomy from anatomical models, skeletons, textbooks, diagrams, photographs, lectures and tutorials and in addition, medical students generally also learn gross anatomy through practical experience of [dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection "Dissection") and inspection of [cadavers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadaver "Cadaver"). The study of microscopic anatomy (or [histology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histology "Histology")) can be aided by practical experience examining histological preparations (or slides) under a [microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscope "Microscope").[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-43)

Human anatomy, physiology and biochemistry are complementary basic medical sciences, which are generally taught to medical students in their first year at medical school. Human anatomy can be taught regionally or systemically; that is, respectively, studying anatomy by bodily regions such as the head and chest, or studying by specific systems, such as the nervous or respiratory systems.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-intro_HGray-3) The major anatomy textbook, [Gray's Anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray%27s_Anatomy "Gray's Anatomy"), has been reorganized from a systems format to a regional format, in line with modern teaching methods.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-44)[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-45) A thorough working knowledge of anatomy is required by physicians, especially [surgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgery "Surgery") and doctors working in some diagnostic specialties, such as [histopathology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histopathology "Histopathology") and [radiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiology "Radiology").[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-AAA-46)

Academic anatomists are usually employed by universities, medical schools or teaching hospitals. They are often involved in teaching anatomy, and research into certain systems, organs, tissues or cells.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-AAA-46)

## Invertebrate anatomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=14 "Edit section: Invertebrate anatomy")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Chirocephalus_diaphanus_male_head.png/220px-Chirocephalus_diaphanus_male_head.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Chirocephalus_diaphanus_male_head.png)

Head of a male *[Daphnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daphnia "Daphnia")*, a [planktonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planktonic "Planktonic") crustacean

[Invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate "Invertebrate") constitute a vast array of living organisms ranging from the simplest unicellular [eukaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote") such as *[Paramecium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramecium "Paramecium")* to such complex multicellular animals as the [octopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octopus "Octopus"), [lobster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobster "Lobster") and [dragonfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonfly "Dragonfly"). They constitute about 95% of the animal species. By definition, none of these creatures has a backbone. The cells of single-cell [protozoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protozoa "Protozoa") have the same basic structure as those of multicellular animals but some parts are specialized into the equivalent of tissues and organs. Locomotion is often provided by [cilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilia "Cilia") or [flagella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagella "Flagella") or may proceed via the advance of [pseudopodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudopodia "Pseudopodia"), food may be gathered by [phagocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocytosis "Phagocytosis"), energy needs may be supplied by [photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis "Photosynthesis") and the cell may be supported by an [endoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoskeleton "Endoskeleton") or an [exoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoskeleton "Exoskeleton"). Some protozoans can form multicellular colonies.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-47)

[Metazoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metazoa "Metazoa") are a multicellular organism, with different groups of cells serving different functions. The most basic types of metazoan tissues are epithelium and connective tissue, both of which are present in nearly all invertebrates. The outer surface of the epidermis is normally formed of epithelial cells and secretes an [extracellular matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracellular_matrix "Extracellular matrix") which provides support to the organism. An endoskeleton derived from the [mesoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoderm "Mesoderm") is present in [echinoderms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echinoderm "Echinoderm"), [sponges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge "Sponge") and some [cephalopods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalopod "Cephalopod"). [Exoskeletons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoskeleton "Exoskeleton") are derived from the epidermis and is composed of [chitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitin "Chitin") in [arthropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod "Arthropod") (insects, spiders, ticks, shrimps, crabs, lobsters). [Calcium carbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_carbonate "Calcium carbonate") constitutes the shells of [molluscs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molluscs "Molluscs"), [brachiopods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiopod "Brachiopod") and some tube-building [polychaete worms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychaete_worms "Polychaete worms") and [silica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silica "Silica") forms the exoskeleton of the microscopic [diatoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatom "Diatom") and [radiolaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiolaria "Radiolaria").[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-48) Other invertebrates may have no rigid structures but the epidermis may secrete a variety of surface coatings such as the [pinacoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinacoderm "Pinacoderm") of sponges, the gelatinous cuticle of cnidarians ([polyps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyp_\(zoology\) "Polyp (zoology)"), [sea anemones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_anemone "Sea anemone"), [jellyfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jellyfish "Jellyfish")) and the [collagenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collagen "Collagen") cuticle of [annelids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annelid "Annelid"). The outer epithelial layer may include cells of several types including sensory cells, gland cells and stinging cells. There may also be protrusions such as [microvilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microvilli "Microvilli"), cilia, bristles, [spines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spine_\(zoology\) "Spine (zoology)") and [tubercles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubercle "Tubercle").[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-49)

[Marcello Malpighi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcello_Malpighi "Marcello Malpighi"), the father of microscopical anatomy, discovered that plants had tubules similar to those he saw in insects like the silk worm. He observed that when a ring-like portion of bark was removed on a trunk a swelling occurred in the tissues above the ring, and he unmistakably interpreted this as growth stimulated by food coming down from the leaves, and being captured above the ring.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ArberAgnes-50)

### Arthropod anatomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=15 "Edit section: Arthropod anatomy")]

Main articles: [Arthropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod "Arthropod"), [Insect morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect_morphology "Insect morphology"), and [Spider anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider_anatomy "Spider anatomy")

[Arthropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod "Arthropod") comprise the largest phylum of [invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate "Invertebrate") in the animal kingdom with over a million known species.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-51)

[Insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect "Insect") possess [segmented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segmentation_\(biology\) "Segmentation (biology)") bodies supported by a hard-jointed outer covering, the [exoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoskeleton "Exoskeleton"), made mostly of [chitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitin "Chitin"). The segments of the body are organized into three distinct parts, a head, a [thorax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorax_\(insect_anatomy\) "Thorax (insect anatomy)") and an [abdomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdomen "Abdomen").[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-52) The head typically bears a pair of sensory [antennae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_\(biology\) "Antenna (biology)"), a pair of [compound eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_eye "Compound eye"), one to three simple eyes ([ocelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocelli "Ocelli")) and three sets of modified appendages that form the [mouthparts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect_mouthparts "Insect mouthparts"). The thorax has three pairs of segmented [legs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod_leg "Arthropod leg"), one pair each for the three segments that compose the thorax and one or two pairs of [wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect_wing "Insect wing"). The abdomen is composed of eleven segments, some of which may be fused and houses the [digestive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestion "Digestion"), [respiratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiration_\(physiology\) "Respiration (physiology)"), [excretory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excretory "Excretory") and reproductive systems.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Gullan_and_Cranston-53) There is considerable variation between species and many adaptations to the body parts, especially wings, legs, antennae and mouthparts.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-54)

[Spiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider "Spider") a class of [arachnids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachnid "Arachnid") have four pairs of legs; a body of two segments—a [cephalothorax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalothorax "Cephalothorax") and an [abdomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdomen "Abdomen"). Spiders have no wings and no antennae. They have mouthparts called [chelicerae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelicerae "Chelicerae") which are often connected to venom glands as most spiders are venomous. They have a second pair of appendages called [pedipalps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedipalp "Pedipalp") attached to the cephalothorax. These have similar segmentation to the legs and function as taste and smell organs. At the end of each male pedipalp is a spoon-shaped cymbium that acts to support the [copulatory organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palpal_bulb "Palpal bulb").

## Other branches of anatomy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=16 "Edit section: Other branches of anatomy")]

- [Surface anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_anatomy "Surface anatomy") is important as the study of anatomical landmarks that can be readily seen from the exterior contours of the body.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-intro_HGray-3) It enables medics and [veterinarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterinarian "Veterinarian") to gauge the position and anatomy of the associated deeper structures. Superficial is a directional term that indicates that structures are located relatively close to the surface of the body.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-55)
- [Comparative anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_anatomy "Comparative anatomy") relates to the comparison of anatomical structures (both gross and microscopic) in different animals.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-intro_HGray-3)
- [Artistic anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artistic_anatomy "Artistic anatomy") relates to anatomic studies of [body proportions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_proportions "Body proportions") for artistic reasons.

## History

### Ancient

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=18 "Edit section: Ancient")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Foetal_positions_in_uterus%2C_pregnant_female_Wellcome_L0000845.jpg/230px-Foetal_positions_in_uterus%2C_pregnant_female_Wellcome_L0000845.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Foetal_positions_in_uterus,_pregnant_female_Wellcome_L0000845.jpg)

Image of early rendition of anatomy findings

In 1600 BCE, the [Edwin Smith Papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Smith_Papyrus "Edwin Smith Papyrus"), an [Ancient Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_medicine "Ancient Egyptian medicine") [medical text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_manual "Medical manual"), described the [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart "Heart") and its vessels, as well as the brain and its [meninges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meninges "Meninges") and [cerebrospinal fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebrospinal_fluid "Cerebrospinal fluid"), and the [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver "Liver"), [spleen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spleen "Spleen"), [kidneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidneys "Kidneys"), [uterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterus "Uterus") and [bladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bladder "Bladder"). It showed the [blood vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_vessel "Blood vessel") diverging from the heart.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-56)[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-57)[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-58) The [Ebers Papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebers_Papyrus "Ebers Papyrus") (c. 1550 BCE) features a "treatise on the heart", with vessels carrying all the body's fluids to or from every member of the body.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-59)

Ancient Greek anatomy and physiology underwent great changes and advances throughout the early medieval world. Over time, this medical practice expanded due to a continually developing understanding of the functions of organs and structures in the body. Phenomenal anatomical observations of the human body were made, which contributed to the understanding of the brain, eye, liver, reproductive organs, and nervous system.

The [Hellenistic Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_Egypt "Hellenistic Egypt") city of [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria "Alexandria") was the stepping-stone for Greek anatomy and physiology. Alexandria not only housed the biggest library for medical records and books of the liberal arts in the world during the time of the Greeks but was also home to many medical practitioners and philosophers. Great patronage of the arts and sciences from the [Ptolemaic dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_dynasty "Ptolemaic dynasty") of Egypt helped raise Alexandria up, further rivalling other Greek states' cultural and scientific achievements.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Longrigg-60)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/The_Blue_Beryl-Anatomy.jpg/220px-The_Blue_Beryl-Anatomy.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Blue_Beryl-Anatomy.jpg)

An anatomy [thangka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thangka "Thangka"), part of [Desi Sangye Gyatso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desi_Sangye_Gyatso "Desi Sangye Gyatso")'s The Blue Beryl, 17th century

Some of the most striking advances in early anatomy and physiology took place in Hellenistic Alexandria.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Longrigg-60) Two of the most famous anatomists and physiologists of the third century were [Herophilus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herophilus "Herophilus") and [Erasistratus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasistratus "Erasistratus"). These two physicians helped pioneer human [dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection "Dissection") for medical research, using the cadavers of condemned criminals, which was considered taboo until the Renaissance—Herophilus was recognized as the first person to perform systematic dissections.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-bay-61) Herophilus became known for his anatomical works, making impressive contributions to many branches of anatomy and many other aspects of medicine.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-62) Some of the works included classifying the system of the pulse, the discovery that human arteries had thicker walls than veins, and that the atria were parts of the heart. Herophilus's knowledge of the human body has provided vital input towards understanding the brain, eye, liver, reproductive organs, and nervous system and characterizing the course of the disease.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-bay-61) Erasistratus accurately described the structure of the brain, including the cavities and membranes, and made a distinction between its cerebrum and cerebellum[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-63) During his study in Alexandria, Erasistratus was particularly concerned with studies of the circulatory and nervous systems. He could distinguish the human body's sensory and motor nerves and believed air entered the lungs and heart, which was then carried throughout the body. His distinction between the arteries and veins—the arteries carrying the air through the body, while the veins carry the blood from the heart was a great anatomical discovery. Erasistratus was also responsible for naming and describing the function of the epiglottis and the heart's valves, including the tricuspid.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-64) During the third century, Greek physicians were able to differentiate nerves from blood vessels and tendons[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-65) and to realize that the nerves convey neural impulses.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Longrigg-60) It was Herophilus who made the point that damage to motor nerves induced paralysis.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-bay-61) Herophilus named the meninges and ventricles in the brain, appreciated the division between cerebellum and cerebrum and recognized that the brain was the "seat of intellect" and not a "cooling chamber" as propounded by Aristotle[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-66) Herophilus is also credited with describing the optic, oculomotor, motor division of the trigeminal, facial, vestibulocochlear and hypoglossal nerves.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Cambridge_University_Press-67)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Zahrawi1.png/200px-Zahrawi1.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Zahrawi1.png)

Surgical instruments were invented by [Abulcasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abulcasis "Abulcasis") in the 11th century

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Cheshm_manuscript.jpg/200px-Cheshm_manuscript.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cheshm_manuscript.jpg)

Anatomy of the eye for the first time in history by [Hunayn ibn Ishaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunayn_ibn_Ishaq "Hunayn ibn Ishaq") in the 9th century

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/13th_century_anatomical_illustration.jpg/220px-13th_century_anatomical_illustration.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:13th_century_anatomical_illustration.jpg)

13th century anatomical illustration

Incredible feats were made during the third century BCE in both the digestive and reproductive systems. Herophilus discovered and described not only the salivary glands but also the small intestine and liver.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Cambridge_University_Press-67) He showed that the uterus is a hollow organ and described the ovaries and uterine tubes. He recognized that spermatozoa were produced by the testes and was the first to identify the prostate gland.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Cambridge_University_Press-67)

The anatomy of the muscles and skeleton is described in the *[Hippocratic Corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocratic_Corpus "Hippocratic Corpus")*, an Ancient Greek medical work written by unknown authors.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-68) [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle") described [vertebrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate "Vertebrate") anatomy based on animal [dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection "Dissection"). [Praxagoras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praxagoras "Praxagoras") identified the difference between [arteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arteries "Arteries") and [veins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vein "Vein"). Also in the 4th century BCE, [Herophilos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herophilos "Herophilos") and [Erasistratus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasistratus "Erasistratus") produced more accurate anatomical descriptions based on [vivisection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivisection "Vivisection") of criminals in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria "Alexandria") during the [Ptolemaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Kingdom "Ptolemaic Kingdom").[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Bodies-69)[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-70)

In the 2nd century, [Galen of Pergamum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galen_of_Pergamum "Galen of Pergamum"), an anatomist, [clinician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinician "Clinician"), writer, and philosopher,[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-BritBrit-Galen-71) wrote the final and highly influential anatomy treatise of ancient times.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-pmid1081972-72) He compiled existing knowledge and studied anatomy through the dissection of animals.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-BritBrit-Galen-71) He was one of the first experimental physiologists through his [vivisection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivisection "Vivisection") experiments on animals.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-73) Galen's drawings, based mostly on dog anatomy, became effectively the only anatomical textbook for the next thousand years.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Boas-74) His work was known to [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance") doctors only through [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age") medicine until it was translated from Greek sometime in the 15th century.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Boas-74)

### Medieval to early modern

[Michiel Jansz van Mierevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michiel_Jansz_van_Mierevelt "Michiel Jansz van Mierevelt") – *Anatomy lesson of Dr. Willem van der Meer*, 1617

Anatomy developed little from classical times until the sixteenth century; as the historian Marie Boas writes, "Progress in anatomy before the sixteenth century is as mysteriously slow as its development after 1500 is startlingly rapid".[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Boas-74): 120–121  Between 1275 and 1326, the anatomists [Mondino de Luzzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondino_de_Luzzi "Mondino de Luzzi"), [Alessandro Achillini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Achillini "Alessandro Achillini") and [Antonio Benivieni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Benivieni "Antonio Benivieni") at [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna "Bologna") carried out the first systematic human dissections since ancient times.[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ZimmermanVeith1993-75)[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Crombie1959-76)[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Thorndike1958-77) Mondino's *Anatomy* of 1316 was the first textbook in the medieval rediscovery of human anatomy. It describes the body in the order followed in Mondino's dissections, starting with the abdomen, thorax, head, and limbs. It was the standard anatomy textbook for the next century.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Boas-74)

[Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci "Leonardo da Vinci") (1452–1519) was trained in anatomy by [Andrea del Verrocchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_del_Verrocchio "Andrea del Verrocchio").[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Boas-74) He made use of his anatomical knowledge in his artwork, making many sketches of skeletal structures, muscles and organs of humans and other vertebrates that he dissected.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Boas-74)[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-78)

[Andreas Vesalius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Vesalius "Andreas Vesalius") (1514–1564), professor of anatomy at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua "University of Padua"), is considered the founder of modern human anatomy.[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-79) Originally from [Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant "Duchy of Brabant"), Vesalius published the influential book *[De humani corporis fabrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_humani_corporis_fabrica "De humani corporis fabrica")* ("the structure of the human body"), a large format book in seven volumes, in 1543.[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-80) The accurate and intricately detailed illustrations, often in [allegorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegorical "Allegorical") poses against Italianate landscapes, are thought to have been made by the artist [Jan van Calcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Calcar "Jan van Calcar"), a pupil of [Titian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titian "Titian").[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-81)

In England, anatomy was the subject of the first public lectures given in any science; these were provided by the [Company of Barbers and Surgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barber_surgeon "Barber surgeon") in the 16th century, joined in 1583 by the Lumleian lectures in surgery at the [Royal College of Physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians "Royal College of Physicians").[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Boas229-82)

### Late modern

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anatomy\&action=edit\&section=20 "Edit section: Late modern")]

Further information: [History of anatomy in the 19th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_anatomy_in_the_19th_century "History of anatomy in the 19th century")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Lektion_i_anatomi_vid_Gymnastiska_Centralinstitutet_Stockholm_kvinnliga_kursen_1891-1893_gih0124.jpg/220px-Lektion_i_anatomi_vid_Gymnastiska_Centralinstitutet_Stockholm_kvinnliga_kursen_1891-1893_gih0124.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lektion_i_anatomi_vid_Gymnastiska_Centralinstitutet_Stockholm_kvinnliga_kursen_1891-1893_gih0124.jpg)

Anatomy teaching with female students, 1891–1893

Medical schools began to be set up in the United States towards the end of the 18th century. Classes in anatomy needed a continual stream of cadavers for dissection, and these were difficult to obtain. Philadelphia, Baltimore, and New York were all renowned for [body snatching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_snatching "Body snatching") activity as criminals raided graveyards at night, removing newly buried corpses from their coffins.[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-trafficdead-83) A similar problem existed in Britain where demand for bodies became so great that grave-raiding and even [anatomy murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy_murder "Anatomy murder") were practised to obtain cadavers.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Rosner,_Lisa._2010-84) Some graveyards were, in consequence, protected with watchtowers. The practice was halted in Britain by the [Anatomy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy_Act "Anatomy Act") of 1832,[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-85)[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-86) while in the United States, similar legislation was enacted after the physician [William S. Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Forbes "William S. Forbes") of [Jefferson Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Medical_College "Jefferson Medical College") was found guilty in 1882 of "complicity with resurrectionists in the despoliation of graves in Lebanon Cemetery".[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-87)

The teaching of anatomy in Britain was transformed by Sir [John Struthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Struthers_\(anatomist\) "John Struthers (anatomist)"), [Regius Professor of Anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Professor_of_Anatomy_\(Aberdeen\) "Regius Professor of Anatomy (Aberdeen)") at the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen "University of Aberdeen") from 1863 to 1889. He was responsible for setting up the system of three years of "pre-clinical" academic teaching in the sciences underlying medicine, including especially anatomy. This system lasted until the reform of medical training in 1993 and 2003. As well as teaching, he collected many vertebrate skeletons for his museum of [comparative anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_anatomy "Comparative anatomy"), published over 70 research papers, and became famous for his public dissection of the [Tay Whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tay_Whale "Tay Whale").[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-pmid17373426-88)[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-pmid15712576-89) From 1822 the Royal College of Surgeons regulated the teaching of anatomy in medical schools.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-McLachlan,_J._2006._p.243-53-90) Medical museums provided examples in comparative anatomy, and were often used in teaching.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-91) [Ignaz Semmelweis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignaz_Semmelweis "Ignaz Semmelweis") investigated [puerperal fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerperal_fever "Puerperal fever") and he discovered how it was caused. He noticed that the frequently fatal fever occurred more often in mothers examined by medical students than by midwives. The students went from the dissecting room to the hospital ward and examined women in childbirth. Semmelweis showed that when the trainees washed their hands in chlorinated lime before each clinical examination, the incidence of puerperal fever among the mothers could be reduced dramatically.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-92)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Siemens-electron-microscope.jpg/170px-Siemens-electron-microscope.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Siemens-electron-microscope.jpg)

An electron microscope from 1973

Before the modern medical era, the primary means for studying the internal structures of the body were [dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection "Dissection") of the dead and [inspection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspection "Inspection"), [palpation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palpation "Palpation"), and [auscultation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auscultation "Auscultation") of the living. The advent of [microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscopy "Microscopy") opened up an understanding of the building blocks that constituted living tissues. Technical advances in the development of [achromatic lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achromatic_lens "Achromatic lens") increased the [resolving power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_resolution "Angular resolution") of the microscope, and around 1839, [Matthias Jakob Schleiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Jakob_Schleiden "Matthias Jakob Schleiden") and [Theodor Schwann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Schwann "Theodor Schwann") identified that cells were the fundamental unit of organization of all living things. The study of small structures involved passing light through them, and the [microtome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtome "Microtome") was invented to provide sufficiently thin slices of tissue to examine. Staining techniques using artificial dyes were established to help distinguish between different tissue types. Advances in the fields of [histology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histology "Histology") and [cytology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytology "Cytology") began in the late 19th century[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-BritMicro-93) along with advances in surgical techniques allowing for the painless and safe removal of [biopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biopsy "Biopsy") specimens. The invention of the [electron microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscope "Electron microscope") brought a significant advance in resolution power and allowed research into the [ultrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrastructure "Ultrastructure") of cells and the [organelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organelle "Organelle") and other structures within them. About the same time, in the 1950s, the use of [X-ray diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_diffraction "X-ray diffraction") for studying the crystal structures of proteins, nucleic acids, and other biological molecules gave rise to a new field of [molecular anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_anatomy "Molecular anatomy").[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-BritMicro-93)

Equally important advances have occurred in *non-invasive* techniques for examining the body's interior structures. [X-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray "X-ray") can be passed through the body and used in medical [radiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiography "Radiography") and [fluoroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluoroscopy "Fluoroscopy") to differentiate interior structures that have varying degrees of opaqueness. [Magnetic resonance imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging "Magnetic resonance imaging"), [computed tomography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computed_tomography "Computed tomography"), and [ultrasound imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasound_imaging "Ultrasound imaging") have all enabled the examination of internal structures in unprecedented detail to a degree far beyond the imagination of earlier generations.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-:1-6)
