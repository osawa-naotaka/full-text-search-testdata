The **foreign relations of Angola** are based on Angola's strong support of [U.S. foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._foreign_policy "U.S. foreign policy") as the [Angolan economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_economy "Angolan economy") is dependent on U.S. foreign aid. From 1975 to 1989, Angola was aligned with the [Eastern bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_bloc "Eastern bloc"), in particular the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"),[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2008-1) [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya_under_Muammar_Gaddafi#Great_Socialist_People's_Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya_\(1977%E2%80%932012\) "History of Libya under Muammar Gaddafi"),\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] and [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2008-1) Since then, it has focused on improving relationships with [Western countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world "Western world"), cultivating links with other Portuguese-speaking countries, and asserting its own national interests in Central Africa through military and diplomatic intervention.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2008-1) In 1993, it established formal diplomatic relations with the United States.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2008-1) It has entered the [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community "Southern African Development Community") as a vehicle for improving ties with its largely Anglophone neighbors to the south.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2008-1) [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe "Zimbabwe") and [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia") joined Angola in its military intervention in the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo"), where Angolan troops remain in support of the [Joseph Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kabila "Joseph Kabila") government.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2008-1) It also has intervened in the [Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Congo "Republic of the Congo") (Brazzaville) in support of Denis Sassou-Nguesso in the civil war.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2008-1)

Since 1998, Angola has successfully worked with the [United Nations Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council") to impose and carry out sanctions on [UNITA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNITA "UNITA").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2001-2) More recently, it has extended those efforts to controls on [conflict diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_diamonds "Conflict diamonds"), the primary source of revenue for UNITA during the Civil War that ended in 2002.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2001-2) At the same time, Angola has promoted the revival of the Community of Portuguese-Speaking Countries ([CPLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPLP "CPLP")) as a forum for cultural exchange and expanding ties with [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal "Portugal") (its former ruler) and Brazil (which shares many cultural affinities with Angola) in particular.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2008-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_2001-2) Angola is a member of the [Port Management Association of Eastern and Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Management_Association_of_Eastern_and_Southern_Africa "Port Management Association of Eastern and Southern Africa") (PMAESA).
