**Algae** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English "British English"): [/ˈælɡiː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*AL-ghee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"), [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English "American English"): [/ˈældʒiː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*AL-jee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key");[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-3) sg.: **alga** [/ˈælɡə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*AL-gə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key")) is an informal term for any [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism "Organism") of a large and diverse group of [photosynthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis "Photosynthesis") [eukaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote"), which include [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species") from multiple distinct [clades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade "Clade"). Such organisms range from [unicellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicellular "Unicellular") [microalgae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microalgae "Microalgae") such as *[Chlorella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorella "Chlorella")*, *[Prototheca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prototheca "Prototheca")* and the [diatoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatom "Diatom"), to [multicellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular "Multicellular") [macroalgae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroalgae "Macroalgae") such as the [giant kelp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrocystis_pyrifera "Macrocystis pyrifera"), a large [brown alga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_algae "Brown algae") which may grow up to 50 metres (160 ft) in length. Most algae are aquatic organisms and lack many of the distinct cell and tissue types, such as [stomata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoma "Stoma"), [xylem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylem "Xylem") and [phloem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phloem "Phloem") that are found in [land plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryophyte "Embryophyte"). The largest and most complex marine algae are called [seaweeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed "Seaweed"). In contrast, the most complex freshwater forms are the *[Charophyta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charophyta "Charophyta")*, a [division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylum "Phylum") of [green algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_algae "Green algae") which includes, for example, *[Spirogyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirogyra "Spirogyra")* and [stoneworts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewort "Stonewort"). Algae that are carried passively by water are [plankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plankton "Plankton"), specifically [phytoplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton "Phytoplankton").

Algae constitute a [polyphyletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphyletic "Polyphyletic") group[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Nabors-2004-4) since they do not include a [common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ancestor "Common ancestor"), and although their [chlorophyll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyll "Chlorophyll")-bearing [plastids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastid "Plastid") seem to have a single origin (from [symbiogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiogenesis "Symbiogenesis") with [cyanobacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanobacteria "Cyanobacteria")),[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Keeling-2004-5) they were acquired in different ways. Green algae are a prominent examples of algae that have primary [chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast") derived from [endosymbiont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosymbiont "Endosymbiont") cyanobacteria. [Diatoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatom "Diatom") and brown algae are examples of algae with secondary chloroplasts derived from endosymbiotic [red algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_algae "Red algae"), which they acquired via [phagocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocytosis "Phagocytosis").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-6) Algae exhibit a wide range of reproductive strategies, from simple [asexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asexual_reproduction "Asexual reproduction") cell division to complex forms of [sexual reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_reproduction "Sexual reproduction") via [spores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spore "Spore").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7)

Algae lack the various structures that characterize [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant "Plant") (which evolved from freshwater green algae), such as the phyllids (leaf-like structures) and [rhizoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhizoid "Rhizoid") of [bryophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryophyte "Bryophyte") ([non-vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-vascular_plant "Non-vascular plant")), and the [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root "Root"), [leaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaf "Leaf") and other [xylemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylem "Xylem")/[phloemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phloem "Phloem") [organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_\(biology\) "Organ (biology)") found in [tracheophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracheophyte "Tracheophyte") ([vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant "Vascular plant")). Most algae are [autotrophic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autotroph "Autotroph"), although some are [mixotrophic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixotroph "Mixotroph"), deriving energy both from photosynthesis and uptake of organic carbon either by [osmotrophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmotrophy "Osmotrophy"), [myzotrophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myzocytosis "Myzocytosis") or [phagotrophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocytosis "Phagocytosis"). Some unicellular species of green algae, many [golden algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_algae "Golden algae"), [euglenids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euglenid "Euglenid"), [dinoflagellates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinoflagellate "Dinoflagellate"), and other algae have become [heterotrophs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotroph "Heterotroph") (also called colorless or apochlorotic algae), sometimes [parasitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasitic "Parasitic"), relying entirely on external energy sources and have limited or no photosynthetic apparatus.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10) Some other heterotrophic organisms, such as the [apicomplexans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apicomplexans "Apicomplexans"), are also derived from cells whose ancestors possessed chlorophyllic plastids, but are not traditionally considered as algae. Algae have photosynthetic machinery ultimately derived from cyanobacteria that produce [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen "Oxygen") as a [byproduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byproduct "Byproduct") of splitting [water molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_molecule "Water molecule"), unlike other organisms that conduct [anoxygenic photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anoxygenic_photosynthesis "Anoxygenic photosynthesis") such as [purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_sulfur_bacteria "Purple sulfur bacteria") and [green sulfur bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_sulfur_bacteria "Green sulfur bacteria"). Fossilized filamentous algae from the [Vindhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindhya "Vindhya") basin have been dated to 1.6 to 1.7 billion years ago.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-11)

Because of the wide range of algae types, they have increasingly different industrial and traditional applications in human society. Traditional [seaweed farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed_farming "Seaweed farming") practices have existed for thousands of years and have strong traditions in [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") food cultures. More modern [algaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algaculture "Algaculture") applications extend the [food traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edible_seaweed "Edible seaweed") for other applications, including cattle feed, using algae for [bioremediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioremediation "Bioremediation") or pollution control, transforming sunlight into [algae fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_fuel "Algae fuel") or other chemicals used in industrial processes, and in medical and scientific applications. A 2020 review found that these applications of algae could play an important role in [carbon sequestration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sequestration "Carbon sequestration") to [mitigate climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation "Climate change mitigation") while providing lucrative value-added products for global economies.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-12)

## Etymology and study

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: Etymology and study")]

The singular *alga* is the Latin word for 'seaweed' and retains that meaning in English.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-13) The [etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology "Etymology") is obscure. Although some speculate that it is related to Latin *algēre*, 'be cold',[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-14) no reason is known to associate seaweed with temperature. A more likely source is *alliga*, 'binding, entwining'.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-15)

The [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") word for 'seaweed' was φῦκος (*phŷkos*), which could mean either the seaweed (probably red algae) or a red dye derived from it. The Latinization, *fūcus*, meant primarily the cosmetic rouge. The etymology is uncertain, but a strong candidate has long been some word related to the [Biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_Hebrew "Biblical Hebrew") פוך (*pūk*), 'paint' (if not that word itself), a [cosmetic eye-shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohl_\(cosmetics\) "Kohl (cosmetics)") used by the [ancient Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") and other inhabitants of the eastern Mediterranean. It could be any color: black, red, green, or blue.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-16)

The study of algae is most commonly called [phycology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phycology "Phycology") (from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") *phykos* 'seaweed'); the term [algology](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/algology "wikt:algology") is falling out of use.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-17)

## Classifications

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Classifications")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Gephyrocapsa_oceanica_color.jpg/220px-Gephyrocapsa_oceanica_color.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gephyrocapsa_oceanica_color.jpg)

False-color [scanning electron micrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scanning_electron_micrograph "Scanning electron micrograph") of the unicellular [coccolithophore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coccolithophore "Coccolithophore") *[Gephyrocapsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gephyrocapsa "Gephyrocapsa") oceanica*

One definition of algae is that they "have [chlorophyll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyll "Chlorophyll") as their primary [photosynthetic pigment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthetic_pigment "Photosynthetic pigment") and lack a sterile covering of cells around their [reproductive cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete "Gamete")".[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-18) On the other hand, the colorless *Prototheca* under *[Chlorophyta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyta "Chlorophyta")* are all devoid of any chlorophyll. Although [cyanobacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanobacteria "Cyanobacteria") are often referred to as "blue-green algae", most authorities exclude all [prokaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryote "Prokaryote"), including cyanobacteria, from the definition of algae.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Nabors-2004-4)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-19)

The algae contain [chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast") that are similar in structure to cyanobacteria. Chloroplasts contain circular [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") like that in cyanobacteria and are interpreted as representing reduced endosymbiotic [cyanobacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanobacteria "Cyanobacteria"). However, the exact origin of the chloroplasts is different among separate lineages of algae, reflecting their acquisition during different endosymbiotic events. Many of these groups contain some members that are no longer photosynthetic. Some retain plastids, but not chloroplasts, while others have lost plastids entirely.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-20)

Title page of [Gmelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Gottlieb_Gmelin "Samuel Gottlieb Gmelin")'s *Historia Fucorum*, dated 1768

[Linnaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Linnaeus "Carl Linnaeus"), in *[Species Plantarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_Plantarum "Species Plantarum")* (1753),[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-30) the starting point for modern [botanical nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_nomenclature "Botanical nomenclature"), recognized 14 genera of algae, of which only four are currently considered among algae.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-31) In *[Systema Naturae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_edition_of_Systema_Naturae "10th edition of Systema Naturae")*, Linnaeus described the genera *[Volvox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvox "Volvox")* and *[Corallina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corallina "Corallina")*, and a species of *[Acetabularia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetabularia "Acetabularia")* (as *[Madrepora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrepora "Madrepora")*), among the animals.

In 1768, [Samuel Gottlieb Gmelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Gottlieb_Gmelin "Samuel Gottlieb Gmelin") (1744–1774) published the *Historia Fucorum*, the first work dedicated to marine algae and the first book on [marine biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biology "Marine biology") to use the then new binomial nomenclature of Linnaeus. It included elaborate illustrations of seaweed and marine algae on folded leaves.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-32)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-33)

[W. H. Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Harvey "William Henry Harvey") (1811–1866) and [Lamouroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamouroux "Lamouroux") (1813)[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Medlin-1997-34) were the first to divide macroscopic algae into four divisions based on their pigmentation. This is the first use of a biochemical criterion in plant systematics. Harvey's four divisions are: red algae (Rhodospermae), brown algae (Melanospermae), green algae (Chlorospermae), and Diatomaceae.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-35)[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-36)

At this time, microscopic algae were discovered and reported by a different group of workers (e.g., [O. F. Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Friedrich_M%C3%BCller "Otto Friedrich Müller") and [Ehrenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Gottfried_Ehrenberg "Christian Gottfried Ehrenberg")) studying the [Infusoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infusoria "Infusoria") (microscopic organisms). Unlike [macroalgae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroalgae "Macroalgae"), which were clearly viewed as plants, [microalgae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microalgae "Microalgae") were frequently considered animals because they are often motile.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Medlin-1997-34) Even the nonmotile (coccoid) microalgae were sometimes merely seen as stages of the lifecycle of plants, macroalgae, or animals.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-37)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-38)

Although used as a taxonomic category in some pre-Darwinian classifications, e.g., Linnaeus (1753),[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Ragan-2010-39) de Jussieu (1789),[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-de_Jussieu-1789-40) Lamouroux (1813), Harvey (1836), Horaninow (1843), Agassiz (1859), Wilson & Cassin (1864),[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Ragan-2010-39) in further classifications, the "algae" are seen as an artificial, polyphyletic group.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Khan-2020b-41)

Throughout the 20th century, most classifications treated the following groups as divisions or classes of algae: [cyanophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanophyte "Cyanophyte"), [rhodophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodophyte "Rhodophyte"), [chrysophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysophyte "Chrysophyte"), [xanthophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xanthophyte "Xanthophyte"), [bacillariophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatom "Diatom"), [phaeophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phaeophyte "Phaeophyte"), [pyrrhophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinoflagellate#History "Dinoflagellate") ([cryptophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptomonad "Cryptomonad") and [dinophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinophyte "Dinophyte")), [euglenophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euglenophyte "Euglenophyte"), and [chlorophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyte "Chlorophyte"). Later, many new groups were discovered (e.g., [Bolidophyceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolidophyceae "Bolidophyceae")), and others were splintered from older groups: [charophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charophyte "Charophyte") and [glaucophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaucophyte "Glaucophyte") (from chlorophytes), many [heterokontophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterokontophyte "Heterokontophyte") (e.g., [synurophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synurophyceae "Synurophyceae") from chrysophytes, or [eustigmatophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eustigmatophyte "Eustigmatophyte") from xanthophytes), [haptophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haptophyte "Haptophyte") (from chrysophytes), and [chlorarachniophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorarachniophyte "Chlorarachniophyte") (from xanthophytes).[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-42)

With the abandonment of plant-animal dichotomous classification, most groups of algae (sometimes all) were included in [Protista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protist "Protist"), later also abandoned in favour of [Eukaryota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryota "Eukaryota"). However, as a legacy of the older plant life scheme, some groups that were also treated as [protozoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protozoa "Protozoa") in the past still have duplicated classifications (see [ambiregnal protists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambiregnal_protist "Ambiregnal protist")).[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-43)

Some parasitic algae (e.g., the green algae *[Prototheca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prototheca "Prototheca")* and *[Helicosporidium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicosporidium "Helicosporidium")*, parasites of metazoans, or *[Cephaleuros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephaleuros "Cephaleuros")*, parasites of plants) were originally classified as [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi"), [sporozoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporozoan "Sporozoan"), or [protistans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protist "Protist") of *[incertae sedis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incertae_sedis "Incertae sedis")*,[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-44) while others (e.g., the green algae *[Phyllosiphon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllosiphon "Phyllosiphon")* and *[Rhodochytrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodochytrium "Rhodochytrium")*, parasites of plants, or the red algae *[Pterocladiophila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterocladiophila "Pterocladiophila")* and *Gelidiocolax mammillatus*, parasites of other red algae, or the dinoflagellates *[Oodinium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oodinium "Oodinium")*, parasites of fish) had their relationship with algae conjectured early. In other cases, some groups were originally characterized as parasitic algae (e.g., *[Chlorochytrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorochytrium "Chlorochytrium")*), but later were seen as [endophytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endophytic "Endophytic") algae.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-45) Some filamentous bacteria (e.g., *[Beggiatoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beggiatoa "Beggiatoa")*) were originally seen as algae. Furthermore, groups like the [apicomplexans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apicomplexan "Apicomplexan") are also parasites derived from ancestors that possessed plastids, but are not included in any group traditionally seen as algae.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-46)[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-47)

## Evolution

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=3 "Edit section: Evolution")]

Algae are [polyphyletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphyletic "Polyphyletic") thus their origin cannot be traced back to single hypothetical [common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ancestor "Common ancestor"). It is thought that they came into existence when photosynthetic [coccoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coccus "Coccus") [cyanobacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanobacteria "Cyanobacteria") got [phagocytized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocytosis "Phagocytosis") by a [unicellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicellular "Unicellular") [heterotrophic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotrophic "Heterotrophic") eukaryote (a [protist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protist "Protist")),[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Reyes-Prieto-2007-48) giving rise to double-membranous primary [plastids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastid "Plastid"). Such [symbiogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiogenesis "Symbiogenesis") events (primary symbiogenesis) are believed to have occurred more than 1.5 billion years ago during the [Calymmian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calymmian "Calymmian") [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_\(geology\) "Period (geology)"), early in [Boring Billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boring_Billion "Boring Billion"), but it is difficult to track the key events because of so much time gap.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Khan-2020a-49) Primary symbiogenesis gave rise to three [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(taxonomy\) "Division (taxonomy)") of [archaeplastids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeplastid "Archaeplastid"), namely the [Viridiplantae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viridiplantae "Viridiplantae") ([green algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_algae "Green algae") and later [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant "Plant")), [Rhodophyta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodophyta "Rhodophyta") ([red algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_algae "Red algae")) and [Glaucophyta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaucophyta "Glaucophyta") ("grey algae"), whose plastids further spread into other protist lineages through eukaryote-eukaryote [predation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predation "Predation"), engulfments and subsequent endosymbioses (secondary and tertiary symbiogenesis).[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Khan-2020a-49) This process of serial cell "capture" and "enslavement" explains the diversity of photosynthetic eukaryotes.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Reyes-Prieto-2007-48)

Recent [genomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genomic "Genomic") and [phylogenomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenomic "Phylogenomic") approaches have significantly clarified plastid [genome evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome_evolution "Genome evolution"), the [horizontal movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_gene_transfer "Horizontal gene transfer") of [endosymbiont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosymbiont "Endosymbiont") [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genes "Genes") to the "host" [nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus") [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome "Genome"), and plastid spread throughout the eukaryotic [tree of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_of_life_\(biology\) "Tree of life (biology)").[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Reyes-Prieto-2007-48)

### Relationship to land plants

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Relationship to land plants")]

Fossils of isolated [spores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spore "Spore") suggest [land plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_plant "Land plant") may have been around as long as 475 [million years ago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Million_years_ago "Million years ago") (mya) during the [Late Cambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Cambrian "Late Cambrian")/[Early Ordovician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Ordovician "Early Ordovician") period,[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-50)[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-51) from [sessile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sessility_\(motility\) "Sessility (motility)") shallow [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater "Freshwater") [charophyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charophyte "Charophyte") algae much like *[Chara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chara_\(alga\) "Chara (alga)")*,[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Kenrick-1997-52) which likely got stranded ashore when [riverine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverine "Riverine")/[lacustrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacustrine "Lacustrine") [water levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_level "Water level") dropped during [dry seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season "Dry season").[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Raven-2001-53) These charophyte algae probably already developed filamentous [thalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalli "Thalli") and [holdfasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holdfast_\(biology\) "Holdfast (biology)") that superficially resembled [plant stems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_stem "Plant stem") and [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root "Root"), and probably had an isomorphic [alternation of generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternation_of_generations "Alternation of generations"). They perhaps evolved some 850 mya[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Knauth-2009-54) and might even be as early as 1 [Gya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gya_\(unit\) "Gya (unit)") during the late phase of the [Boring Billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boring_Billion "Boring Billion").[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Strother-2011-55)

## Morphology

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Morphology")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8e/Kelp-forest-Monterey.jpg/170px-Kelp-forest-Monterey.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kelp-forest-Monterey.jpg)

The [kelp forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelp_forest "Kelp forest") exhibit at the Monterey Bay Aquarium: A three-dimensional, multicellular thallus

A range of algal [morphologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_\(biology\) "Morphology (biology)") is exhibited, and [convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_evolution "Convergent evolution") of features in unrelated groups is common. The only groups to exhibit three-dimensional multicellular [thalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thallus "Thallus") are the [reds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_algae "Red algae") and [browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_algae "Brown algae"), and some [chlorophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyta "Chlorophyta").[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Xiao-2004-56) Apical growth is constrained to subsets of these groups: the [florideophyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florideophyceae "Florideophyceae") reds, various browns, and the charophytes.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Xiao-2004-56) The form of charophytes is quite different from those of reds and browns, because they have distinct nodes, separated by internode 'stems'; whorls of branches reminiscent of the [horsetails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsetail "Horsetail") occur at the nodes.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Xiao-2004-56) [Conceptacles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptacle "Conceptacle") are another [polyphyletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphyletic "Polyphyletic") trait; they appear in the [coralline algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coralline_algae "Coralline algae") and the [Hildenbrandiales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildenbrandiales "Hildenbrandiales"), as well as the browns.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Xiao-2004-56)

Most of the simpler algae are unicellular [flagellates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagellate "Flagellate") or [amoeboids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoeboid "Amoeboid"), but colonial and nonmotile forms have developed independently among several of the groups. Some of the more common organizational levels, more than one of which may occur in the [lifecycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_life_cycle "Biological life cycle") of a species, are

- [Colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_\(biology\) "Colony (biology)"): small, regular groups of motile cells
- Capsoid: individual non-motile cells embedded in [mucilage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucilage "Mucilage")
- Coccoid: individual non-motile cells with cell walls
- Palmelloid: nonmotile cells embedded in mucilage
- Filamentous: a string of connected nonmotile cells, sometimes branching
- Parenchymatous: cells forming a thallus with partial differentiation of tissues

In three lines, even higher levels of organization have been reached, with full tissue differentiation. These are the brown algae,[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-57)—some of which may reach 50 m in length ([kelps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelp "Kelp"))[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-58)—the red algae,[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-59) and the green algae.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-60) The most complex forms are found among the charophyte algae (see [Charales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charales "Charales") and [Charophyta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charophyta "Charophyta")), in a lineage that eventually led to the higher land plants. The innovation that defines these nonalgal plants is the presence of female reproductive organs with protective cell layers that protect the zygote and developing embryo. Hence, the land plants are referred to as the [Embryophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryophyte "Embryophyte").

### Turfs

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=6 "Edit section: Turfs")]

The term algal turf is commonly used but poorly defined. Algal turfs are thick, carpet-like beds of seaweed that retain [sediment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment "Sediment") and compete with foundation species like [corals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef "Coral reef") and [kelps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelp_forest#Kelp "Kelp forest"), and they are usually less than 15 cm tall. Such a turf may consist of one or more species, and will generally cover an area in the order of a square metre or more. Some common characteristics are listed:[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Connell-2014-61)

- Algae that form aggregations that have been described as turfs include diatoms, cyanobacteria, chlorophytes, phaeophytes and rhodophytes. Turfs are often composed of numerous species at a wide range of spatial scales, but monospecific turfs are frequently reported.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Connell-2014-61)
- Turfs can be morphologically highly variable over geographic scales and even within species on local scales and can be difficult to identify in terms of the constituent species.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Connell-2014-61)
- Turfs have been defined as short algae, but this has been used to describe height ranges from less than 0.5 cm to more than 10 cm. In some regions, the descriptions approached heights which might be described as canopies (20 to 30 cm).[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Connell-2014-61)

## Physiology

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=7 "Edit section: Physiology")]

Many algae, particularly species of the [Characeae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characeae "Characeae"),[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-62) have served as model experimental organisms to understand the mechanisms of the water permeability of membranes, [osmoregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmoregulation "Osmoregulation"), [salt tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_tolerance "Salt tolerance"), [cytoplasmic streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoplasmic_streaming "Cytoplasmic streaming"), and the generation of [action potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potentials "Action potentials"). [Plant hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_hormones "Plant hormones") are found not only in higher plants, but in algae, too.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-63)

## Symbiotic algae

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=8 "Edit section: Symbiotic algae")]

Some species of algae form [symbiotic relationships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiosis "Symbiosis") with other organisms. In these symbioses, the algae supply photosynthates (organic substances) to the host organism providing protection to the algal cells. The host organism derives some or all of its energy requirements from the algae. Examples are:

### Lichens

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=9 "Edit section: Lichens")]

Main article: [Lichen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen "Lichen")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7e/Lichens_near_Clogher_Head_%28stevefe%29.jpg/220px-Lichens_near_Clogher_Head_%28stevefe%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lichens_near_Clogher_Head_\(stevefe\).jpg)

Rock lichens in Ireland

[Lichens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen "Lichen") are defined by the [International Association for Lichenology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_for_Lichenology "International Association for Lichenology") to be "an association of a fungus and a photosynthetic [symbiont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiont "Symbiont") resulting in a stable vegetative body having a specific structure".[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-64) The fungi, or mycobionts, are mainly from the [Ascomycota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascomycota "Ascomycota") with a few from the [Basidiomycota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basidiomycota "Basidiomycota"). In nature, they do not occur separate from lichens. It is unknown when they began to associate.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-65) One or more[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-66) mycobiont associates with the same phycobiont species, from the green algae, except that alternatively, the mycobiont may associate with a species of cyanobacteria (hence "photobiont" is the more accurate term). A photobiont may be associated with many different mycobionts or may live independently; accordingly, lichens are named and classified as fungal species.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-67) The association is termed a morphogenesis because the lichen has a form and capabilities not possessed by the symbiont species alone (they can be experimentally isolated). The photobiont possibly triggers otherwise latent genes in the mycobiont.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-68)

[Trentepohlia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentepohlia_\(alga\) "Trentepohlia (alga)") is an example of a common green alga genus worldwide that can grow on its own or be lichenised. Lichen thus share some of the habitat and often similar appearance with specialized species of algae (*[aerophytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerophyte "Aerophyte")*) growing on exposed surfaces such as tree trunks and rocks and sometimes discoloring them.

### Coral reefs

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=10 "Edit section: Coral reefs")]

Main articles: [Coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral "Coral"), [Coral reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef "Coral reef"), and [Symbiodinium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiodinium "Symbiodinium")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Coral_Reef.jpg/220px-Coral_Reef.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Coral_Reef.jpg)

Floridian coral reef

[Coral reefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef "Coral reef") are accumulated from the [calcareous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcareous "Calcareous") exoskeletons of [marine invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_invertebrate "Marine invertebrate") of the order [Scleractinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scleractinia "Scleractinia") (stony [corals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral "Coral")). These animals [metabolize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism "Metabolism") sugar and oxygen to obtain energy for their cell-building processes, including [secretion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretion "Secretion") of the exoskeleton, with water and [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide") as byproducts. Dinoflagellates (algal protists) are often [endosymbionts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosymbiont "Endosymbiont") in the cells of the coral-forming marine invertebrates, where they accelerate host-cell metabolism by generating sugar and oxygen immediately available through photosynthesis using incident light and the carbon dioxide produced by the host. Reef-building stony corals ([hermatypic corals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermatypic_coral "Hermatypic coral")) require endosymbiotic algae from the genus *[Symbiodinium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiodinium "Symbiodinium")* to be in a healthy condition.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-69) The loss of *Symbiodinium* from the host is known as [coral bleaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_bleaching "Coral bleaching"), a condition which leads to the deterioration of a reef.

### Sea sponges

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=11 "Edit section: Sea sponges")]

Main article: [Sea sponge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_sponge "Sea sponge")

[Endosymbiontic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosymbiont "Endosymbiont") green algae live close to the surface of some sponges, for example, breadcrumb sponges (*[Halichondria panicea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halichondria_panicea "Halichondria panicea")*). The alga is thus protected from predators; the sponge is provided with oxygen and sugars which can account for 50 to 80% of sponge growth in some species.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-70)

## Life cycle

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=12 "Edit section: Life cycle")]

Further information: [Conceptacle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptacle "Conceptacle")

[Rhodophyta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_algae "Red algae"), [Chlorophyta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyta "Chlorophyta"), and [Heterokontophyta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterokont "Heterokont"), the three main algal [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylum "Phylum"), have life cycles which show considerable variation and complexity. In general, an asexual phase exists where the seaweed's cells are [diploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ploidy "Ploidy"), a sexual phase where the cells are [haploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haploid "Haploid"), followed by fusion of the male and female [gametes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete "Gamete"). Asexual reproduction permits efficient population increases, but less variation is possible. Commonly, in sexual reproduction of unicellular and colonial algae, two specialized, sexually compatible, haploid gametes make physical contact and fuse to form a [zygote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygote "Zygote"). To ensure a successful mating, the development and release of gametes is highly synchronized and regulated; pheromones may play a key role in these processes.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-71) Sexual reproduction allows for more variation and provides the benefit of efficient recombinational repair of DNA damages during [meiosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiosis "Meiosis"), a key stage of the sexual cycle.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-72) However, sexual reproduction is more costly than asexual reproduction.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-73) Meiosis has been shown to occur in many different species of algae.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-74)

## Numbers

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=13 "Edit section: Numbers")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Taiwan_2009_East_Coast_ShihTiPing_Giant_Stone_Steps_Algae_FRD_6581.jpg/220px-Taiwan_2009_East_Coast_ShihTiPing_Giant_Stone_Steps_Algae_FRD_6581.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Taiwan_2009_East_Coast_ShihTiPing_Giant_Stone_Steps_Algae_FRD_6581.jpg)

Algae on coastal rocks at [Shihtiping](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%9F%B3%E6%A2%AF%E5%9D%AA "zh:石梯坪") in [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan")

The *Algal Collection of the US National Herbarium* (located in the [National Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Natural_History "National Museum of Natural History")) consists of approximately 320,500 dried specimens, which, although not exhaustive (no exhaustive collection exists), gives an idea of the order of magnitude of the number of algal species (that number remains unknown).[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-75) Estimates vary widely. For example, according to one standard textbook,[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-76) in the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles "British Isles"), the *UK Biodiversity Steering Group Report* estimated there to be 20,000 algal species in the UK. Another checklist reports only about 5,000 species. Regarding the difference of about 15,000 species, the text concludes: "It will require many detailed field surveys before it is possible to provide a reliable estimate of the total number of species ..."

Regional and group estimates have been made, as well:

- 5,000–5,500 species of red algae worldwide
- "some 1,300 in Australian Seas"[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-77)
- 400 seaweed species for the western coastline of South Africa,[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-78) and 212 species from the coast of KwaZulu-Natal.[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-79) Some of these are duplicates, as the range extends across both coasts, and the total recorded is probably about 500 species. Most of these are listed in [List of seaweeds of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_seaweeds_of_South_Africa_\(disambiguation\) "List of seaweeds of South Africa (disambiguation)"). These exclude [phytoplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton "Phytoplankton") and crustose corallines.
- 669 marine species from California (US)[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-80)
- 642 in the check-list of Britain and Ireland[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-81)

and so on, but lacking any scientific basis or reliable sources, these numbers have no more credibility than the British ones mentioned above. Most estimates also omit microscopic algae, such as phytoplankton.

The most recent estimate suggests 72,500 algal species worldwide.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-82)

## Distribution

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=14 "Edit section: Distribution")]

The distribution of algal species has been fairly well studied since the founding of [phytogeography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytogeography "Phytogeography") in the mid-19th century.[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Round-1981-83) Algae spread mainly by the dispersal of [spores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spore "Spore") analogously to the dispersal of [cryptogamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptogam "Cryptogam") [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant "Plant") by [spores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spore "Spore"). Spores can be found in a variety of environments: fresh and marine waters, air, soil, and in or on other organisms.[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Round-1981-83) Whether a spore is to grow into an adult organism depends on the species and the environmental conditions where the spore lands.

The spores of freshwater algae are dispersed mainly by running water and wind, as well as by living carriers.[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Round-1981-83) However, not all bodies of water can carry all species of algae, as the chemical composition of certain water bodies limits the algae that can survive within them.[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Round-1981-83) Marine spores are often spread by ocean currents. Ocean water presents many vastly different habitats based on temperature and nutrient availability, resulting in phytogeographic zones, regions, and provinces.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-84)

To some degree, the distribution of algae is subject to floristic discontinuities caused by geographical features, such as [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica"), long distances of ocean or general land masses. It is, therefore, possible to identify species occurring by locality, such as "[Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific "Pacific") algae" or "[North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea "North Sea") algae". When they occur out of their localities, hypothesizing a transport mechanism is usually possible, such as the hulls of ships. For example, *[Ulva reticulata](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ulva_reticulata\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Ulva reticulata (page does not exist)")* and *[U. fasciata](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=U._fasciata\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "U. fasciata (page does not exist)")* travelled from the mainland to [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii "Hawaii") in this manner.

Mapping is possible for select species only: "there are many valid examples of confined distribution patterns."[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-85) For example, *[Clathromorphum](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Clathromorphum\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Clathromorphum (page does not exist)")* is an arctic genus and is not mapped far south of there.[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-86) However, scientists regard the overall data as insufficient due to the "difficulties of undertaking such studies."[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-87)

## Ecology

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=15 "Edit section: Ecology")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Phytoplankton_Lake_Chuzenji.jpg/220px-Phytoplankton_Lake_Chuzenji.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Phytoplankton_Lake_Chuzenji.jpg)

Phytoplankton, [Lake Chūzenji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ch%C5%ABzenji "Lake Chūzenji")

Algae are prominent in bodies of water, common in terrestrial environments, and are found in unusual environments, such as on [snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_algae "Snow algae") and [ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_algae "Ice algae"). Seaweeds grow mostly in shallow marine waters, under 100 m (330 ft) deep; however, some such as *[Navicula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navicula "Navicula") pennata* have been recorded to a depth of 360 m (1,180 ft).[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-88) A type of algae, *Ancylonema nordenskioeldii*, was found in [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland "Greenland") in areas known as the 'Dark Zone', which caused an increase in the rate of melting ice sheet.[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-89) The same algae was found in the [Italian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Alps "Italian Alps"), after pink ice appeared on parts of the Presena glacier.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-90)

The various sorts of algae play significant roles in aquatic ecology. Microscopic forms that live suspended in the water column ([phytoplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton "Phytoplankton")) provide the food base for most marine [food chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_chain "Food chain"). In very high densities ([algal blooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algal_bloom "Algal bloom")), these algae may discolor the water and outcompete, poison, or [asphyxiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asphyxiate "Asphyxiate") other life forms.

Algae can be used as [indicator organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indicator_organism "Indicator organism") to monitor pollution in various aquatic systems.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Omar-2010-91) In many cases, algal metabolism is sensitive to various pollutants. Due to this, the species composition of algal populations may shift in the presence of chemical pollutants.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Omar-2010-91) To detect these changes, algae can be sampled from the environment and maintained in laboratories with relative ease.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Omar-2010-91)

On the basis of their habitat, algae can be categorized as: [aquatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_plant "Aquatic plant") ([planktonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton "Phytoplankton"), [benthic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benthic_zone "Benthic zone"), [marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biology "Marine biology"), [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_ecosystem "Freshwater ecosystem"), [lentic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_ecosystem "Lake ecosystem"), [lotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_ecosystem "River ecosystem")),[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-92) [terrestrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrestrial_plant "Terrestrial plant"), [aerial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerobiology "Aerobiology") (subaerial),[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-93) [lithophytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithophyte "Lithophyte"), [halophytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halophyte "Halophyte") (or [euryhaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euryhaline "Euryhaline")), [psammon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psammon "Psammon"), [thermophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermophile "Thermophile"), [cryophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychrophile "Psychrophile"), [epibiont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epibiont "Epibiont") ([epiphytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphyte "Epiphyte"), [epizoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epizoic "Epizoic")), [endosymbiont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosymbiont "Endosymbiont") ([endophytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endophytic "Endophytic"), endozoic), [parasitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasitic "Parasitic"), [calcifilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcareous "Calcareous") or [lichenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen "Lichen") (phycobiont).[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-94)

## Cultural associations

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=16 "Edit section: Cultural associations")]

In [classical Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Chinese "Classical Chinese"), the word [藻](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%97%BB "wikt:藻") is used both for "algae" and (in the modest tradition of the [imperial scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholar-official "Scholar-official")) for "literary talent". ## Cultivation

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=17 "Edit section: Cultivation")]

This section is an excerpt from [Algaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algaculture "Algaculture").\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algaculture\&action=edit)]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Seaweed_farm_uroa_zanzibar.jpg/220px-Seaweed_farm_uroa_zanzibar.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Seaweed_farm_uroa_zanzibar.jpg)

A seaweed farm in [Uroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uroa "Uroa"), Zanzibar

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Algoculture_au_kibboutz_Ketura.JPG/220px-Algoculture_au_kibboutz_Ketura.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Algoculture_au_kibboutz_Ketura.JPG)

Algaculture in [Kibbutz Ketura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketura,_Israel "Ketura, Israel"), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel")

[Algaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algaculture "Algaculture") is a form of [aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture "Aquaculture") involving the farming of species of algae.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Algaculture_:1-95)

The majority of algae that are intentionally cultivated fall into the category of [microalgae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microalgae "Microalgae") (also referred to as [phytoplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton "Phytoplankton"), [microphytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphytes "Microphytes"), or [planktonic algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planktonic_algae "Planktonic algae")). [Macroalgae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroalgae "Macroalgae"), commonly known as [seaweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed "Seaweed"), also have many commercial and industrial uses, but due to their size and the specific requirements of the environment in which they need to grow, they do not lend themselves as readily to cultivation (this may change, however, with the advent of newer seaweed cultivators, which are basically [algae scrubbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_scrubber "Algae scrubber") using upflowing air bubbles in small containers, known as tumble culture).[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-96)

Commercial and industrial algae cultivation has numerous uses, including production of [nutraceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutraceutical "Nutraceutical") such as [omega-3 fatty acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega-3_fatty_acids "Omega-3 fatty acids") (as algal oil)[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-97)[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-98)[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-99) or natural food [colorants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorants "Colorants") and [dyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyes "Dyes"), [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food "Food"), [fertilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilizer "Fertilizer"), [bioplastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioplastics "Bioplastics"), chemical feedstock (raw material), protein-rich animal/[aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_fish_feed "Commercial fish feed") feed, [pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceuticals "Pharmaceuticals"), and [algal fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algal_fuel "Algal fuel"),[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10.1007/s43615-021-00084-3-100) and can also be used as a means of [pollution control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution_control "Pollution control") and [natural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature-based_solutions "Nature-based solutions") [carbon sequestration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sequestration#Seaweed_farming_and_algae "Carbon sequestration").[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10.3389/fnut.2022.1029841-101)

Global production of farmed aquatic plants, overwhelmingly dominated by seaweeds, grew in output volume from 13.5 million tonnes in 1995, to just over 30 million tonnes in 2016 and 37.8 million tonnes in 2022.[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Algaculture_:12-102)[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Algaculture_:0-103) This increase was the result of production expansions led by China, followed by Malaysia, the Philippines, the United Republic of Tanzania, the Russian Federation.[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Algaculture_:12-102)

Cultured microalgae already contribute to a wide range of sectors in the emerging [bioeconomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioeconomy "Bioeconomy").[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-104) Research suggests there are large potentials and benefits of algaculture for the development of a future [healthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthy_diet "Healthy diet") and [sustainable food system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_food_system "Sustainable food system").[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10.5670/oceanog.2022.213-105)[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10.3389/fnut.2022.1029841-101)

### Seaweed farming

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=18 "Edit section: Seaweed farming")]

This section is an excerpt from [Seaweed farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed_farming "Seaweed farming").\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Seaweed_farming\&action=edit)]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Eucheuma_farming%2C_Philippines_%285211726476%29.jpg/300px-Eucheuma_farming%2C_Philippines_%285211726476%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Eucheuma_farming,_Philippines_\(5211726476\).jpg)

Underwater *[Eucheuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucheuma "Eucheuma")* farming in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines")

[![A seaweed farmer stands in shallow water, gathering edible seaweed that has grown on a rope](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/Seaweed_farming_-Nusa_Lembongan%2C_Bali-16Aug2009_edit.jpg/300px-Seaweed_farming_-Nusa_Lembongan%2C_Bali-16Aug2009_edit.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Seaweed_farming_-Nusa_Lembongan,_Bali-16Aug2009_edit.jpg)

A seaweed farmer in [Nusa Lembongan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusa_Lembongan "Nusa Lembongan") (Indonesia) gathers edible seaweed that has grown on a rope.

[Seaweed farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed_farming "Seaweed farming") or [kelp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelp "Kelp") farming is the practice of [cultivating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture "Aquaculture") and [harvesting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvest "Harvest") [seaweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed "Seaweed"). In its simplest form farmers gather from natural beds, while at the other extreme farmers fully control the crop's [life cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_life_cycle "Biological life cycle").

The seven most cultivated [taxa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxa "Taxa") are *[Eucheuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucheuma "Eucheuma")* spp., *[Kappaphycus alvarezii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kappaphycus_alvarezii "Kappaphycus alvarezii")*, *[Gracilaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracilaria "Gracilaria")* spp., *[Saccharina japonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saccharina_japonica "Saccharina japonica")*, *[Undaria pinnatifida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undaria_pinnatifida "Undaria pinnatifida")*, *[Pyropia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyropia "Pyropia")* spp., and *[Sargassum fusiforme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargassum_fusiforme "Sargassum fusiforme")*. *Eucheuma* and *K. alvarezii* are attractive for [carrageenan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrageenan "Carrageenan") (a [gelling agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelling_agent "Gelling agent")); *Gracilaria* is farmed for [agar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agar "Agar"); the rest are eaten after limited processing.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seaweed_farming_:3-106) Seaweeds are different from [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangroves "Mangroves") and [seagrasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagrasses "Seagrasses"), as they are photosynthetic algal organisms[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-107) and are non-flowering.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seaweed_farming_:3-106)

The largest seaweed-producing countries as of 2022 are [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") (58.62%) and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") (28.6%); followed by [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea") (5.09%) and the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") (4.19%). Other notable producers include [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea") (1.6%), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") (1.15%), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") (0.53%), [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar "Zanzibar") ([Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania"), 0.5%), and [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile "Chile") (0.3%).[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seaweed_farming_Zhang2022-108)[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seaweed_farming_Buschmann-109) Seaweed farming has frequently been developed to improve economic conditions and to reduce fishing pressure.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seaweed_farming_ask-110)

The [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization "Food and Agriculture Organization") (FAO) reported that world production in 2019 was over 35 million tonnes. North America produced some 23,000 tonnes of wet seaweed. Alaska, Maine, France, and Norway each more than doubled their seaweed production since 201&#x38;*.* As of 2019, seaweed represented 30% of marine [aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture "Aquaculture").[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seaweed_farming_:4-111) In 2023, the global seaweed extract market was valued at $16.5 billion, with strong projected growth.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-112)

Seaweed farming is a [carbon negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_removal "Carbon dioxide removal") crop, with a high potential for [climate change mitigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation "Climate change mitigation").[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seaweed_farming_:0-113)[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seaweed_farming_:1-114) The IPCC [Special Report on the Ocean and Cryosphere in a Changing Climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Report_on_the_Ocean_and_Cryosphere_in_a_Changing_Climate "Special Report on the Ocean and Cryosphere in a Changing Climate") recommends "further research attention" as a mitigation tactic.[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seaweed_farming_:2-115) [World Wildlife Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wildlife_Fund "World Wildlife Fund"), Oceans 2050, and [The Nature Conservancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nature_Conservancy "The Nature Conservancy") publicly support expanded seaweed cultivation.[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seaweed_farming_:4-111)

### Bioreactors

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=19 "Edit section: Bioreactors")]

This section is an excerpt from [Algae bioreactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_bioreactor "Algae bioreactor").\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae_bioreactor\&action=edit)]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/CSIRO_ScienceImage_7604_Microalgae.jpg/280px-CSIRO_ScienceImage_7604_Microalgae.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:CSIRO_ScienceImage_7604_Microalgae.jpg)

A close up of microalgae – *Pavlova* sp.

An [algae bioreactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_bioreactor "Algae bioreactor") is used for cultivating [micro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microalgae "Microalgae") or [macroalgae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed "Seaweed"). Algae may be cultivated for the purposes of [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass "Biomass") production (as in a [seaweed cultivator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed_cultivator "Seaweed cultivator")), [wastewater treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wastewater_treatment "Wastewater treatment"), [CO2 fixation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_fixation "Carbon fixation"), or aquarium/pond filtration in the form of an [algae scrubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_scrubber "Algae scrubber").[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-116) Algae bioreactors vary widely in design, falling broadly into two categories: open reactors and enclosed reactors. Open reactors are exposed to the atmosphere while enclosed reactors, also commonly called [photobioreactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photobioreactor "Photobioreactor"), are isolated to varying extents from the atmosphere. Specifically, algae bioreactors can be used to produce fuels such as [biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel "Biodiesel") and [bioethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioethanol "Bioethanol"), to generate animal feed, or to reduce pollutants such as [NOx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOx "NOx") and [CO2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide") in flue

gases of power plants. Fundamentally, this kind of bioreactor is based on the [photosynthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthese "Photosynthese") reaction, which is performed by the [chlorophyll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyll "Chlorophyll")-containing algae itself using dissolved carbon dioxide and sunlight. The carbon dioxide is dispersed into the reactor fluid to make it accessible to the algae. The bioreactor has to be made out of transparent material.

## Uses

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=20 "Edit section: Uses")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Algae_Harvester.jpg/220px-Algae_Harvester.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Algae_Harvester.jpg)

Harvesting algae

### Agar

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=21 "Edit section: Agar")]

[Agar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agar "Agar"), a [gelatinous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelatin "Gelatin") substance derived from red algae, has a number of commercial uses.[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-117) It is a good medium on which to grow bacteria and fungi, as most microorganisms cannot digest agar.

### Alginates

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=22 "Edit section: Alginates")]

[Alginic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alginic_acid "Alginic acid"), or alginate, is extracted from [brown algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_algae "Brown algae"). Its uses range from gelling agents in food, to medical dressings. Alginic acid also has been used in the field of [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology "Biotechnology") as a [biocompatible medium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biocompatibility "Biocompatibility") for cell encapsulation and cell immobilization. [Molecular cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_cuisine "Molecular cuisine") is also a user of the substance for its gelling properties, by which it becomes a delivery vehicle for flavours.

Between 100,000 and 170,000 wet tons of *[Macrocystis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrocystis "Macrocystis")* are harvested annually in [New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico "New Mexico") for [alginate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alginic_acid "Alginic acid") extraction and [abalone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abalone "Abalone") feed.[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-118)[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-119)

### Energy source

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=23 "Edit section: Energy source")]

Main articles: [Algae fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_fuel "Algae fuel"), [Biological hydrogen production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_hydrogen_production "Biological hydrogen production"), [Biohydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biohydrogen "Biohydrogen"), [Biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel "Biodiesel"), [Ethanol fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel "Ethanol fuel"), [Butanol fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butanol_fuel "Butanol fuel"), [Vegetable oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable_oil "Vegetable oil"), [Biogas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogas "Biogas"), and [Hydrothermal Liquefaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal_Liquefaction "Hydrothermal Liquefaction")

To be competitive and independent from fluctuating support from (local) policy on the long run, biofuels should equal or beat the cost level of fossil fuels. Here, algae-based fuels hold great promise,[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-120)[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-121) directly related to the potential to produce more biomass per unit area in a year than any other form of biomass. The break-even point for algae-based biofuels is estimated to occur by 2025.[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-122)

### Fertilizer

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=24 "Edit section: Fertilizer")]

Further information: [Seaweed fertiliser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed_fertiliser "Seaweed fertiliser")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Inisheer_landscape.jpg/220px-Inisheer_landscape.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Inisheer_landscape.jpg)

Seaweed-fertilized gardens on [Inisheer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inisheer "Inisheer")

For centuries, seaweed has been used as a fertilizer; [George Owen of Henllys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Owen_of_Henllys "George Owen of Henllys") writing in the 16th century referring to drift weed in [South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Wales "South Wales"):[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-123)

> This kind of ore they often gather and lay on great heapes, where it heteth and rotteth, and will have a strong and loathsome smell; when being so rotten they cast on the land, as they do their muck, and thereof springeth good corn, especially barley ... After spring-tydes or great rigs of the sea, they fetch it in sacks on horse backes, and carie the same three, four, or five miles, and cast it on the lande, which doth very much better the ground for corn and grass.

Today, algae are used by humans in many ways; for example, as [fertilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilizer "Fertilizer"), [soil conditioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_conditioner "Soil conditioner"), and livestock feed.[\[124\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-124) Aquatic and microscopic species are cultured in clear tanks or ponds and are either harvested or used to treat effluents pumped through the ponds. [Algaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algaculture "Algaculture") on a large scale is an important type of [aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture "Aquaculture") in some places. [Maerl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maerl "Maerl") is commonly used as a soil conditioner.

### As food

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=25 "Edit section: As food")]

See also: [Edible seaweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edible_seaweed "Edible seaweed") and [Algae powder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_powder "Algae powder")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b7/Dulse.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dulse.JPG)

Dulse, a type of edible seaweed

Algae are used as foods in many countries: China consumes more than 70 species, including *[fat choy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_choy_\(vegetable\) "Fat choy (vegetable)")*, a cyanobacterium considered a vegetable; Japan, over 20 species such as *[nori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nori "Nori")* and *[aonori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aonori "Aonori")*;[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-125) Ireland, [dulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulse "Dulse"); [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile "Chile"), [cochayuyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochayuyo "Cochayuyo").[\[126\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-126) [Laver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyra_umbilicalis "Porphyra umbilicalis") is used to make [laverbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laverbread "Laverbread") in [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales "Wales"), where it is known as *bara lawr*. In [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea "Korea"), [green laver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_laver "Green laver") is used to make *[gim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gim_\(Korean_food\) "Gim (Korean food)")*.[\[127\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-127)

Three forms of algae used as food:

- *[Chlorella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorella "Chlorella")*: This form of alga is found in freshwater and contains [photosynthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthetic "Photosynthetic") pigments in its [chloroplast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast").
- [Klamath AFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klamath_Lake_AFA "Klamath Lake AFA"): A subspecies of Aphanizomenon flos-aquae found wild in many bodies of water worldwide but harvested only from [Upper Klamath Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Klamath_Lake "Upper Klamath Lake"), Oregon.
- *[Spirulina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirulina_\(genus\) "Spirulina (genus)")*: Known otherwise as a cyanobacterium (a [prokaryote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryote "Prokaryote") or a "blue-green alga")

The oils from some algae have high levels of [unsaturated fatty acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsaturated_fatty_acid "Unsaturated fatty acid"). Some varieties of algae favored by [vegetarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarianism "Vegetarianism") and [veganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veganism "Veganism") contain the long-chain, essential [omega-3 fatty acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega-3_fatty_acid "Omega-3 fatty acid"), [docosahexaenoic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docosahexaenoic_acid "Docosahexaenoic acid") (DHA) and [eicosapentaenoic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eicosapentaenoic_acid "Eicosapentaenoic acid") (EPA).[\[128\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-:0-128) Fish oil contains the omega-3 fatty acids, but the original source is algae (microalgae in particular), which are eaten by marine life such as [copepods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copepod "Copepod") and are passed up the food chain.[\[128\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-:0-128)

### Pollution control

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=26 "Edit section: Pollution control")]

- Sewage can be treated with algae,[\[129\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-129) reducing the use of large amounts of toxic chemicals that would otherwise be needed.
- Algae can be used to capture fertilizers in runoff from farms. When subsequently harvested, the enriched algae can be used as fertilizer.
- Aquaria and ponds can be filtered using algae, which absorb nutrients from the water in a device called an [algae scrubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_scrubber "Algae scrubber"), also known as an algae turf scrubber.[\[130\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-130)[\[131\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-131)

[Agricultural Research Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_Research_Service "Agricultural Research Service") scientists found that 60–90% of nitrogen runoff and 70–100% of phosphorus runoff can be captured from [manure effluents](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Manure_effluents\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Manure effluents (page does not exist)") using a horizontal algae scrubber, also called an [algal turf scrubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algal_turf_scrubber "Algal turf scrubber") (ATS). Scientists developed the ATS, which consists of shallow, 100-foot raceways of nylon netting where algae colonies can form, and studied its efficacy for three years. They found that algae can readily be used to reduce the nutrient runoff from agricultural fields and increase the quality of water flowing into rivers, streams, and oceans. Researchers collected and dried the nutrient-rich algae from the ATS and studied its potential as an organic fertilizer. They found that cucumber and corn seedlings grew just as well using ATS organic fertilizer as they did with commercial fertilizers.[\[132\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-132) Algae scrubbers, using bubbling upflow or vertical waterfall versions, are now also being used to filter aquaria and ponds.

### Polymers

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=27 "Edit section: Polymers")]

Various polymers can be created from algae, which can be especially useful in the creation of bioplastics. These include hybrid plastics, cellulose-based plastics, poly-lactic acid, and bio-polyethylene.[\[133\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-133) Several companies have begun to produce algae polymers commercially, including for use in flip-flops[\[134\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-134) and in surf boards.[\[135\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-135)

### Bioremediation

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=28 "Edit section: Bioremediation")]

The alga *[Stichococcus bacillaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Stichococcus_bacillaris\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Stichococcus bacillaris (page does not exist)")* has been seen to colonize silicone resins used at archaeological sites; [biodegrading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodegradation "Biodegradation") the synthetic substance.[\[136\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-136)

### Pigments

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Algae\&action=edit\&section=29 "Edit section: Pigments")]

The natural [pigments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigment "Pigment") ([carotenoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carotenoid "Carotenoid") and [chlorophylls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyll "Chlorophyll")) produced by algae can be used as alternatives to chemical [dyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dye "Dye") and coloring agents.[\[137\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-137) The presence of some individual algal pigments, together with specific pigment concentration ratios, are taxon-specific: analysis of their concentrations with various analytical methods, particularly [high-performance liquid chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_liquid_chromatography "High-performance liquid chromatography"), can therefore offer deep insight into the taxonomic composition and relative abundance of natural algae populations in sea water samples.[\[138\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-138)[\[139\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-139)
