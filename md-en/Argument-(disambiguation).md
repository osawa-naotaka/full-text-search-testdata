# Argument (disambiguation)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Wiktionary-logo-en-v2.svg/40px-Wiktionary-logo-en-v2.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wiktionary-logo-en-v2.svg)

Look up ***[argument](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/argument "wiktionary:argument")*** in Wiktionary, the free dictionary.

In logic and philosophy, an **[argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument "Argument")** is an attempt to persuade someone of something, or give evidence or reasons for accepting a particular conclusion.

**Argument** may also refer to:

## Mathematics and computer science

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Argument_\(disambiguation\)\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: Mathematics and computer science")]

- [Argument (complex analysis)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_\(complex_analysis\) "Argument (complex analysis)"), a function which returns the polar angle of a complex number
- [Command-line argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command-line_argument "Command-line argument"), an item of information provided to a program when it is started
- [Parameter (computer programming)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parameter_\(computer_programming\) "Parameter (computer programming)"), a piece of data provided as input to a subroutine
- [Argument principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_principle "Argument principle"), a theorem in complex analysis
- An [argument of a function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_of_a_function "Argument of a function"), also known as an independent variable

## Language and rhetoric

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Argument_\(disambiguation\)\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Language and rhetoric")]

- [Argument (literature)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_\(literature\) "Argument (literature)"), a brief summary, often in prose, of a poem or section of a poem or other work
- [Argument (linguistics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_\(linguistics\) "Argument (linguistics)"), a phrase that appears in a syntactic relationship with the verb in a clause
- [Oral argument in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_argument_in_the_United_States "Oral argument in the United States"), a spoken presentation to a judge or appellate court by a lawyer (or parties when representing themselves) of the legal reasons why they should prevail
- [Closing argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closing_argument "Closing argument"), in law, the concluding statement of each party's counsel reiterating the important arguments in a court case
