"**America the Beautiful**" is a [patriotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotism "Patriotism") American song. Its lyrics were written by [Katharine Lee Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharine_Lee_Bates "Katharine Lee Bates") and its music was composed by church organist and choirmaster [Samuel A. Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_A._Ward "Samuel A. Ward") at [Grace Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Church_\(Newark\) "Grace Church (Newark)") in Newark, New Jersey,[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-1) though the two never met.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-2)

Bates wrote the words as a poem, originally titled "**Pikes Peak**". It was first published in the [Fourth of July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_of_July "Fourth of July") 1895 edition of the church periodical, *The Congregationalist*. At that time, the poem was titled "America".

Ward had initially composed the song's melody in 1882 to accompany lyrics to "Materna", basis of the hymn, "[O Mother dear, Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Mother_dear,_Jerusalem "O Mother dear, Jerusalem")", though the hymn was not first published until 1892.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-3) The combination of Ward's melody and Bates's poem was first entitled "America the Beautiful" in 1910. The song is one of the most popular of the many American patriotic songs.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-4)

## History

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=America_the_Beautiful\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Americathebeautiful.jpg/170px-Americathebeautiful.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Americathebeautiful.jpg)

Commemoration plaque atop [Pikes Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikes_Peak "Pikes Peak") in July 1999

In 1893, at the age of 33, Bates, an English professor at [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College "Wellesley College"), had taken a train trip to [Colorado Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Springs,_Colorado "Colorado Springs, Colorado"), Colorado, to teach at [Colorado College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_College "Colorado College").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-5) Several of the sights on her trip inspired her, and they found their way into her poem, including the [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World%27s_Columbian_Exposition "World's Columbian Exposition") in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), the "White City" with its promise of the future contained within its gleaming white buildings;[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-6) the wheat fields of North America's heartland [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas "Kansas"), through which her train was riding on July 16; and the majestic view of the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains "Great Plains") from high atop [Pikes Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikes_Peak "Pikes Peak").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FromSeatoShiningSea-8)

On the pinnacle of that mountain, the words of the poem started to come to her, and she wrote them down upon returning to her hotel room at the original [Antlers Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antlers_Hilton_Hotel "Antlers Hilton Hotel"). The poem was initially published two years later in *The Congregationalist* to commemorate [the Fourth of July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(United_States\) "Independence Day (United States)"). It quickly caught the public's fancy. An amended version was published in 1904.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10)

The first known melody written for the song was sent in by [Silas Pratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silas_Pratt "Silas Pratt") when the poem was published in *The Congregationalist*. By 1900, at least 75 different melodies had been written.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Collins2009-11) A [hymn tune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymn_tune "Hymn tune") composed in 1882 by [Samuel A. Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_A._Ward "Samuel A. Ward"), the organist and choir director at [Grace Church, Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Church,_Newark "Grace Church, Newark"), was generally considered the best music as early as 1910 and is still the popular tune today. Just as Bates had been inspired to write her poem, Ward, too, was inspired. The tune came to him while he was on a ferryboat trip from [Coney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island "Coney Island") back to his home in New York City after a leisurely summer day and he immediately wrote it down. He composed the tune for the old hymn "O Mother Dear, Jerusalem", retitling the work "Materna". Ward's music combined with Bates's poem were first published together in 1910 and titled "America the Beautiful".[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-12)

Ward died in 1903, not knowing the national stature his music would attain. The song's popularity was well established by the time of Bates's death in 1929.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Collins2009-11) It is included in songbooks in many religious congregations in the United States.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-13)

At various times in the more than one hundred years that have elapsed since the song was written, particularly during the [John F. Kennedy administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_John_F._Kennedy "Presidency of John F. Kennedy"), there have been efforts to give "America the Beautiful" legal status either as a national hymn or as a national anthem equal to, or in place of, "[The Star-Spangled Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star-Spangled_Banner "The Star-Spangled Banner")", but so far this has not succeeded. Proponents prefer "America the Beautiful" for various reasons, saying it is easier to sing, more melodic, and more adaptable to new orchestrations while still remaining as easily recognizable as "The Star-Spangled Banner". Some prefer "America the Beautiful" over "The Star-Spangled Banner" due to the latter's war-oriented imagery, while others object to the implicit support of slavery and racism in its third verse; others prefer "The Star-Spangled Banner" *because* of its war themes. While that national dichotomy has stymied any effort at changing the tradition of the national anthem, "America the Beautiful" continues to be held in high esteem by a large number of Americans, and was even being considered before 1931 as a candidate to become the national anthem of the United States.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-14)

## Notable performances

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=America_the_Beautiful\&action=edit\&section=3 "Edit section: Notable performances")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Portrait_of_Katharine_Lee_Bates%2C_ca._1880-1890.jpg/220px-Portrait_of_Katharine_Lee_Bates%2C_ca._1880-1890.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Portrait_of_Katharine_Lee_Bates,_ca._1880-1890.jpg)

Katharine Lee Bates, ca. 1880–1890

[Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley "Elvis Presley") performed it many times in concerts starting in 1976.

[Bing Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bing_Crosby "Bing Crosby") included the song in a medley on his album *[101 Gang Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101_Gang_Songs "101 Gang Songs")* (1961).

[Frank Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Sinatra "Frank Sinatra") recorded the song with [Nelson Riddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Riddle "Nelson Riddle") during the sessions for *[The Concert Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Concert_Sinatra "The Concert Sinatra")* in February 1963, for a projected 45 single release. The 45 was not commercially issued however, but the song was later added as a bonus track to the enhanced 2012 CD release of *[The Concert Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Concert_Sinatra "The Concert Sinatra")*.

In 1976, while the United States celebrated its bicentennial, a soulful version popularized by [Ray Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Charles "Ray Charles") peaked at number 98 on the US R\&B chart.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-18)[\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-19) His version was traditionally played on New Year's Eve in [Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square "Times Square") following the ball drop. Charles performed the song at Republican National Convention in August 1984 and [Super Bowl XXXV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXV "Super Bowl XXXV") in January 2001.

Three different renditions of the song have entered the [Hot Country Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs "Hot Country Songs") charts. The first was by [Charlie Rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Rich "Charlie Rich"), which went to number 22 in 1976.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-20) A second, by [Mickey Newbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Newbury "Mickey Newbury"), peaked at number 82 in 1980.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-21) An [all-star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-star "All-star") version of "America the Beautiful" performed by [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") singers [Trace Adkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_Adkins "Trace Adkins"), [Sherrié Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherri%C3%A9_Austin "Sherrié Austin"), [Billy Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Dean "Billy Dean"), [Vince Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Gill "Vince Gill"), [Carolyn Dawn Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolyn_Dawn_Johnson "Carolyn Dawn Johnson"), [Toby Keith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Keith "Toby Keith"), [Brenda Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Lee "Brenda Lee"), [Lonestar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonestar "Lonestar"), [Lyle Lovett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyle_Lovett "Lyle Lovett"), [Lila McCann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lila_McCann "Lila McCann"), [Lorrie Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorrie_Morgan "Lorrie Morgan"), [Jamie O'Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_O%27Neal "Jamie O'Neal"), [The Oak Ridge Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oak_Ridge_Boys "The Oak Ridge Boys"), [Collin Raye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collin_Raye "Collin Raye"), [Kenny Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Rogers "Kenny Rogers"), [Keith Urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Urban "Keith Urban") and [Phil Vassar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Vassar "Phil Vassar") reached number 58 in July 2001. The song re-entered the chart following the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks").[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-22)

[Barbra Streisand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbra_Streisand "Barbra Streisand") released an official music video footage during [Norman Lear's Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Love_Liberty "I Love Liberty") in 1982.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-23)

The song has been sung before many editions of the [WWE's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE "WWE") flagship annual show [WrestleMania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania "WrestleMania") beginning at [WrestleMania 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_2 "WrestleMania 2") in 1986, interchangeably with [The Star-Spangled Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star-Spangled_Banner "The Star-Spangled Banner"). The song has been performed by artists such as [Ray Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Charles "Ray Charles"), [Aretha Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aretha_Franklin "Aretha Franklin"), [Gladys Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladys_Knight "Gladys Knight"), [Willie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson "Willie Nelson"), [Little Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Richard "Little Richard"), [Boyz II Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyz_II_Men "Boyz II Men"), [Boys Choir of Harlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys_Choir_of_Harlem "Boys Choir of Harlem"), [John Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Legend "John Legend"), [Nicole Scherzinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Scherzinger "Nicole Scherzinger") and [Fifth Harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Harmony "Fifth Harmony").[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-24)

During her rise to stardom, R\&B singer [Mariah Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariah_Carey "Mariah Carey") sang the song at the [1990 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_NBA_Finals "1990 NBA Finals").

[Whitney Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Houston "Whitney Houston") also recorded the song, covering Ray Charles' soulful rearranged version as the [b-side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-side "B-side") to her 1991 rendition of "[The Star Spangled Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star_Spangled_Banner_\(Whitney_Houston_recording\) "The Star Spangled Banner (Whitney Houston recording)")."

The song has been performed as part of the [Indianapolis 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_500 "Indianapolis 500") pre-race ceremonies since 1991.

The US singer/songwriter Martin Sexton recorded a gospel-tinged version on his LP "Black Sheep," released in 1996.

Popularity of the song increased greatly in the decades following 9/11; at some sporting events it was sung in addition to the traditional singing of the national anthem. During the first taping of the *[Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman "Late Show with David Letterman")* following the attacks, CBS newsman [Dan Rather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Rather "Dan Rather") cried briefly as he quoted the fourth verse.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-25)

The hymn has been featured in the pregame for a number of [Super Bowls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl "Super Bowl"), the championship game for each [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL "NFL") season. It is sung along with the "Star-Spangled Banner" and, more recently, the hymn "[Lift Every Voice and Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_Every_Voice_and_Sing "Lift Every Voice and Sing")," commonly referred to as the "Black national anthem". For [Super Bowl XLVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLVIII "Super Bowl XLVIII"), [The Coca-Cola Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Coca-Cola_Company "The Coca-Cola Company") aired a multilingual version of the song, sung in several different languages. The commercial received some criticism on social media sites, such as Twitter and Facebook, and from some conservatives, such as [Glenn Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Beck "Glenn Beck").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-26)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-27)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-28) Despite the controversies, Coca-Cola later reused the Super Bowl ad during [Super Bowl LI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LI "Super Bowl LI"), the opening ceremonies of the [2014 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics "2014 Winter Olympics") and [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics "2016 Summer Olympics") and for patriotic holidays.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-29)[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-sbnation-cocacolaagain-30) [Notable performers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_anthem_performers_at_the_Super_Bowl#Other_patriotic_performances "List of national anthem performers at the Super Bowl") at the Super Bowl include [Ray Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Charles "Ray Charles"), [Alicia Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicia_Keys "Alicia Keys"), [John Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Legend "John Legend"), [Jhené Aiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhen%C3%A9_Aiko "Jhené Aiko"), [Faith Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Hill "Faith Hill"), [Mary J. Blige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_J._Blige "Mary J. Blige") with [Marc Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Anthony "Marc Anthony"), [Blake Shelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Shelton "Blake Shelton") with [Miranda Lambert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miranda_Lambert "Miranda Lambert"), [Queen Latifah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Latifah "Queen Latifah"), [Leslie Odom Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Odom_Jr. "Leslie Odom Jr."), and [Babyface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babyface_\(musician\) "Babyface (musician)"). [Post Malone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_Malone "Post Malone") performed the song for the most recent game, [Super Bowl LVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVIII "Super Bowl LVIII"), in 2024.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-31)

The song, performed by 5 Alarm Music, is featured heavily in a dystopian action horror franchise [The Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Purge "The Purge") in both trailers and films.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-32)

In 2016, American five-piece girl group [Fifth Harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Harmony "Fifth Harmony") performed a rendition to honor the [United States women's national soccer team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team "United States women's national soccer team") on defeating [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team "Japan women's national football team") 5–2 in the [Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_Final "2015 FIFA Women's World Cup Final")[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-33) to win the [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2015 FIFA Women's World Cup") [FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "FIFA Women's World Cup")[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-34) last July at [BC Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Place "BC Place") in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver "Vancouver"), [British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia "British Columbia"), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada")[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-35) before an undisputed [AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT%26T_Stadium "AT\&T Stadium") audience of 101,763 to open [WrestleMania 32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_32 "WrestleMania 32")[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-36) in [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas "Dallas"), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas").[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-37)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-38)

In 2017, [Jackie Evancho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Evancho "Jackie Evancho") released *Together We Stand*, a disc containing three patriotic songs including "America the Beautiful."[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-39) The song charted at No. 4 on *Billboard's* Classical Digital Song sales chart.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-40)

An abbreviated cover with the 1911 lyrics was performed by [Greg Jong](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Greg_Jong\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Greg Jong (page does not exist)") for the soundtrack of the 2020 video game *[Wasteland 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasteland_3 "Wasteland 3")* and is played during the final hostile encounters in the Denver section.

In 2021, [Jennifer Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Lopez "Jennifer Lopez") performed the song at the [inauguration of Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inauguration_of_Joe_Biden "Inauguration of Joe Biden"), as the second half of a medley with "[This Land Is Your Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Land_Is_Your_Land "This Land Is Your Land")" by [Woody Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie "Woody Guthrie").[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-41)

In 2023, [Cécile McLorin Salvant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9cile_McLorin_Salvant "Cécile McLorin Salvant") performed the song at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Open_\(tennis\) "US Open (tennis)") woman's final. In her rendition, Salvant notably skipped ahead to the lyrics of the second half of the second verse while singing the first verse (replacing "God shed His grace on thee..." with "God mend thine every flaw\...", etc.). Jazz Critic Nate Chinen wrote the following day of the performance, "What does it mean for a singer such as Salvant to inhabit a platform like the US Open, and implore God to mend America’s every flaw? What does it mean, in the Year of Our Lord 2023, for a singer like Salvant to urge the nation to confirm thy soul in self-control, and find liberty in law? I’m not going to spell it out, but it means a lot."

In 2025 [Carrie Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Underwood "Carrie Underwood") sang the song [*a capella*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_cappella "A cappella") after what were described as “technical difficulties” caused the failure of her backing music, during the [second inauguration of Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump "Second inauguration of Donald Trump").[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-42)

## Idioms

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=America_the_Beautiful\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Idioms")]

"From sea to shining sea" is an American [idiom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiom "Idiom") meaning "from the [Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean") to the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean")" (or vice versa). Other songs that have used this phrase include the American patriotic song "[God Bless the U.S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_Bless_the_U.S.A. "God Bless the U.S.A.")" and [Schoolhouse Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoolhouse_Rock "Schoolhouse Rock")'s "Elbow Room". The phrase and the song are also the namesake of the [Shining Sea Bikeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shining_Sea_Bikeway "Shining Sea Bikeway"), a [bike path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_path "Bike path") in Bates's hometown of [Falmouth, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falmouth,_Massachusetts "Falmouth, Massachusetts"). The phrase is similar to the Latin phrase "**[A Mari Usque Ad Mare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Mari_Usque_Ad_Mare "A Mari Usque Ad Mare")**" ("From sea to sea"), which is the official motto of [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada").[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-43)

"Purple mountain majesties" refers to the shade of [Pikes Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikes_Peak "Pikes Peak") in [Colorado Springs, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Springs,_Colorado "Colorado Springs, Colorado"), which inspired Bates to write the poem.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-44) The idiom inspired the [Colorado Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Rockies "Colorado Rockies") to have [purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple "Purple") as one of its team colors.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-45)

In 2003, [Tori Amos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tori_Amos "Tori Amos") appropriated the phrase "for amber waves of grain" to create a [personification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personification "Personification") for her song "Amber Waves". Amos imagines Amber Waves as an exotic dancer, like the character of the same name portrayed by [Julianne Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianne_Moore "Julianne Moore") in *[Boogie Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boogie_Nights "Boogie Nights")*.

[Lynn Sherr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Sherr "Lynn Sherr")'s 2001 book *America the Beautiful: The Stirring True Story Behind Our Nation's Favorite Song* discusses the origins of the song and the backgrounds of its authors in depth. The book points out that the poem has [the same meter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_meter "Common meter") as that of "[Auld Lang Syne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auld_Lang_Syne "Auld Lang Syne")"; the songs can be sung interchangeably. Additionally, Sherr discusses the evolution of the lyrics, for instance, changes to the original third verse written by Bates.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Sherr2001-16)

Melinda M. Ponder, in her 2017 biography *Katharine Lee Bates: From Sea to Shining Sea*,[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FromSeatoShiningSea-8) draws heavily on Bates's diaries and letters to trace the history of the poem and its place in American culture.
