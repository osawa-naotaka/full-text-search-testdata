# Adventure

Exciting or unusual experience

"Adventurer" and "Adventures" redirect here. For other uses, see [Adventure (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_\(disambiguation\) "Adventure (disambiguation)") and [Adventurer (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventurer_\(disambiguation\) "Adventurer (disambiguation)").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Indian_coracle.jpg/330px-Indian_coracle.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Indian_coracle.jpg)

People on a [coracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coracle "Coracle")

An **adventure** is an exciting experience or undertaking that is typically bold, sometimes [risky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk "Risk").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-1) Adventures may be activities with danger such as [traveling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling "Traveling"), [exploring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploring "Exploring"), [skydiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydiving "Skydiving"), [mountain climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_climbing "Mountain climbing"), [scuba diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuba_diving "Scuba diving"), [river rafting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_rafting "River rafting"), or other [extreme sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_sports "Extreme sports"). Adventures are often undertaken to create psychological [arousal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arousal "Arousal") or in order to achieve a greater goal, such as the pursuit of [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge "Knowledge") that can only be obtained by such activities.

## Motivation

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adventure\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: Motivation")]

Adventurous experiences create psychological [arousal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arousal "Arousal"),[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-2) which can be interpreted as negative (e.g. [fear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear "Fear")) or positive (e.g. [flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_\(psychology\) "Flow (psychology)")). For some people, adventure becomes a major pursuit in and of itself. According to adventurer [André Malraux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Malraux "André Malraux"), in his *[Man's Fate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man%27s_Fate "Man's Fate")* (1933), "If a man is not ready to risk his life, where is his dignity?"

Similarly, [Helen Keller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller "Helen Keller") stated that "Life is either a daring adventure or nothing."[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-3)

Outdoor adventurous activities are typically undertaken for the purposes of [recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreation "Recreation") or [excitement](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/excitement "wikt:excitement"): examples are [adventure racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_racing "Adventure racing") and [adventure tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_travel "Adventure travel"). Adventurous activities can also lead to gains in knowledge, such as those undertaken by explorers and pioneers – the British adventurer [Jason Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Lewis_\(adventurer\) "Jason Lewis (adventurer)"), for example, uses adventures to draw global [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability "Sustainability") lessons from living within finite environmental constraints on expeditions to share with schoolchildren. [Adventure education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_education "Adventure education") intentionally uses challenging experiences for [learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning "Learning").

Author [Jon Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Levy_\(behaviorist\) "Jon Levy (behaviorist)") suggests that an experience should meet several criteria to be considered an adventure:[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-4)

1. Be remarkable—that is, worth talking about
2. Involve adversity or perceived risk
3. Bring about personal growth.

## Mythology and fiction

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adventure\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Mythology and fiction")]

Some of the oldest and most widespread stories in the world are stories of adventure, such as [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer "Homer")'s *[Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Odyssey "The Odyssey")*.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7)

The [knight errant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_errant "Knight errant") was the form the "adventure seeker" character took in the [Late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages "Late Middle Ages").

[Adventure fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_novel "Adventure novel") exhibits these "protagonist on adventurous journey" characteristics, as do many popular feature [films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_film "Adventure film"), such as *[Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_\(film\) "Star Wars (film)")*[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-8) and *[Raiders of the Lost Ark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raiders_of_the_Lost_Ark "Raiders of the Lost Ark")*.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-9)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Alice%27s_Adventures_in_Wonderland_-_Carroll%2C_Robinson_-_S001_-_Cover.jpg/220px-Alice%27s_Adventures_in_Wonderland_-_Carroll%2C_Robinson_-_S001_-_Cover.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Alice%27s_Adventures_in_Wonderland_-_Carroll,_Robinson_-_S001_-_Cover.jpg)

[Lewis Carroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Carroll "Lewis Carroll")'s *[Alice's Adventures in Wonderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice%27s_Adventures_in_Wonderland "Alice's Adventures in Wonderland")* is a well-known example of a fantasized adventure story.

### Outdoors

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adventure\&action=edit\&section=3 "Edit section: Outdoors")]

Adventure books may have the theme of the hero or main character going to face the [wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilderness "Wilderness") or [Mother Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Nature "Mother Nature"). Examples include books such as *[Hatchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatchet_\(book\) "Hatchet (book)")* or *[My Side of the Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Side_of_the_Mountain "My Side of the Mountain")*. These books are less about "questing", such as in mythology or other adventure novels, but more about [surviving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_skills "Survival skills") on their own, living off the land, gaining new experiences, and becoming closer to the natural world.

### Questing

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adventure\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Questing")]

Many adventures are based on the idea of a quest: the hero goes off in pursuit of a reward, whether it be a skill, prize, treasure, or perhaps the safety of a person. On the way, the hero must overcome various obstacles to obtain their reward.

### Video games

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adventure\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Video games")]

See also: [Adventure game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_game "Adventure game")

In [video game culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_culture "Video game culture"), an adventure game is a [video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game") in which the player assumes the role of a protagonist in an interactive story driven by exploration and [puzzle solving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle "Puzzle").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10) The [genre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_genres "Video game genres")'s focus on story allows it to draw heavily from other [narrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrative "Narrative")-based media, [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature "Literature") and [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film "Film"), encompassing a wide variety of literary genres. Many adventure games ([text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_text-based_computer_games "List of text-based computer games") and [graphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_graphic_adventure_games "List of graphic adventure games")) are designed for a single player, since this emphasis on story and character makes multi-player design difficult.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-11)

## Nonfiction works

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adventure\&action=edit\&section=6 "Edit section: Nonfiction works")]

From ancient times, travelers and explorers have written about their adventures.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-12) Journals which became best-sellers in their day were written, such as [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo "Marco Polo")'s journal *[The Travels of Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Travels_of_Marco_Polo "The Travels of Marco Polo")* or [Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain "Mark Twain")'s *[Roughing It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roughing_It "Roughing It")*. Others were personal journals, only later published, such as the journals of [Meriwether Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meriwether_Lewis "Meriwether Lewis") and [William Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Clark "William Clark") or [Captain James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_James_Cook "Captain James Cook")'s journals. There are also books written by those not directly a part of the adventure in question, such as *[The Right Stuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Right_Stuff_\(book\) "The Right Stuff (book)")* by [Tom Wolfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Wolfe "Tom Wolfe") or books written by those participating in the adventure but in a format other than that of a journal, such as *Conquistadors of the Useless* by [Lionel Terray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Terray "Lionel Terray"). Documentaries often use the theme of adventure as well.

## Adventure sports

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Adventure\&action=edit\&section=7 "Edit section: Adventure sports")]

Main article: [Extreme sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_sport "Extreme sport")

There are many sports classified as adventure sports, due to their inherent danger and excitement. Some of these include [mountain climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_climbing "Mountain climbing"), [skydiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydiving "Skydiving"), or other [extreme sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_sports "Extreme sports").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-13)
