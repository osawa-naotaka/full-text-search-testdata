**Abel**[\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-1) is a biblical figure in the [Book of Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis "Book of Genesis") within the [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religion "Abrahamic religion"). Born as the second son of [Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam "Adam") and [Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eve "Eve"), the first two humans created by [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Judaism "God in Judaism"),[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-2) he was a [shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shepherd "Shepherd") who offered his firstborn flock to God as a religious offering. God accepted Abel's offering but not the offering of his older brother [Cain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cain "Cain"), leading Cain to stone Abel to death out of jealousy. This act marked the first death in biblical history, making Abel the first murder victim.

The story of [Cain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cain "Cain")'s murder of Abel and its consequences is told in Genesis 4:1–18:[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-3)

> Now the man knew his wife Eve, and she conceived and bore Cain, saying, "I have produced a man with the help of the Lord."[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-4) Next she bore his brother Abel.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-5) Now Abel was a keeper of sheep, and Cain a tiller of the ground. In the course of time Cain brought to the Lord an offering of the fruit of the ground, and Abel for his part brought of the firstlings of his flock, their fat portions. And the Lord had regard for Abel and his offering, but for Cain and his offering he had no regard. So Cain was very angry, and his countenance fell. The Lord said to Cain,
>
> > "Why are you angry,\
> > and why has your countenance fallen?\
> > If you do well,\
> > will you not be accepted?\
> > And if you do not do well,\
> > sin is lurking at the door;\
> > its desire is for you,\
> > but you must master it."
>
> Cain said to his brother Abel, "Let us go out to the field."[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-6) And when they were in the field, Cain rose up against his brother Abel, and killed him.
>
> Then the Lord said to Cain, "Where is your brother Abel?" He said, "I do not know; am I my brother's keeper?"[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7) And the Lord said, "What have you done? Listen; your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground! And now you are cursed from the ground, which has opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-8) When you till the ground, it will no longer yield to you its strength; you will be a fugitive and a wanderer on the earth." Cain said to the Lord, "My punishment is greater than I can bear! Today you have driven me away from the soil, and I shall be hidden from your face; I shall be a fugitive and a wanderer on the earth, and anyone who meets me may kill me." Then the Lord said to him, "Not so! Whoever kills Cain will suffer a sevenfold vengeance." And the Lord put a mark on Cain, so that no one who came upon him would kill him.
>
> Then Cain went away from the presence of the Lord, and settled in the land of Nod, east of Eden. Cain knew his wife, and she conceived and bore Enoch; and he built a city, and named it Enoch after his son Enoch.
>

## Interpretations

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Abel\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Interpretations")]

### Jewish and Christian interpretations

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Abel\&action=edit\&section=3 "Edit section: Jewish and Christian interpretations")]

According to the narrative in [Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis "Book of Genesis"), **Abel** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): הֶבֶל *Hébel*, in [pausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pausa "Pausa") הָבֶל‎ *Hā́ḇel*; [Biblical Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_Greek_language "Biblical Greek language"): Ἅβελ *Hábel*; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"): [هابيل](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D9%87%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%84 "wikt:هابيل"), *Hābēl*) is Eve's second son. His name in Hebrew is composed of the same three consonants as a [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_root "Semitic root") meaning "the air that remains after you exhale" also synonymous in Hebrew to "nothing", as stated in [Ecclesiastes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastes "Ecclesiastes"). [Julius Wellhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Wellhausen "Julius Wellhausen") has proposed that the name is independent of the root.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10) [Eberhard Schrader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eberhard_Schrader "Eberhard Schrader") had previously put forward the [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_\(language\) "Akkadian (language)") (Old Assyrian dialect) *ablu* ("son") as a more likely etymology.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-11)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg/220px-William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg)

*[The First Mourning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_First_Mourning "The First Mourning")* (Adam and Eve mourn the death of Abel); oil on canvas 1888 painting by [William-Adolphe Bouguereau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William-Adolphe_Bouguereau "William-Adolphe Bouguereau")

In [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), comparisons are sometimes made between the death of Abel and that of [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus"), the former thus seen as being the first martyr. In Matthew 23:35 Jesus speaks of Abel as "righteous", and the [Epistle to the Hebrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Hebrews "Epistle to the Hebrews") states that "The blood of sprinkling ... \[speaks] better things than that of Abel" (Hebrews 12:24). The blood of Jesus is interpreted as bringing mercy; but that of Abel as demanding vengeance (hence the curse and mark).[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-12)

Abel is invoked in the [litany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litany "Litany") for the dying in the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church "Roman Catholic Church"), and his sacrifice is mentioned in the [Canon of the Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_of_the_Mass "Canon of the Mass") along with those of [Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham "Abraham") and [Melchizedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melchizedek "Melchizedek"). The [Alexandrian Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandrian_Rite "Alexandrian Rite") commemorates him with a [feast day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_day "Feast day") on December 28.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-13)

According to the Coptic [Book of Adam and Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Adam_and_Eve "Book of Adam and Eve") (at 2:1–15), and the [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language "Syriac language") [Cave of Treasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_of_Treasures "Cave of Treasures"), Abel's body, after many days of mourning, was placed in the *Cave of Treasures*, before which Adam and Eve, and descendants, offered their prayers. In addition, the [Sethite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth "Seth") line of the [Generations of Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generations_of_Adam "Generations of Adam") swear by Abel's blood to segregate themselves from the *unrighteous*.

In the [Book of Enoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Enoch "Book of Enoch") (22:7), regarded by most Christian and Jewish traditions as extra-biblical, the soul of Abel is described as having been appointed as the chief of martyrs, crying for vengeance, for the destruction of the seed of Cain. A similar view is later shown in the [Testament of Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testament_of_Abraham "Testament of Abraham") (A:13 / B:11), where Abel has been raised to the position as the judge of the souls.

In [Bereshit Rabbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bereshit_Rabbah "Bereshit Rabbah") (22:2), a discussion of Gen. 4:1 ff. has Rabbi [Yehoshua ben Korcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_ben_Karha "Joshua ben Karha") mentioning that Cain was born with a twin sister, and Abel with two twin sisters. This is based on the principle that the otherwise superfluous accusative article "et" always conveys some additional teaching ([Pesachim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesachim "Pesachim") 22b). The "et"'s are parsed slightly differently in [Yebamot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yebamot "Yebamot") 62a where the two "et"'s in Gen. 4:2 indicate Cain and his sister, and Abel and his (one) sister.

### Sethian Gnostic interpretation

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Abel\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Sethian Gnostic interpretation")]

In the [Apocryphon of John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocryphon_of_John "Apocryphon of John"), a work belonging to [Sethian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sethianism "Sethianism") [Gnosticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnosticism "Gnosticism"), Abel is the offspring of [Yaldaboath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demiurge#Yaldaboath "Demiurge") and [Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eve "Eve"), who is placed over the elements of water and earth as [Elohim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elohim "Elohim"), but was only given his name as a form of deception.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-14)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-15)

### Islamic interpretation

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Abel\&action=edit\&section=6 "Edit section: Islamic interpretation")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/NabiHabeel02.JPG/220px-NabiHabeel02.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:NabiHabeel02.JPG)

Grave of Abel within the [Nabi Habeel Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Habeel_Mosque "Nabi Habeel Mosque")

Main articles: [Cain and Abel in Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cain_and_Abel_in_Islam "Cain and Abel in Islam") and [Al-Ma'ida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ma%27ida "Al-Ma'ida")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/NabiHabeel01.jpg/220px-NabiHabeel01.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:NabiHabeel01.jpg)

The [Mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum "Mausoleum") of Abel in the [Nabi Habeel Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Habeel_Mosque "Nabi Habeel Mosque")

According to [Shi'a Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam "Shia Islam") belief, Abel (*"Habeel"*) is buried in the [Nabi Habeel Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Habeel_Mosque "Nabi Habeel Mosque"), located on the west mountains of [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus "Damascus"), near the [Zabadani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabadani "Zabadani") Valley, overlooking the villages of the [Barada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barada "Barada") river (Wadi Barada), in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria"). Shi'a are frequent visitors of this mosque for [ziyarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziyarat "Ziyarat"). The mosque was built by [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") [Wali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wali_\(administrative_title\) "Wali (administrative title)") Ahmad Pasha in 1599.
