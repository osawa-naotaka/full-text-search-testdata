The **American Football Conference** (**AFC**) is one of the two [conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_conference "Athletic conference") of the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League") (NFL), the highest level of professional [American football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football "American football") in the United States. The AFC and its counterpart, the [National Football Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_Conference "National Football Conference") (NFC), each have 16 teams organized into four [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(sport\) "Division (sport)"). Both conferences were created as part of the [1970 merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL%E2%80%93NFL_merger "AFL–NFL merger") between the National Football League, and the [American Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_League "American Football League") (AFL). All ten of the AFL teams, and three NFL teams, became members of the new AFC, with the remaining thirteen NFL teams forming the NFC. A series of league expansions and division realignments have occurred since the merger, thus making the current total of 16 teams in each conference. The current AFC champions are the [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs "Kansas City Chiefs"), who defeated the [Buffalo Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bills "Buffalo Bills") in the [2024 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NFL_season "2024 NFL season")'s [AFC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Championship_Game "AFC Championship Game") for their fifth conference championship and went on to [Super Bowl LIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIX "Super Bowl LIX") against the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles "Philadelphia Eagles").

Currently, the fourteen opponents each team faces over the 17-game regular season schedule are set using a predetermined formula:

Each AFC team plays the other teams in their respective division twice (home and away) during the regular season, in addition to eleven other games assigned to their schedule by the NFL: three games are assigned on the basis of a particular team's final divisional standing from the previous season, and the remaining eight games are split between the roster of two other NFL divisions. This assignment shifts each year and will follow a standard cycle. Using the 2023 regular season schedule as an example, each team in the AFC West plays against every team in the AFC East and NFC North. In this way, non-divisional competition will be mostly among common opponents – the exception being the three games assigned based on the team's prior-season divisional standing.

At the end of each season, the four division winners and three [wild cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_card_\(sports\) "Wild card (sports)") (non-division winners with best regular season record) in the AFC qualify for the [playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_playoffs "NFL playoffs"). The AFC playoffs culminate in the [AFC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Championship_Game "AFC Championship Game"), with the winner receiving the [Lamar Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamar_Hunt "Lamar Hunt") Trophy. The AFC champion then plays the [NFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_Conference "National Football Conference") champion in the [Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl "Super Bowl").

## History

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=American_Football_Conference\&action=edit\&section=3 "Edit section: History")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Oldafclogo.JPG/150px-Oldafclogo.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Oldafclogo.JPG)

Original American Football Conference logo, based on the AFL logo with blue stars

With the impending [merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL%E2%80%93NFL_merger "AFL–NFL merger") with the [American Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_League "American Football League") (AFL) for the [1970 NFL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_NFL_season "1970 NFL season"), the league had careful discussion over the nature of which teams would play in the newly instituted conferences. Then-NFL President and owner of the [Cleveland Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns "Cleveland Browns") [Art Modell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Modell "Art Modell") had suggested of a format in which three teams from the NFL would move to the AFC to create two thirteen-team conferences. But negotiations between NFL commissioner [Pete Rozelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Rozelle "Pete Rozelle") and other owners was rampant in who would move (for his part, Modell had first assumed the three most recent expansion NFL teams - [Minnesota Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Vikings "Minnesota Vikings"), [Atlanta Falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Falcons "Atlanta Falcons"), [New Orleans Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans_Saints "New Orleans Saints") would be the ones to move to the AFC), since others wanted to simply have no realignment of NFL teams.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-s806-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-21) The AFL had begun play in 1960 with eight teams before adding two more expansion clubs (the [Miami Dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Dolphins "Miami Dolphins") in 1966 and the [Cincinnati Bengals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Bengals "Cincinnati Bengals") in 1968) before the merger.

Modell was hospitalized for internal bleeding around the time negotiations were still going. Moreover, Modell was struggling to service the debt he incurred from his purchase of the Browns. Furthermore, he realized there was an opportunity to establish a [lucrative in-state rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengals-Browns_rivalry "Bengals-Browns rivalry") with the newly-established Bengals, who had been founded by [Paul Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Brown "Paul Brown") after Modell had forced him out of Cleveland after purchasing the team.

When Modell was visited in the hospital by [Art Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Rooney "Art Rooney") (owner of the [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers "Pittsburgh Steelers")) and [Wellington Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Mara "Wellington Mara") (owner of the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants "New York Giants")), Modell offered to have his franchise move to the AFC, provided two other "old guard" franchises did so as well and the three affected teams to move were adequately compensated for joining what was still looked down on in NFL circles as a "junior" or "inferior" circuit. Not wanting to lose his [long-established rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browns-Steelers_rivalry "Browns-Steelers rivalry") with Cleveland, the equally cash-strapped Rooney quickly agreed to join the Browns in the AFC. The other NFL owner to ultimately agree to move was the [Baltimore Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Colts "Baltimore Colts")' [Carroll Rosenbloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carroll_Rosenbloom "Carroll Rosenbloom").

Thus, in order to equalize the number of teams in each conference, three NFL teams that predated the AFL's launch (the [Cleveland Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns "Cleveland Browns"), [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers "Pittsburgh Steelers"), and the then-[Baltimore Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Colts "Baltimore Colts")) joined the ten former AFL teams to form the AFC in exchange for $3 million each in indemnities, with the announcement coming on May 10, 1969.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-22) The two AFL divisions [AFL East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_East "AFL East") and [AFL West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_West "AFL West") were more or less intact, while the NFL's [Century Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League_Century_Division "National Football League Century Division"), in which the Browns and the Steelers had played since 1967, was moved from the NFL to become the new AFC Central. Upon the completion of the merger of the AFL and NFL in 1970, the newly minted American Football Conference had already agreed upon their divisional setup along mostly geographical lines for the [1970 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_NFL_season "1970 NFL season"); the National Football Conference, however, could not agree upon their setup, and one was chosen from a fishbowl on January 16, 1970.

Since the merger, five expansion teams have joined the AFC and two have left, thus making the current total 16. When the [Seattle Seahawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Seahawks "Seattle Seahawks") and the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers "Tampa Bay Buccaneers") joined the league in 1976, they were temporarily placed in the NFC and AFC respectively. This arrangement lasted for one season only before the two teams switched conferences. The Seahawks eventually returned to the NFC as a result of the [2002 realignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_NFL_season#Expansion_and_realignment "2002 NFL season"). The expansion [Jacksonville Jaguars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_Jaguars "Jacksonville Jaguars") joined the AFC in 1995. There have been five teams that have relocated at least once. In 1984, the [Baltimore Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Baltimore_Colts "History of the Baltimore Colts") [relocated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Colts_relocation_to_Indianapolis "Baltimore Colts relocation to Indianapolis") to Indianapolis. In 1995, the [Cleveland Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns "Cleveland Browns") had attempted to move to Baltimore; the resulting [dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns_relocation_controversy "Cleveland Browns relocation controversy") between Cleveland and the team led to Modell establishing the [Baltimore Ravens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Ravens "Baltimore Ravens") with the players and personnel from the Browns, while the Browns were placed in suspended operations before they were reinstated by the NFL. The Ravens were treated as an expansion team.

In [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"), the [Oakland Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Raiders "Oakland Raiders") relocated to [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") in 1982, back to Oakland in 1995, and then to [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas "Las Vegas") in 2020, while the [San Diego Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_San_Diego_Chargers "History of the San Diego Chargers") returned to [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") in 2017 after 56 years in [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego "San Diego").

The [Houston Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Houston_Oilers "History of the Houston Oilers") moved to [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee "Tennessee") in 1997, where they were renamed the Tennessee Oilers. The team would change its name again, two years later, to the [Tennessee Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Titans "Tennessee Titans").

The NFL would again expand in 2002, adding the [Houston Texans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Texans "Houston Texans") to the AFC. With the exception of the aforementioned relocations since that time, the divisional setup has remained static ever since.

Between 1995 and 2022, the AFC has sent only 9 of its 16 teams to the [Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl "Super Bowl"): [New England Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Patriots "New England Patriots") (10 times), [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers "Pittsburgh Steelers") (4 times), [Denver Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Broncos "Denver Broncos") (4 times), [Baltimore Ravens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Ravens "Baltimore Ravens") (2 times), [Indianapolis Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Colts "Indianapolis Colts") (2 times), [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs "Kansas City Chiefs") (3 times), [Cincinnati Bengals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Bengals "Cincinnati Bengals") (1 time), [Las Vegas Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Raiders "Las Vegas Raiders") (1 time), [Tennessee Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Titans "Tennessee Titans") (1 time). By contrast, the [NFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_Conference "National Football Conference") has sent 13 of the 16 NFC teams during that same time frame with only the [Detroit Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Lions "Detroit Lions"), [Minnesota Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Vikings "Minnesota Vikings"), and [Washington Commanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Commanders "Washington Commanders") missing out on an appearance in the [Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl "Super Bowl"). 17 of the 19 AFC champions from 2001 to 2019 have started one of just three quarterbacks - [Tom Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Brady "Tom Brady"), [Peyton Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Manning "Peyton Manning") and [Ben Roethlisberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Roethlisberger "Ben Roethlisberger") - in the Super Bowl. The AFC has started 7 quarterbacks in the last 20 Super Bowls, while the NFC has started 16.

## Logo

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=American_Football_Conference\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Logo")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/American_Football_Conference_logo_old.svg/216px-American_Football_Conference_logo_old.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:American_Football_Conference_logo_old.svg)

2nd American Football Conference logo used from 1970 to 2009

The original AFC logo was very similar to the AFL logo, however the merged league quickly created an updated logo for the AFC that, while preserving the basic elements of the old AFL logo, used a much bolder red "A" and six similarly bold red stars surrounding it, in contrast to the six blue AFL stars. The league also created a logo for the NFC in 1970, which like the AFL and AFC logos also contained only the first letter as opposed to a full abbreviation, but with only three stars (to represent the then-three divisions of the Conference). The AFC logo basically remained unchanged from 1970 to 2009. The 2010 NFL season introduced an updated AFC logo, with the most notable revision being the removal of two stars (leaving four representing the four divisions of the AFC), and moving the stars inside the letter, similar to the NFC logo, which itself was updated at the same time to add a fourth star.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-23)

Notably, the [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs "Kansas City Chiefs") (still owned by the descendants of AFL founder [Lamar Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamar_Hunt "Lamar Hunt")) continue to regularly feature the original AFL logo on their jerseys as of 2025.

## Television

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=American_Football_Conference\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Television")]

[NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_NBC "NFL on NBC") aired the AFC's Sunday afternoon and playoff games from 1970 through the 1997 season. From 1998 to 2013, [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_CBS "NFL on CBS") was the primary broadcast rightsholder to the AFC; in those years, all interconference games in which the AFC team was the visiting team were broadcast on either NBC or CBS. Since 2014, the cross-flex policy allows select AFC games (that involve them playing an NFC team at home or intraconference games) to be moved from CBS to Fox. Since 1990, select AFC playoff games have been seen on ABC or ESPN.
