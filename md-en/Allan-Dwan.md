**Allan Dwan** (born **Joseph Aloysius Dwan**; April 3, 1885 – December 28, 1981) was a pioneering Canadian-born American motion picture director, producer, and screenwriter.

## Early life

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Allan_Dwan\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")]

Born Joseph Aloysius Dwan in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto "Toronto"), Ontario, Canada, Dwan was the younger son of commercial traveler of woolen clothing Joseph Michael Dwan (1857–1917) and his wife Mary Jane Dwan (née Hunt). The family moved to the United States when he was seven years old on December 4, 1892, by ferry from Windsor to Detroit, according to his [naturalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalization "Naturalization") petition of August 1939. His elder brother, Leo Garnet Dwan (1883–1964), became a physician.

Allan Dwan studied engineering at the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame "University of Notre Dame") and then worked for a lighting company in Chicago. He had a strong interest in the fledgling motion picture industry, and when [Essanay Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essanay_Studios "Essanay Studios") offered him the opportunity to become a scriptwriter, he took the job.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Kevin_Brownlow_1968-1) At that time, some of the [East Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States "East Coast of the United States") movie makers began to spend winters in California where the climate allowed them to continue productions requiring warm weather. Soon, a number of movie companies worked there year-round, and in 1911, Dwan began working part-time in [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles "Hollywood, Los Angeles"). While still in New York, in 1917 he was the founding president of the East Coast chapter of the [Motion Picture Directors Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Directors_Association "Motion Picture Directors Association").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-frankenstein-2)

## Career

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Allan_Dwan\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Career")]

Dwan started his directing career by accident in 1911, when he was sent by his employers to California, in order to locate a company that had vanished. Dwan managed to track the company down, and learned that they were waiting for a film director (who was an alcoholic) to return from a binge and allow them to resume their work. Dwan wired back to his employers in Chicago, informing them of the situation, and suggested that they disband the company. They wired back, instructing Dwan to direct the stalled film. When Dwan informed the company of the situation, and that their jobs were on the line, they responded: "You're the best damn director we ever saw".[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-HollywoodDoc-3)

Dwan operated [Flying A Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_A_Studios "Flying A Studios") in [La Mesa, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Mesa,_California "La Mesa, California"), from August 1911 to July 1912.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-5) Flying A was one of the first motion pictures studios in California history. On August 12, 2011, a plaque was unveiled on the Wolff building at Third Avenue and La Mesa Boulevard commemorating Dwan and the [Flying A Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_A_Studios "Flying A Studios") origins in [La Mesa, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Mesa,_California "La Mesa, California").

After making a series of westerns and comedies, Dwan directed fellow Canadian-American [Mary Pickford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Pickford "Mary Pickford") in several very successful movies as well as her husband, [Douglas Fairbanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Fairbanks "Douglas Fairbanks"), notably in the acclaimed 1922 *[Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood_\(1922_film\) "Robin Hood (1922 film)")*. Around that time, he also directed [Carole Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carole_Lombard "Carole Lombard") in [*A Perfect Crime*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Perfect_Crime_\(film\) "A Perfect Crime (film)"), her film debut. Dwan directed [Gloria Swanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Swanson "Gloria Swanson") in eight feature films, and one short film made in the short-lived sound-on-film process [Phonofilm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonofilm "Phonofilm"). This short, also featuring [Thomas Meighan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Meighan "Thomas Meighan") and [Henri de la Falaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_la_Falaise "Henri de la Falaise"), was produced as a joke, for the April 26, 1925 "Lambs' Gambol" for [The Lambs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lambs "The Lambs"), with the film showing Swanson crashing the all-male club.

Following the introduction of the [talkies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_film "Sound film"), Dwan directed child-star [Shirley Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Temple "Shirley Temple") in *[Heidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidi_\(1937_film\) "Heidi (1937 film)")* (1937) and *[Rebecca of Sunnybrook Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_of_Sunnybrook_Farm_\(1938_film\) "Rebecca of Sunnybrook Farm (1938 film)")* (1938).

Dwan helped launch the career of two other successful Hollywood directors, [Victor Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Fleming "Victor Fleming"), who went on to direct *[The Wizard of Oz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wizard_of_Oz_\(1939_film\) "The Wizard of Oz (1939 film)")* and *[Gone With the Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_\(film\) "Gone with the Wind (film)")*, and [Marshall Neilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Neilan "Marshall Neilan"), who became an actor, director, writer and producer. Over a long career spanning almost 50 years, Dwan directed 125 motion pictures, some of which were highly acclaimed, such as the 1949 box office hit, *[Sands of Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sands_of_Iwo_Jima "Sands of Iwo Jima")*. He directed his last movie in 1961.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-6)

Being one of the last surviving pioneers of the cinema, he was interviewed at length for the 1980 documentary series *[Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_\(British_TV_series\) "Hollywood (British TV series)")*.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-HollywoodDoc-3)

He died in Los Angeles at the age of 96, and is interred in the [San Fernando Mission Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Mission_Cemetery "San Fernando Mission Cemetery"), [Mission Hills, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Hills,_Los_Angeles "Mission Hills, Los Angeles").

Dwan has a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame") at 6263 [Hollywood Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Boulevard "Hollywood Boulevard").

Daniel Eagan of *[Film Journal International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Journal_International "Film Journal International")* described Dwan as one of the early pioneers of cinema, stating that his style "is so basic as to seem invisible, but he treats his characters with uncommon sympathy and compassion."[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7)
