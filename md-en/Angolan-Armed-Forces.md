The **Angolan Armed Forces** ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language"): *Forças Armadas Angolanas*) or **FAA** is the military of [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola "Angola"). The FAA consist of the [Angolan Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Army "Angolan Army") (*Exército Angolano*), the [Angolan Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Navy "Angolan Navy") (*Marinha de Guerra Angolana*) and the [National Air Force of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Air_Force_of_Angola "National Air Force of Angola") (*Força Aérea Nacional de Angola*). Reported total manpower in 2021 was about 107,000.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-IISSp448-2) The FAA is headed by the [Chief of the General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_General_Staff_\(Angola\) "Chief of General Staff (Angola)") António Egídio de Sousa Santos since 2018, who reports to the [minister of National Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_National_Defence_\(Angola\) "Minister of National Defence (Angola)"), currently [João Ernesto dos Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Ernesto_dos_Santos "João Ernesto dos Santos").

## History

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

### Roots

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Roots")]

The FAA succeeded to the previous [People's Armed Forces for the Liberation of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Armed_Forces_for_the_Liberation_of_Angola "People's Armed Forces for the Liberation of Angola") (FAPLA) following the abortive [Bicesse Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicesse_Accord "Bicesse Accord") with the [Armed Forces of the Liberation of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Liberation_of_Angola "Armed Forces of the Liberation of Angola") (FALA), armed wing of the [National Union for the Total Independence of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_for_the_Total_Independence_of_Angola "National Union for the Total Independence of Angola") (UNITA). As part of the peace agreement, troops from both armies were to be [demilitarized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demilitarized "Demilitarized") and then integrated. Integration was never completed as UNITA and FALA went back to war in 1992. Later, consequences for FALA personnel in Luanda were harsh with FAPLA veterans persecuting their erstwhile opponents in certain areas and reports of [vigilantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigilantism "Vigilantism").

### Founding

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=3 "Edit section: Founding")]

The Angolan Armed Forces were created on 9 October 1991. The institutionalization of the FAA was made in the [Bicesse Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicesse_Accords "Bicesse Accords"), signed in 1991, between the Angolan Government and UNITA. The principles that would govern the FAA were defined in a joint proposal presented on September 24, 1991, and approved on 9 October. On 14 November 1991, Generals João Baptista de Matos and Abílio Kamalata Numa were appointed to the Superior Command of the Armed Forces. The ceremony took place at the [Hotel Presidente Luanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Presidente_Luanda "Hotel Presidente Luanda"), and was presided over by the then-minister França Vandúnem.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-6)

## Branches

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Branches")]

### Army

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Army")]

Main article: [Angolan Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Army "Angolan Army")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/Angolan_soldiers_training_in_Russia.png/300px-Angolan_soldiers_training_in_Russia.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Angolan_soldiers_training_in_Russia.png)

Angolan Second Lieutenant, First Lieutenant, and Captain training in Russia in August 2015

The Army (*Exército*) is the land component of the FAA. It is organized in six military regions (Cabinda, Luanda, North, Center, East and South), with an infantry division being based in each one. Distributed by the six military regions / infantry divisions, there are 25 motorized infantry brigades, one tank brigade and one engineering brigade. The Army also includes an artillery regiment, the Military Artillery School, the Army Military Academy, an anti-aircraft defense group, a composite land artillery group, a military police regiment, a logistical transportation regiment and a field artillery brigade. The Army further includes the Special Forces Brigade (including Commandos and Special Operations units), but this unit is under the direct command of the General Staff of the FAA.

### Air Force

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=6 "Edit section: Air Force")]

Main article: [National Air Force of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Air_Force_of_Angola "National Air Force of Angola")

The [National Air Force of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Air_Force_of_Angola "National Air Force of Angola") (FANA, *Força Aérea Nacional de Angola*) is the air component of the FAA. It is organized in six aviation regiments, each including several squadrons. To each of the regiments correspond an air base. Besides the aviation regiments, there is also a Pilot Training School.

The Air Force's personnel total about 8,000; its equipment includes transport aircraft and six Russian-manufactured [Sukhoi Su-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-27 "Sukhoi Su-27") [fighter aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter_aircraft "Fighter aircraft").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7) In 2002, one was lost during the civil war with UNITA forces.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-8)

In 1991, the Air Force/Air Defense Forces had 8,000 personnel and 90 combat-capable aircraft, including 22 fighters, 59 fighter ground attack aircraft and 16 attack helicopters.

### Navy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=7 "Edit section: Navy")]

Main article: [Angolan Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Navy "Angolan Navy")

The Angola Navy (MGA, *Marinha de Guerra de Angola*) is the naval component of the FAA. It is organized in two naval zones (North and South), with naval bases in Luanda, Lobito and Moçâmedes. It includes a Marines Brigade and a Marines School, based in Ambriz. The Navy numbers about 1,000 personnel and operates only a handful of small patrol craft and barges.

The Navy has been neglected and ignored as a military arm mainly due to the guerrilla struggle against the Portuguese and the nature of the civil war. From the early 1990s to the present the Angolan Navy has shrunk from around 4,200 personnel to around 1,000, resulting in the loss of skills and expertise needed to maintain equipment. Portugal has been providing training through its Technical Military Cooperation (CTM) programme. The Navy is requesting procurement of a frigate, three corvettes, three offshore patrol vessel and additional fast patrol boats.

Most of the vessels in the navy's inventory dates back from the 1980s or earlier, and many of its ships are inoperable due to age and lack of maintenance. However the navy acquired new boats from Spain and France in the 1990s. Germany has delivered several [Fast Attack Craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Attack_Craft "Fast Attack Craft") for border protection in 2011.

In September 2014 it was reported that the Angolan Navy would acquire seven [Macaé-class patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maca%C3%A9-class_patrol_vessel "Macaé-class patrol vessel") from Brazil as part of a Technical Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) covering the production of the vessels as part of Angola's Naval Power Development Programme (Pronaval).[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-DWMacae-9) The military of Angola aims to modernize its naval capability, presumably due to a rise in maritime piracy within the Gulf of Guinea which may have an adverse effect on the country's economy.

The FAA include several types of special forces, namely the Commandos, the Special Operations and the Marines. The Angolan special forces follow the general model of the analogous Portuguese special forces, receiving a similar training.

The Commandos and the Special forces are part of the Special Forces Brigade (BRIFE, *Brigada de Forças Especiais*), based at Cabo Ledo, in the [Bengo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengo_Province "Bengo Province"). The BRIFE includes two battalions of commandos, a battalion of special operations and sub-units of combat support and service support. The BRIFE also included the Special Actions Group (GAE, *Grupo de Ações Especiais*), which is presently inactive and that was dedicated to long range reconnaissance, covert and sabotage operations. In the Cabo Ledo base is also installed the Special Forces Training School (EFFE, *Escola de Formação de Forças Especiais*). Both the BRIFE and the EFFE are directly under the Directorate of Special Forces of the General Staff of the Armed Forces.

The marines (*fuzileiros navais*) constitute the Marines Brigade of the Angolan Navy. The Marines Brigade is not permanently dependent of the Directorate of Special Forces, but can detach their units and elements to be put under the command of that body for the conduction of exercises or real operations.

Since the disbandment of the Angolan Parachute Battalion in 2004, the FAA do not have a specialized paratrooper unit. However, elements of the commandos, special operations and marines are parachute qualified.

### Territorial troops

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=10 "Edit section: Territorial troops")]

The Directorate of People's Defense and Territorial Troops of the Defence Ministry or ODP was established in late 1975. It had 600,000 members, having personnel in virtually every village by 1979. It had both armed and unarmed units dispersed in villages throughout the country.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-13) The People's Vigilance Brigades ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language"): *Brigadas Populares de Vigilância, BPV*) also serve a similar purpose.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-14)

### Armed Forces Academy

- Tanks

- Military Engineering
- Logistics
- Telecommunications
- Hidden Direction of Troops
- Military Administration
- Armament and Technique
- Chemical Defense
- Operational Military Intelligence
- Technical Repair and Maintenance Platoon of Auto and Armored Technique

### Military Hospitals

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=17 "Edit section: Military Hospitals")]

The [Military hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_hospital "Military hospital") of the FAA is the Main Military Hospital. It has the following lineage:

- 1961 – Evacuation Infirmary
- 1962 – Military Hospital of Luanda
- 1975 – Military Hospital
- 1976 – Central Military Hospital
- 1989 – Main Military Hospital

It provides specialized medical assistance in accordance with the military health system; It also promotes post-graduate education and scientific research. Currently, the Main Military Hospital serves 39 special medical specialties. It is a headed by a Director General whose main supporting body is the board of directors.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-17)

### Supreme Military Court

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=18 "Edit section: Supreme Military Court")]

The Supreme Military Court is the highest organ of the hierarchy of [military courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_court "Military court"). The Presiding Judge, the Deputy Presiding Judge and the other Counselor Judges of the Supreme Military Court are appointed by the President of the Republic. The composition, organization, powers and functioning of the Supreme Military Court are established by law.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-18)

### Military Bands

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=19 "Edit section: Military Bands")]

The FAA maintains Portuguese-style military bands in all three branches and in individual units. The primary band is the 100-member Music Band of the Presidential Security Household.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-19) The music band of the Army Command was created on 16 June 1994[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-20) and four years later, on 15 August 1998, the National Air Force created a music band within an artistic brigade.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-21) The navy has its own marching band, as well as a small musical group known as *Banda 10 de Julho* (10 July Band), based at the [Luanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luanda "Luanda") Naval Base.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-22)

## Foreign deployments

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angolan_Armed_Forces\&action=edit\&section=20 "Edit section: Foreign deployments")]

The FAPLA's main counterinsurgency effort was directed against UNITA in the southeast, and its conventional capabilities were demonstrated principally in the undeclared [South African Border War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Border_War "South African Border War").[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ReferenceA-23) The FAPLA first performed its external assistance mission with the dispatch of 1,000 to 1,500 troops to [São Tomé and Príncipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Tom%C3%A9_and_Pr%C3%ADncipe "São Tomé and Príncipe") in 1977 to bolster the socialist regime of President Manuel Pinto da Costa. During the next several years, Angolan forces conducted joint exercises with their counterparts and exchanged technical operational visits. The Angolan expeditionary force was reduced to about 500 in early 1985.

The Angolan Armed Forces were controversially involved in training the armed forces of fellow [Lusophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusophone "Lusophone") states [Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde "Cape Verde") and Guinea-Bissau. In the case of the latter, the [2012 Guinea-Bissau coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Guinea-Bissau_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "2012 Guinea-Bissau coup d'état") was cited by the coup leaders as due to Angola's involvement in trying to "reform" the military in connivance with the civilian leadership.

Occasionally skirmishes on the [DRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo")-Angola border happening, sometimes also in connection with the [Cabinda conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinda_War "Cabinda War"). In 2020 one Angolan soldier died after a gun battle with congolese forces in [Kasai region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasa%C3%AF_region "Kasaï region") on DRC territory.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-24) A presence during the unrest in [Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory_Coast "Ivory Coast"), 2010–2011, were not officially confirmed. However, the *[Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Allgemeine_Zeitung "Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung")*, citing *[Jeune Afrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeune_Afrique "Jeune Afrique")*, said that among President [Gbagbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gbagbo "Gbagbo")'s guards were 92 personnel of President Dos Santos's [Presidential Guard Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Guard_Unit_\(Angola\) "Presidential Guard Unit (Angola)").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-25) Angola is basically interested in the participation of the FAA operations of the [African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union "African Union") and has formed special units for this purpose.

In 2021, the Angolan Parliament approved integration of FAA into [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community "Southern African Development Community") (SADC)'s mission for peace in [Cabo Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Cabo_Delgado "Insurgency in Cabo Delgado"), Mozambique. Angola sent a team of 20 officers to participate.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-26)
