# Astronomer

Scientist in the field of astronomy

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/IAU_2006_General_Assembly-_Result_of_the_IAU_Resolution_Votes_%28iau0603g%29.jpg/330px-IAU_2006_General_Assembly-_Result_of_the_IAU_Resolution_Votes_%28iau0603g%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:IAU_2006_General_Assembly-_Result_of_the_IAU_Resolution_Votes_\(iau0603g\).jpg)

A voting session is conducted in 2006 [International Astronomical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union "International Astronomical Union")'s general assembly for determining a [new definition of a planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAU_definition_of_planet "IAU definition of planet")

An **astronomer** is a [scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist "Scientist") in the field of [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy") who focuses on a specific question or field outside the scope of [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth"). Astronomers observe [astronomical objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_object "Astronomical object"), such as [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star "Star"), [planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet "Planet"), [moons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_satellite "Natural satellite"), [comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comet "Comet") and [galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy "Galaxy") – in either [observational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observational_astronomy "Observational astronomy") (by analyzing the data) or [theoretical astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_astronomy "Theoretical astronomy"). Examples of topics or fields astronomers study include [planetary science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_science "Planetary science"), [solar astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun "Sun"), the [origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_formation "Star formation") or [evolution of stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_evolution "Stellar evolution"), or the [formation of galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_formation_and_evolution "Galaxy formation and evolution"). A related but distinct subject is [physical cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology "Physical cosmology"), which studies the [Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe "Universe") as a whole.

## Types

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Astronomer\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: Types")]

Astronomers typically fall under either of two main types: [observational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observational_astronomy "Observational astronomy") and [theoretical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_astronomy "Theoretical astronomy"). Observational astronomers make direct [observations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation "Observation") of [celestial objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_object "Astronomical object") and analyze the data. In contrast, theoretical astronomers create and investigate [models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_model "Conceptual model") of things that cannot be observed. Because it takes millions to billions of years for a system of stars or a galaxy to complete a life cycle, astronomers must observe snapshots of different systems at unique points in their evolution to determine how they form, evolve, and die. They use this data to create models or [simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation "Simulation") to theorize how different celestial objects work.

Further subcategories under these two main [branches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branches_of_science "Branches of science") of astronomy include [planetary astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_science "Planetary science"), [astrobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrobiology "Astrobiology"), [stellar astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_astronomy "Stellar astronomy"), [astrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrometry "Astrometry"), [galactic astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_astronomy "Galactic astronomy"), [extragalactic astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extragalactic_astronomy "Extragalactic astronomy"), or [physical cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology "Physical cosmology"). Astronomers can also specialize in certain specialties of observational astronomy, such as [infrared astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_astronomy "Infrared astronomy"), [neutrino astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino_astronomy "Neutrino astronomy"), [x-ray astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_astronomy "X-ray astronomy"), and [gravitational-wave astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational-wave_astronomy "Gravitational-wave astronomy").

### History

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Astronomer\&action=edit\&section=3 "Edit section: History")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Justus_Sustermans_-_Portrait_of_Galileo_Galilei_%28Uffizi%29.jpg/170px-Justus_Sustermans_-_Portrait_of_Galileo_Galilei_%28Uffizi%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Justus_Sustermans_-_Portrait_of_Galileo_Galilei_\(Uffizi\).jpg)

[Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo "Galileo") is often referred to as the father of [modern astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_astronomy "Modern astronomy"). Portrait by [Justus Sustermans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justus_Sustermans "Justus Sustermans").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/JKepler.jpg/170px-JKepler.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:JKepler.jpg)

[Johannes Kepler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kepler "Johannes Kepler"), one of the fathers of [modern astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_astronomy "Modern astronomy")

[Historically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_astronomy "History of astronomy"), astronomy was more concerned with the [classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification "Classification") and description of [phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomena "Phenomena") in the sky, while [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics") attempted to explain these phenomena and the differences between them using [physical laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_law "Scientific law"). Today, that distinction has mostly disappeared and the terms "astronomer" and "astrophysicist" are interchangeable. Professional astronomers are highly educated individuals who typically have a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") or astronomy and are employed by research institutions or universities.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-1) They spend the majority of their time working on research, although they quite often have other duties such as teaching, building instruments, or aiding in the operation of an observatory.

The [American Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Astronomical_Society "American Astronomical Society"), which is the major organization of professional astronomers in [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America"), has approximately 8,200 members (as of 2024). This number includes scientists from other fields such as physics, [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology "Geology"), and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering "Engineering"), whose research interests are closely related to astronomy.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-2) The [International Astronomical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union "International Astronomical Union") comprises about 12,700 members from 92 countries who are involved in astronomical research at the PhD level and beyond (as of 2024).[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-3)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/%E5%8D%97%E5%85%AC%E6%87%B7%E4%BB%81%E5%83%8F.jpg/170px-%E5%8D%97%E5%85%AC%E6%87%B7%E4%BB%81%E5%83%8F.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:%E5%8D%97%E5%85%AC%E6%87%B7%E4%BB%81%E5%83%8F.jpg)

Portrait of the Flemish astronomer [Ferdinand Verbiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Verbiest "Ferdinand Verbiest") who became head of the Mathematical Board and director of the Observatory of the Chinese emperor in 1669

Contrary to the classical image of an old astronomer peering through a [telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope "Telescope") through the dark hours of the night, it is far more common to use a [charge-coupled device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge-coupled_device "Charge-coupled device") (CCD) camera to record a long, deep exposure, allowing a more sensitive image to be created because the [light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light "Light") is added over time. Before CCDs, [photographic plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photographic_plates "Photographic plates") were a common method of observation. Modern astronomers spend relatively little time at telescopes, usually just a few weeks per year. Analysis of observed phenomena, along with making predictions as to the causes of what they observe, takes the majority of observational astronomers' time.

### Activities and graduate degree training

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Astronomer\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Activities and graduate degree training")]

Astronomers who serve as faculty spend much of their time teaching undergraduate and graduate classes. Most universities also have [outreach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outreach "Outreach") programs, including public telescope time and sometimes [planetariums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetarium "Planetarium"), as a public service to encourage interest in the field.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-harvard-outreach-4)

Those who become astronomers usually have a broad background in physics, [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), sciences, and [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing "Computing") in high school. Taking courses that teach how to research, write, and present papers are part of the higher education of an astronomer, while most astronomers attain both a [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master%27s_degree "Master's degree") and eventually a PhD degree in astronomy, physics or [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-harvard-5)

PhD training typically involves 5-6 years of study, including completion of upper-level courses in the core sciences, a competency examination, experience with teaching undergraduates and participating in outreach programs, work on research projects under the student's supervising professor, completion of a [PhD thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis "Thesis"), and passing a final [oral exam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_exam "Oral exam").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-harvard-5) Throughout the PhD training, a successful student is financially supported with a [stipend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stipend "Stipend").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-harvard-5)

## Amateur astronomers

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Astronomer\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Amateur astronomers")]

Main article: [Amateur astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_astronomy "Amateur astronomy")

While there is a relatively low number of professional astronomers, the field is popular among [amateurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateurs "Amateurs"). Most cities have amateur astronomy clubs that meet on a regular basis and often host [star parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_party "Star party"). The [Astronomical Society of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_Society_of_the_Pacific "Astronomical Society of the Pacific") is the largest general astronomical society in the world, comprising both professional and amateur astronomers as well as educators from 70 different nations.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-6)

As with any [hobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobby "Hobby"), most people who practice amateur astronomy may devote a few hours a month to [stargazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargazing "Stargazing") and reading the latest developments in research. However, amateurs span the range from so-called "armchair astronomers" to the highly ambitious people who own science-grade telescopes and instruments with which they are able to make their own discoveries, create [astrophotographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophotography "Astrophotography"), and assist professional astronomers in research.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-explore-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-tejassvi-8)
