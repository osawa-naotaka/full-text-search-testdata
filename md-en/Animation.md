# Animation

Method of creating moving pictures

"Animate" redirects here. For other uses, see [Animate (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animate_\(disambiguation\) "Animate (disambiguation)") and [Animation (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animation_\(disambiguation\) "Animation (disambiguation)").

**Animation** is a filmmaking technique whereby [still images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image "Image") are manipulated to create [moving images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_picture "Motion picture"). In [traditional animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_animation "Traditional animation"), images are drawn or painted by hand on transparent celluloid sheets ([cels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cel "Cel")) to be photographed and exhibited on film. Animation has been recognized as an artistic medium, specifically within the [entertainment industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment#Industry "Entertainment"). Many animations are either traditional animations or [computer animations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_animation "Computer animation") made with [computer-generated imagery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-generated_imagery "Computer-generated imagery") (CGI). [Stop motion animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_motion_animation "Stop motion animation"), in particular [claymation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claymation "Claymation"), has continued to exist alongside these other forms.

Animation is contrasted with [live-action film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-action_film "Live-action film"), although the two do not exist in isolation. Many moviemakers have produced [films that are a hybrid of the two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Films_with_live_action_and_animation "Films with live action and animation"). As CGI increasingly [approximates photographic imagery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photorealism "Photorealism"), filmmakers can easily [composite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compositing "Compositing") 3D animations into their film rather than using [practical effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_effect "Practical effect") for showy [visual effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_effects "Visual effects") (VFX).

## General overview

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: General overview")]

[Computer animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_animation "Computer animation") can be very detailed [3D animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_animation#Animation_methods "Computer animation"), while 2D computer animation (which may have the look of traditional animation) can be used for stylistic reasons, low bandwidth, or faster [real-time renderings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_rendering "Real-time rendering"). Other common animation methods apply a [stop motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_motion "Stop motion") technique to two- and three-dimensional objects like [paper cutouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutout_animation "Cutout animation"), [puppets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet "Puppet"), or [clay figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claymation "Claymation").

An **animated cartoon** or simply **cartoon** is an animated film, usually short, featuring an exaggerated visual style. The style takes inspiration from [comic strips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_strip "Comic strip"), [gag cartoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gag_cartoon "Gag cartoon"), and other non-animated or still [cartoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon "Cartoon"). It often features [anthropomorphic animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropomorphic_animal "Anthropomorphic animal"), [superheroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superhero "Superhero"), or the adventures of human protagonists. Especially with animals that form a natural predator/prey relationship (e.g. cats and mice, coyotes and birds), the action often centers on [violent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_violence "Cartoon violence") [pratfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_comedy "Physical comedy") such as falls, collisions, and explosions that would be lethal in real life. In the 1980s, "cartoon" was shortened to ***toon***, referring to characters in animated productions. This term was popularized in 1988 by the combined live-action/animated film *[Who Framed Roger Rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Framed_Roger_Rabbit "Who Framed Roger Rabbit")*, followed in 1990 by the animated TV series *[Tiny Toon Adventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Toon_Adventures "Tiny Toon Adventures")*.

The illusion of animation—as in motion pictures in general—has traditionally been attributed to the [persistence of vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persistence_of_vision "Persistence of vision") and later to the [phi phenomenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_phenomenon "Phi phenomenon") and [beta movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_movement "Beta movement"), but the exact neurological causes are still uncertain. The illusion of motion caused by a rapid succession of images that minimally differ from each other, with unnoticeable interruptions, is a [stroboscopic effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroboscopic_effect "Stroboscopic effect"). While [animators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animator "Animator") traditionally used to draw each part of the movements and changes of figures on transparent cels that could be moved over a separate background, computer animation is usually based on programming paths between [key frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_frame "Key frame") to maneuver digitally created figures throughout a digitally created environment.

[Analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_device "Analog device") mechanical animation media that rely on the rapid display of sequential images include the [phenakistiscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenakistiscope "Phenakistiscope"), [zoetrope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoetrope "Zoetrope"), [flip book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flip_book "Flip book"), [praxinoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praxinoscope "Praxinoscope"), and film. [Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television "Television") and [video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video "Video") are popular electronic animation media that originally were analog and now operate [digitally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_media "Digital media"). For display on computers, technology such as the [animated GIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animated_GIF "Animated GIF") and [Flash animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_animation "Flash animation") were developed.

In addition to [short films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_film "Short film"), [feature films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_film "Feature film"), [television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_show "Television show"), animated GIFs, and other media dedicated to the display of moving images, animation is also prevalent in [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game"), [motion graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_graphics "Motion graphics"), [user interfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_interface "User interface"), and [visual effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_effects "Visual effects").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuchan2013-1)

The physical movement of image parts through simple mechanics—for instance, moving images in [magic lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_lantern "Magic lantern") shows—can also be considered animation. The mechanical manipulation of three-dimensional puppets and objects to emulate living beings has a very long history in [automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automaton "Automaton"). Electronic automata were popularized by [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney "Disney") as [animatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animatronics "Animatronics").

## Etymology

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Etymology")]

The word *animation* comes to the Latin word *animātiō*, meaning 'bestowing of life'.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-2) The earlier meaning of the English word is 'liveliness' and has been in use much longer than the meaning of 'moving image medium'.

### Before cinematography

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Before cinematography")]

Main article: [Early history of animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_history_of_animation "Early history of animation")

Long before modern animation began, audiences around the world were captivated by the magic of moving characters. For centuries, master artists and craftsmen have brought puppets, [automatons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automaton "Automaton"), [shadow puppets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_puppets "Shadow puppets"), and fantastical [lanterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantern "Lantern") to life, inspiring the imagination through physically manipulated wonders.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-3)

In 1833, the [stroboscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroboscope "Stroboscope") disc (better known as the [phenakistiscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenakistiscope "Phenakistiscope")) introduced the principle of modern animation, which would also be applied in the [zoetrope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoetrope "Zoetrope") (introduced in 1866), the [flip book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flip_book "Flip book") (1868), the [praxinoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praxinoscope "Praxinoscope") (1877) and [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film "Film").

### Silent era

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Silent era")]

When [cinematography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematography "Cinematography") eventually broke through in the 1890s, the wonder of the realistic details in the new medium was seen as its biggest accomplishment. It took years before animation found its way to the cinemas. The successful short *The Haunted Hotel* (1907) by [J. Stuart Blackton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Stuart_Blackton "J. Stuart Blackton") popularized [stop motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_motion "Stop motion") and reportedly inspired [Émile Cohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89mile_Cohl "Émile Cohl") to create *[Fantasmagorie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasmagorie_\(film\) "Fantasmagorie (film)")* (1908), regarded as the oldest known example of a complete [traditional (hand-drawn) animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_animation "Traditional animation") on standard cinematographic film. Other great artistic and very influential short films were created by [Ladislas Starevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladislas_Starevich "Ladislas Starevich") with his puppet animations since 1910 and by [Winsor McCay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winsor_McCay "Winsor McCay") with detailed hand-drawn animation in films such as *[Little Nemo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Nemo_\(1911_film\) "Little Nemo (1911 film)")* (1911) and *[Gertie the Dinosaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertie_the_Dinosaur "Gertie the Dinosaur")* (1914).[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-4)

During the 1910s, the production of animated "[cartoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoons "Cartoons")" became an industry in the US.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon198928-5) Successful producer [John Randolph Bray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Randolph_Bray "John Randolph Bray") and animator [Earl Hurd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Hurd "Earl Hurd"), patented the [cel animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cel_animation "Cel animation") process that dominated the animation industry for the rest of the century.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon198924-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon198934-7) [Felix the Cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_the_Cat "Felix the Cat"), who debuted in 1919, became the first fully realized anthropomorphic animal character in the history of American animation.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-8)

### American golden age

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=6 "Edit section: American golden age")]

Main article: [Golden Age of American animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Age_of_American_animation "Golden Age of American animation")

In 1928, *[Steamboat Willie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat_Willie "Steamboat Willie")*, featuring [Mickey Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mouse "Mickey Mouse") and [Minnie Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnie_Mouse "Minnie Mouse"), popularized film-with-synchronized-sound and put [Walt Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney "Walt Disney")'s studio at the forefront of the animation industry. Although [Disney Animation's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Animation_Studios "Walt Disney Animation Studios") actual output relative to total global animation output, has always been very small; the studio has overwhelmingly dominated the "aesthetic norms" of animation ever since.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Furniss_Page_107-9)

The enormous success of Mickey Mouse is seen as the start of the [golden age of American animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_age_of_American_animation "Golden age of American animation") that would last until the 1960s. The United States dominated the world market of animation with a plethora of cel-animated theatrical shorts.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10) Several studios would introduce characters that would become very popular and would have long-lasting careers, including [Walt Disney Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Productions "Walt Disney Productions")' [Goofy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goofy "Goofy") (1932) and [Donald Duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Duck "Donald Duck") (1934), [Fleischer Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleischer_Studios "Fleischer Studios")/[Paramount Cartoon Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Cartoon_Studios "Paramount Cartoon Studios")' [Out of the Inkwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_the_Inkwell "Out of the Inkwell")' [Koko the Clown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koko_the_Clown "Koko the Clown") (1918), [Bimbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimbo_\(Fleischer_Studios\) "Bimbo (Fleischer Studios)") and [Betty Boop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Boop "Betty Boop") (1930), [Popeye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popeye#Theatrical_animated_cartoons "Popeye") (1933) and [Casper the Friendly Ghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_the_Friendly_Ghost "Casper the Friendly Ghost") (1945), [Warner Bros. Cartoon Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Cartoons "Warner Bros. Cartoons")' [Looney Tunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Looney_Tunes "Looney Tunes")' [Porky Pig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porky_Pig "Porky Pig") (1935), [Daffy Duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daffy_Duck "Daffy Duck") (1937), [Elmer Fudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Fudd "Elmer Fudd") (1937–1940), [Bugs Bunny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugs_Bunny "Bugs Bunny") (1938–1940), [Tweety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweety "Tweety") (1942), [Sylvester the Cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_the_Cat "Sylvester the Cat") (1945), [Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wile_E._Coyote_and_the_Road_Runner "Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner") (1949), [MGM cartoon studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer_cartoon_studio "Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer cartoon studio")'s [Tom and Jerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_and_Jerry "Tom and Jerry") (1940) and [Droopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droopy "Droopy"), [Universal Cartoon Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Lantz_Productions "Walter Lantz Productions")' [Woody Woodpecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Woodpecker "Woody Woodpecker") (1940), [Terrytoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrytoons "Terrytoons")/[20th Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Fox "20th Century Fox")'s [Mighty Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mighty_Mouse "Mighty Mouse") (1942), and [United Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Artists "United Artists")' [Pink Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Panther_\(character\) "Pink Panther (character)") (1963).

### Features before CGI

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=7 "Edit section: Features before CGI")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Quirino_Cristiani_con_una_figura.jpg/180px-Quirino_Cristiani_con_una_figura.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Quirino_Cristiani_con_una_figura.jpg)

Italian-Argentine cartoonist [Quirino Cristiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirino_Cristiani "Quirino Cristiani") showing the cut and articulated figure of his satirical character *El Peludo* (based on President [Yrigoyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip%C3%B3lito_Yrigoyen "Hipólito Yrigoyen")) patented in 1916 for the realization of his films, including the world's first animated feature film *[El Apóstol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ap%C3%B3stol "El Apóstol")*[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBendazzi199449-11)

In 1917, Italian-Argentine director [Quirino Cristiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirino_Cristiani "Quirino Cristiani") made the first feature-length film *[El Apóstol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ap%C3%B3stol "El Apóstol")* (now [lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_film "Lost film")), which became a critical and commercial success. It was followed by Cristiani's *[Sin dejar rastros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sin_dejar_rastros "Sin dejar rastros")* in 1918, but one day after its premiere, the film was confiscated by the government.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-12)

After working on it for three years, [Lotte Reiniger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_Reiniger "Lotte Reiniger") released the German feature-length [silhouette animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silhouette_animation "Silhouette animation") *[Die Abenteuer des Prinzen Achmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Abenteuer_des_Prinzen_Achmed "Die Abenteuer des Prinzen Achmed")* in 1926, the oldest extant animated feature.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-13)

In 1937, [Walt Disney Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Animation_Studios "Walt Disney Animation Studios") premiered their first animated feature *[Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_White_and_the_Seven_Dwarfs_\(1937_film\) "Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937 film)")*, still one of the highest-grossing traditional animation features as of May 2020[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit).[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-snowwhite1-14)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-snowwhite2-15) The Fleischer studios followed this example in 1939 with *[Gulliver's Travels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulliver%27s_Travels_\(1939_film\) "Gulliver's Travels (1939 film)")* with some success. Partly due to foreign markets being cut off by the Second World War, Disney's next features *[Pinocchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinocchio_\(1940_film\) "Pinocchio (1940 film)")*, *[Fantasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasia_\(1940_film\) "Fantasia (1940 film)")* (both 1940), Fleischer Studios' second animated feature *[Mr. Bug Goes to Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Bug_Goes_to_Town "Mr. Bug Goes to Town")* (1941–1942) and Disney's feature films *[Cinderella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinderella_\(1950_film\) "Cinderella (1950 film)")* (1950), *[Alice in Wonderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_in_Wonderland_\(1951_film\) "Alice in Wonderland (1951 film)")* (1951) and *[Lady and the Tramp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_and_the_Tramp "Lady and the Tramp")* (1955) failed at the box office. For several decades, Disney was the only American studio to regularly produce animated features, until [Ralph Bakshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Bakshi "Ralph Bakshi") became the first to release more than a handful of features. [Sullivan-Bluth Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sullivan-Bluth_Studios "Sullivan-Bluth Studios") began to regularly produce animated features starting with *[An American Tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_American_Tail "An American Tail")* in 1986.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-16)

Although relatively few titles became as successful as Disney's features, other countries developed their own animation industries that produced both short and feature theatrical animations in a wide variety of styles, relatively often including [stop motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_motion "Stop motion") and [cutout animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutout_animation "Cutout animation") techniques. Soviet [Soyuzmultfilm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuzmultfilm "Soyuzmultfilm") animation studio, founded in 1936, produced 20 films (including shorts) per year on average and reached 1,582 titles in 2018. China, Czechoslovakia / Czech Republic, Italy, France, and Belgium were other countries that more than occasionally released feature films, while Japan became a true powerhouse of animation production, with its own recognizable and influential [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime "Anime") style of effective [limited animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_animation "Limited animation").\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Television

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=8 "Edit section: Television")]

Animation became very popular on television since the 1950s, when television sets started to become common in most developed countries. Cartoons were mainly programmed for children, on convenient time slots, and especially US youth spent many hours watching [Saturday-morning cartoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday-morning_cartoon "Saturday-morning cartoon"). Many classic cartoons found a new life on the small screen and by the end of the 1950s, the production of new animated cartoons started to shift from theatrical releases to TV series. [Hanna-Barbera Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanna-Barbera_Productions "Hanna-Barbera Productions") was especially prolific and had huge hit series, such as *[The Flintstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flintstones "The Flintstones")* (1960–1966) (the first [prime time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_time "Prime time") animated series), *[Scooby-Doo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scooby-Doo "Scooby-Doo")* (since 1969) and Belgian co-production *[The Smurfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smurfs_\(1981_TV_series\) "The Smurfs (1981 TV series)")* (1981–1989). The constraints of American television programming and the demand for an enormous quantity resulted in cheaper and quicker [limited animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_animation "Limited animation") methods and much more formulaic scripts. Quality dwindled until more daring animation surfaced in the late 1980s and in the early 1990s with hit series, the first cartoon of [The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons_shorts "The Simpsons shorts") (1987), which later developed into [its own show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons") (in 1989) and *[SpongeBob SquarePants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpongeBob_SquarePants "SpongeBob SquarePants")* (since 1999) as part of a "renaissance" of American animation.\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

While US animated series also spawned successes internationally, many other countries produced their own child-oriented programming, relatively often preferring [stop motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_motion "Stop motion") and [puppetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppetry "Puppetry") over cel animation. Japanese [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime "Anime") TV series became very successful internationally since the 1960s, and European producers looking for affordable cel animators relatively often started co-productions with Japanese studios, resulting in hit series such as *[Barbapapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbapapa "Barbapapa")* (The Netherlands/Japan/France 1973–1977), *[Wickie und die starken Männer/小さなバイキング ビッケ (Vicky the Viking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicky_the_Viking "Vicky the Viking")* (Austria/Germany/Japan 1974), *[Maya the Honey Bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_the_Honey_Bee "Maya the Honey Bee")* (Japan/Germany 1975) and *[The Jungle Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jungle_Book_\(1989_TV_series\) "The Jungle Book (1989 TV series)")* (Italy/Japan 1989).\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Switch from cels to computers

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=9 "Edit section: Switch from cels to computers")]

Main article: [History of computer animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_computer_animation "History of computer animation")

[Computer animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_animation "Computer animation") was gradually developed since the 1940s. 3D wireframe animation started popping up in the mainstream in the 1970s, with an early (short) appearance in the sci-fi thriller *[Futureworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futureworld "Futureworld")* (1976).[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-17)

*[The Rescuers Down Under](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rescuers_Down_Under "The Rescuers Down Under")* was the first feature film to be completely created digitally without a camera.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-first_digital_guinness-18) It was produced using the [Computer Animation Production System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Animation_Production_System "Computer Animation Production System") (CAPS), developed by [Pixar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixar "Pixar") in collaboration with [The Walt Disney Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") in the late 1980s, in a style similar to traditional cel animation.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Ness_2015_p197-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Guinness_World_Records_2024_a420-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Allison_2022_u732-21)

The so-called 3D style, more often associated with computer animation, became the dominant technique following the success of Pixar's *[Toy Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toy_Story "Toy Story")* (1995), the first computer-animated feature in this style.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-22)

Most of the cel animation studios switched to producing mostly computer-animated films around the 1990s, as it proved cheaper and more profitable. Not only the very popular 3D animation style was generated with computers, but also most of the films and series with a more traditional hand-crafted appearance, in which the charming characteristics of cel animation could be emulated with software, while new digital tools helped developing new styles and effects.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-23)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-24)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-25)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-27)[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-28)

## Economic status

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=10 "Edit section: Economic status")]

In 2010, the animation market was estimated to be worth circa US$80 billion.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoard_of_Investments2009-29) By 2021, the value had increased to an estimated US$370 billion.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-30) Animated feature-length films returned the highest [gross margins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_margin "Gross margin") (around 52%) of all [film genres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_genre "Film genre") between 2004 and 2013.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDuling2014-31) Animation as an art and industry continues to thrive as of the early 2020s.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-32)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-33)[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-34)

## Education, propaganda and commercials

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=11 "Edit section: Education, propaganda and commercials")]

The clarity of animation makes it a powerful tool for instruction, while its total malleability also allows exaggeration that can be employed to convey strong emotions and to thwart reality. It has therefore been widely used for other purposes than mere entertainment.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-35)

During World War II, animation was widely exploited for propaganda. [Many American studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_and_American_animation "World War II and American animation"), including Warner Bros. and Disney, lent their talents and their cartoon characters to convey to the public certain war values. Some countries, including China, Japan and the United Kingdom, produced their first feature-length animation for their war efforts.\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Animation has been very popular in television commercials, both due to its graphic appeal, and the humour it can provide. Some animated characters in commercials have survived for decades, such as [Snap, Crackle and Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snap,_Crackle_and_Pop "Snap, Crackle and Pop") in advertisements for Kellogg's cereals.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-36) [Tex Avery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tex_Avery "Tex Avery") was the producer of the first [Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raid_\(insecticide\) "Raid (insecticide)") "[Kills Bugs Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kills_Bugs_Dead "Kills Bugs Dead")" commercials in 1966, which were very successful for the company.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-37)

## Other media, merchandise and theme parks

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=12 "Edit section: Other media, merchandise and theme parks")]

Apart from their success in movie theaters and television series, many cartoon characters would also prove lucrative when licensed for all kinds of merchandise and for other media.

Animation has traditionally been very closely related to [comic books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_book "Comic book"). While many comic book characters found their way to the screen (which is often the case in Japan, where many [manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manga "Manga") are adapted into [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime "Anime")), original animated characters also commonly appear in comic books and magazines. Somewhat similarly, characters and plots for [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game") (an interactive form of animation that became its own medium) have been derived from films and vice versa.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-38)

Some of the original content produced for the screen can be used and marketed in other media. Stories and images can easily be adapted into children's books and other printed media. Songs and music have appeared on records and as streaming media.\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

While very many animation companies commercially exploit their creations outside moving image media, [The Walt Disney Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") is the best known and most extreme example. Since first being licensed for a children's writing tablet in 1929, their [Mickey Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mouse "Mickey Mouse") mascot has been [depicted on an enormous amount of products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mouse#Merchandising "Mickey Mouse"), as have many other Disney characters. This may have influenced some [pejorative use of Mickey's name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mouse#Pejorative_use_of_Mickey's_name "Mickey Mouse"), but [licensed Disney products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Consumer_Products "Disney Consumer Products") sell well, and the so-called [Disneyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyana "Disneyana") has many avid collectors, and even a dedicated [Disneyana Fan Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyana_Fan_Club "Disneyana Fan Club") (since 1984).[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-39)

[Disneyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland "Disneyland") opened in 1955 and features many attractions that were based on Disney's cartoon characters. Its enormous success spawned several other [Disney theme parks and resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Experiences#Disney_resorts "Disney Experiences"). [Disney's earnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company#Financial_data "The Walt Disney Company") from the theme parks have relatively often been higher than those from their movies.

## Production

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=15 "Edit section: Production")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/Joy_%26_Heron_-_Animated_CGI_Spot_by_Passion_Pictures.webm/220px--Joy_%26_Heron_-_Animated_CGI_Spot_by_Passion_Pictures.webm.jpg)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/1c/Joy_%26_Heron_-_Animated_CGI_Spot_by_Passion_Pictures.webm/Joy_%26_Heron_-_Animated_CGI_Spot_by_Passion_Pictures.webm.480p.vp9.webm)

*Joy & Heron*

The creation of non-trivial animation works (i.e., longer than a few seconds) has developed as a form of [filmmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmmaking "Filmmaking"), with certain unique aspects.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998117-44) Traits common to both live-action and animated [feature films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_film "Feature film") are labor intensity and high production costs.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon1989274-45)

The most important difference is that once a film is in the production phase, the [marginal cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_cost "Marginal cost") of one more shot is higher for animated films than live-action films.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhite2006151-46) It is relatively easy for a director to ask for one more [take](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take "Take") during [principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") of a live-action film, but every take on an animated film must be manually rendered by animators (although the task of rendering slightly different takes has been made less tedious by modern computer animation).[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998339-47) It is pointless for a studio to pay the salaries of dozens of animators to spend weeks creating a visually dazzling five-minute scene if that scene fails to effectively advance the plot of the film.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECulhane199055-48) Thus, animation studios starting with Disney began the practice in the 1930s of maintaining story departments where [storyboard artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storyboard_artist "Storyboard artist") develop every single scene through [storyboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storyboard "Storyboard"), then handing the film over to the animators only after the production team is satisfied that all the scenes make sense as a whole.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon1989120-49) While live-action films are now also storyboarded, they enjoy more latitude to depart from storyboards (i.e., real-time improvisation).\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*][\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998100%E2%80%9301-50)

Another problem unique to animation is the requirement to maintain a film's consistency from start to finish, even as films have grown longer and teams have grown larger. Animators, like all artists, necessarily have individual styles, but must subordinate their individuality in a consistent way to whatever style is employed on a particular film.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECanemaker1996xi%E2%80%93xii-51)[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasson200794-52) Since the early 1980s, teams of about 500 to 600 people, of whom 50 to 70 are animators, typically have created feature-length animated films. It is relatively easy for two or three artists to match their styles; synchronizing those of dozens of artists is more difficult.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeck200437-53)

This problem is usually solved by having a separate group of visual development artists develop an overall look and palette for each film before the animation begins.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECanemaker1996xi%E2%80%93xii-51) While animators must "sacrifice their personal drawing styles so that the work of many hands appears to be that of one", visual development artists are allowed to "create new worlds, new characters, and new entertainment possibilities in their own individualistic graphic styles".[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECanemaker1996xi%E2%80%93xii-51) Character designers on the visual development team draw [model sheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_sheet "Model sheet") to show how each character should look like with different facial expressions, posed in different positions, and viewed from different angles.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200134-54)[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECulhane1990146-55) On traditionally animated projects, [maquettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquette "Maquette") were often sculpted to further help the animators see how characters would look from different angles.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200134-54)[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200152%E2%80%9357-56)

Unlike live-action films, animated films were traditionally developed beyond the synopsis stage through the storyboard format; the storyboard artists would then receive credit for writing the film.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne199899%E2%80%93100-57)[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarx20073%E2%80%934-58) The traditional approach worked for several decades because prior to the 1960s, no one except Disney was attempting to regularly produce feature-length animated films.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarx20073%E2%80%934-58) All other animation studios, with occasional exceptions, were producing [short films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_film "Short film") only a few minutes in length.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarx20073%E2%80%934-58) For short films, it was enough for the storyboard artists to work up a few [visual gags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_gag "Visual gag") and then string them together to form a crude plot.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarx20073%E2%80%934-58)

In 1960, [Hanna-Barbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanna-Barbera "Hanna-Barbera") pioneered the longer [animated sitcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animated_sitcom "Animated sitcom") format for television with *[The Flintstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flintstones "The Flintstones")*.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarx20073%E2%80%934-58) Hanna-Barbera and the other early television animation studios soon discovered that storyboarding was far too inefficient to fill up a half-hour episode on the extremely tight budgets typical of television.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarx20073%E2%80%934-58) During the 1960s, these studios experimented with a more efficient method for developing story material: a [screenwriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screenwriter "Screenwriter") is hired to draft a written [screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screenplay "Screenplay") which is approved and handed over to the storyboard artists for storyboarding.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarx20073%E2%80%934-58) This method creates significant tension between screenwriters and storyboard artists, in that some artists feel that people who cannot draw should not be writing for animation, while some writers feel that artists do not understand how to write.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarx20073%E2%80%934-58) Despite that tension, it has become and remains the dominant method by which animation studios develop both feature-length films and television shows.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarx20073%E2%80%934-58)

## Techniques

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=16 "Edit section: Techniques")]

### Traditional

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=17 "Edit section: Traditional")]

Main article: [Traditional animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_animation "Traditional animation")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Animhorse.gif/220px-Animhorse.gif)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Animhorse.gif)

An example of traditional animation, a horse animated by [rotoscoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotoscoping "Rotoscoping") from [Eadweard Muybridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eadweard_Muybridge "Eadweard Muybridge")'s 19th-century photos

**Traditional animation** (also called cel animation or hand-drawn animation) is the process that was used for most animated films of the 20th century.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhite200631-59) The individual frames of a traditionally animated film are photographs of drawings, first drawn on paper.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeckerman2003153-60) To create the illusion of movement, each drawing differs slightly from the one before it. The animators' drawings are traced or photocopied onto transparent acetate sheets called [cels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cel "Cel"),[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomasJohnston1981277%E2%80%9379-61) which are filled in with paints in assigned colors or tones on the side opposite the line drawings.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998203-62) The completed character cels are photographed one-by-one against a painted background by a [rostrum camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostrum_camera "Rostrum camera") onto motion picture film.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhite2006195%E2%80%93201-63)

The traditional cel animation process became obsolete by the beginning of the 21st century. In modern traditionally animated films, animators' drawings and the backgrounds are either scanned into or drawn directly into a computer system.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuchan2013-1)[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhite2006394-64) Various software programs are used to color the drawings and simulate camera movement and effects.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECulhane1990296-65) The final animated piece is output to one of several delivery media, including traditional [35 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35_mm_movie_film "35 mm movie film") and newer media with [digital video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_video "Digital video").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuchan2013-1)[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne199835%E2%80%9336,_52%E2%80%9353-66) The "look" of traditional cel animation is still preserved, and the [character animators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_animation "Character animation")' work has remained essentially the same over the past 90 years.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200152%E2%80%9357-56) Some animation producers have used the term "tradigital" (a play on the words "traditional" and "digital") to describe cel animation that uses significant computer technology.

Examples of traditionally animated feature films include *[Pinocchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinocchio_\(1940_film\) "Pinocchio (1940 film)")* (United States, 1940),[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon198963%E2%80%9365-67) *[Animal Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Farm_\(1954_film\) "Animal Farm (1954 film)")* (United Kingdom, 1954), *[Lucky and Zorba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucky_and_Zorba "Lucky and Zorba")* (Italy, 1998), and *[The Illusionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Illusionist_\(2010_film\) "The Illusionist (2010 film)")* (British-French, 2010). Traditionally animated films produced with the aid of computer technology include *[The Lion King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lion_King "The Lion King")* (US, 1994), *[Anastasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_\(1997_film\) "Anastasia (1997 film)")* (US, 1997), *[The Prince of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince_of_Egypt "The Prince of Egypt")* (US, 1998), *[Akira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_\(1988_film\) "Akira (1988 film)")* (Japan, 1988),[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeckerman200380-68) *[Spirited Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirited_Away "Spirited Away")* (Japan, 2001), *[The Triplets of Belleville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Triplets_of_Belleville "The Triplets of Belleville")* (France, 2003), and *[The Secret of Kells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_of_Kells "The Secret of Kells")* (Irish-French-Belgian, 2009).

#### Full

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=18 "Edit section: Full")]

**Full animation** is the process of producing high-quality traditionally animated films that regularly use detailed drawings and plausible movement,[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECulhane199071-69) having a smooth animation.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECulhane1990194%E2%80%9395-70) Fully animated films can be made in a variety of styles, from more realistically animated works like those produced by the [Walt Disney studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Animation_Studios "Walt Disney Animation Studios") (*[The Little Mermaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Mermaid_\(1989_film\) "The Little Mermaid (1989 film)")*, *[Beauty and the Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_and_the_Beast_\(1991_film\) "Beauty and the Beast (1991 film)")*, *[Aladdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_\(1992_Disney_film\) "Aladdin (1992 Disney film)")*, *[The Lion King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lion_King "The Lion King")*) to the more 'cartoon' styles of the [Warner Bros. animation studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Cartoons "Warner Bros. Cartoons"). Many of the [Disney animated features](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_animated_features "Disney animated features") are examples of full animation, as are non-Disney works, *[The Secret of NIMH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_of_NIMH "The Secret of NIMH")* (US, 1982), *[The Iron Giant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Iron_Giant "The Iron Giant")* (US, 1999), and *[Nocturna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturna_\(film\) "Nocturna (film)")* (Spain, 2007). Fully animated films are often animated on "twos", sometimes on "ones", which means that 12 to 24 drawings are required for a single second of film. [\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne199825%E2%80%9326-71)

#### Limited

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=19 "Edit section: Limited")]

Main article: [Limited animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_animation "Limited animation")

**[Limited animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_animation "Limited animation")** involves the use of less detailed or more stylized drawings and methods of movement usually a choppy or "skippy" movement animation.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeckerman2003142-72) Limited animation uses fewer drawings per second, thereby limiting the fluidity of the animation. This is a more economic technique. Pioneered by the artists at the American studio [United Productions of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Productions_of_America "United Productions of America"),[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeckerman200354%E2%80%9355-73) limited animation can be used as a method of stylized artistic expression, as in *[Gerald McBoing-Boing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_McBoing-Boing "Gerald McBoing-Boing")* (US, 1951), *[Yellow Submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Submarine_\(film\) "Yellow Submarine (film)")* (UK, 1968), and certain [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime "Anime") produced in Japan.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELedoux199724,_29-74) Its primary use, however, has been in producing cost-effective animated content for media for television (the work of Hanna-Barbera,[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELawsonPersons200482-75) [Filmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmation "Filmation"),[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon1989241-76) and other TV animation studios[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELawsonPersons2004xxi-77)) and later the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet") ([web cartoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_cartoon "Web cartoon")).

#### Rotoscoping

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=20 "Edit section: Rotoscoping")]

Main article: [Rotoscoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotoscoping "Rotoscoping")

**Rotoscoping** is a technique patented by [Max Fleischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Fleischer "Max Fleischer") in 1917 where animators trace live-action movement, frame by frame.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrafton1993158-78) The source film can be directly copied from actors' outlines into animated drawings,[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998163%E2%80%9364-79) as in *[The Lord of the Rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_\(1978_film\) "The Lord of the Rings (1978 film)")* (US, 1978), or used in a stylized and expressive manner, as in *[Waking Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waking_Life "Waking Life")* (US, 2001) and *[A Scanner Darkly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Scanner_Darkly_\(film\) "A Scanner Darkly (film)")* (US, 2006). Some other examples are *[Fire and Ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_and_Ice_\(1983_film\) "Fire and Ice (1983 film)")* (US, 1983), *[Heavy Metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_Metal_\(film\) "Heavy Metal (film)")* (1981), and *[Aku no Hana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flowers_of_Evil_\(manga\) "The Flowers of Evil (manga)")* (Japan, 2013).\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

#### Live-action blending

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=21 "Edit section: Live-action blending")]

Main article: [Films with live action and animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Films_with_live_action_and_animation "Films with live action and animation")

**[Live-action/animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_with_live_action_and_animation "List of films with live action and animation")** is a technique combining hand-drawn characters into live action shots or live-action actors into animated shots.[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998162%E2%80%9363-80) One of the earlier uses was in [Koko the Clown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koko_the_Clown "Koko the Clown") when Koko was drawn over live-action footage.[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeck200418%E2%80%9319-81) Walt Disney and Ub Iwerks created a series of *[Alice Comedies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Comedies "Alice Comedies")* (1923–1927), in which a live-action girl enters an animated world. Other examples include *[Allegro Non Troppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegro_Non_Troppo "Allegro Non Troppo")* (Italy, 1976), *[Who Framed Roger Rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Framed_Roger_Rabbit "Who Framed Roger Rabbit")* (US, 1988), *[Volere volare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volere_volare "Volere volare")* (Italy 1991), *[Space Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Jam "Space Jam")* (US, 1996) and *[Osmosis Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmosis_Jones "Osmosis Jones")* (US, 2001).\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Stop motion

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=22 "Edit section: Stop motion")]

Main article: [Stop motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_motion "Stop motion")

[Stop motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_motion "Stop motion") is used to describe animation created by physically manipulating real-world objects and photographing them one frame of film at a time to create the illusion of movement.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon1989299-82) There are many different types of stop-motion animation, usually named after the materials used to create the animation.[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998159-83) Computer software is widely available to create this type of animation; traditional stop-motion animation is usually less expensive but more time-consuming to produce than current computer animation.[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998159-83)

- [Stop motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_motion "Stop motion")

  Typically involves stop-motion puppet figures interacting in a constructed environment, in contrast to real-world interaction in model animation.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon1989171-84) The puppets generally have an [armature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armature_\(sculpture\) "Armature (sculpture)") inside of them to keep them still and steady to constrain their motion to particular joints.[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998155%E2%80%9356-85) Examples include *[The Tale of the Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tale_of_the_Fox "The Tale of the Fox")* (France, 1937), *[The Nightmare Before Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nightmare_Before_Christmas "The Nightmare Before Christmas")* (US, 1993), *[Corpse Bride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpse_Bride "Corpse Bride")* (US, 2005), *[Coraline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coraline_\(film\) "Coraline (film)")* (US, 2009), the films of [Jiří Trnka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Trnka "Jiří Trnka") and the adult animated sketch-comedy television series *[Robot Chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot_Chicken "Robot Chicken")* (US, 2005–present).

  - [Puppetoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppetoons "Puppetoons")

    Created using techniques developed by [George Pal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pal "George Pal"),[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeck200470-86) are puppet-animated films that typically use a different version of a puppet for different frames, rather than manipulating one existing puppet.[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeck200492%E2%80%9393-87)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Claychick.jpg/220px-Claychick.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Claychick.jpg)

A clay animation scene from a [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland "Finland") television commercial

- [Clay animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_animation "Clay animation") or [Plasticine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasticine "Plasticine") animation

  (Often called *claymation*, which, however, is a [trademarked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laika,_LLC "Laika, LLC") name). It uses figures made of clay or a similar malleable material to create stop-motion animation.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon1989299-82)[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998150%E2%80%93151-88) The figures may have an [armature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armature_\(sculpture\) "Armature (sculpture)") or wire frame inside, similar to the related puppet animation (below), that can be manipulated to pose the figures.[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998151%E2%80%9354-89) Alternatively, the figures may be made entirely of clay, in the films of [Bruce Bickford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Bickford_\(animator\) "Bruce Bickford (animator)"), where clay creatures morph into a variety of different shapes. Examples of clay-animated works include *The [Gumby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gumby "Gumby") Show* (US, 1957–1967), *[Mio Mao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mio_Mao "Mio Mao")* (Italy, 1974–2005), *[Morph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morph_\(TV_series\) "Morph (TV series)")* shorts (UK, 1977–2000), *[Wallace & Gromit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_%26_Gromit "Wallace & Gromit")* shorts (UK, as of 1989), [Jan Švankmajer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_%C5%A0vankmajer "Jan Švankmajer")'s *[Dimensions of Dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensions_of_Dialogue "Dimensions of Dialogue")* ([Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia "Czechoslovakia"), 1982), *[The Trap Door](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trap_Door "The Trap Door")* (UK, 1984). Films include *[Wallace & Gromit: The Curse of the Were-Rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_%26_Gromit:_The_Curse_of_the_Were-Rabbit "Wallace & Gromit: The Curse of the Were-Rabbit")*, *[Chicken Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_Run "Chicken Run")* and *[The Adventures of Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Mark_Twain_\(1985_film\) "The Adventures of Mark Twain (1985 film)")*.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeck2004250-90)

  - [Strata-cut animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strata-cut_animation "Strata-cut animation")

    Most commonly a form of clay animation in which a long bread-like "loaf" of clay, internally packed tight and loaded with varying imagery, is sliced into thin sheets, with the animation camera taking a frame of the end of the loaf for each cut, eventually revealing the movement of the internal images within.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFurniss199852%E2%80%9354-91)

* [Cutout animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutout_animation "Cutout animation")

  A type of stop-motion animation produced by moving two-dimensional pieces of material paper or cloth.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne199859%E2%80%9360-92) Examples include [Terry Gilliam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Gilliam "Terry Gilliam")'s animated sequences from *[Monty Python's Flying Circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monty_Python%27s_Flying_Circus "Monty Python's Flying Circus")* (UK, 1969–1974); *[Fantastic Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantastic_Planet "Fantastic Planet")* (France/Czechoslovakia, 1973); *[Tale of Tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tale_of_Tales_\(1979_film\) "Tale of Tales (1979 film)")* (Russia, 1979), [Matt Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Stone "Matt Stone") and [Trey Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trey_Parker "Trey Parker") the first cutout animation [South Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park_\(franchise\) "South Park (franchise)") (1992), the pilot episode of the adult television sitcom series (and sometimes in episodes) of *[South Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park "South Park")* (US, 1997) and the music video Live for the moment, from Verona Riots band (produced by Alberto Serrano and Nívola Uyá, Spain 2014).

  - [Silhouette animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silhouette_animation "Silhouette animation")

    A variant of cutout animation in which the characters are backlit and only visible as silhouettes.[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECulhane1990170%E2%80%93171-93) Examples include *[The Adventures of Prince Achmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Prince_Achmed "The Adventures of Prince Achmed")* ([Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic"), 1926) and *[Princes et Princesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princes_et_Princesses "Princes et Princesses")* (France, 2000).

- [Model animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_animation "Model animation")

  Stop-motion animation created to interact with and exist as a part of a live-action world.[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarryhausenDalton20089%E2%80%9311-94) Intercutting, [matte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matte_\(filmmaking\) "Matte (filmmaking)") effects and split screens are often employed to blend stop-motion characters or objects with live actors and settings.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Harryhausen_Dalton222%E2%80%93226-95) Examples include the work of [Ray Harryhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Harryhausen "Ray Harryhausen"), as seen in films, *[Jason and the Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_and_the_Argonauts_\(1963_film\) "Jason and the Argonauts (1963 film)")* (1963),[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Harryhausen_Dalton18-96) and the work of [Willis H. O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_H._O%27Brien "Willis H. O'Brien") on films, *[King Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Kong_\(1933_film\) "King Kong (1933 film)")* (1933).

  - [Go motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_motion "Go motion")

    A variant of model animation that uses various techniques to create [motion blur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_blur "Motion blur") between frames of film, which is not present in traditional stop motion.[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESmith198690-97) The technique was invented by [Industrial Light & Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Light_%26_Magic "Industrial Light & Magic") and [Phil Tippett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Tippett "Phil Tippett") to create [special effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_effect "Special effect") scenes for the film *[Star Wars: Episode V – The Empire Strikes Back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_Episode_V_%E2%80%93_The_Empire_Strikes_Back "Star Wars: Episode V – The Empire Strikes Back")* (1980).[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWatercutter2012-98) Another example is the dragon named "Vermithrax" from the 1981 film *[Dragonslayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonslayer_\(1981_film\) "Dragonslayer (1981 film)")*.[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESmith198691%E2%80%9395-99)

* [Object animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_animation "Object animation")

  The use of regular inanimate objects in stop-motion animation, as opposed to specially created items.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne199851%E2%80%9357-100)

  - [Graphic animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_animation "Graphic animation")

    Uses non-drawn flat visual graphic material (photographs, newspaper clippings, magazines, etc.), which are sometimes manipulated frame by frame to create movement.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998128-101) At other times, the graphics remain stationary, while the stop-motion camera is moved to create on-screen action.

  - [Brickfilm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brickfilm "Brickfilm")

    A subgenre of object animation involving using [Lego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego "Lego") or other similar brick toys to make an animation.[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaul2005357%E2%80%9363-102)[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHerman2014-103) These have had a recent boost in popularity with the advent of video sharing sites, [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") and the availability of cheap cameras and [animation software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animation_software "Animation software").[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-AMPS-104)

- [Pixilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixilation "Pixilation")

  Involves the use of live humans as stop-motion characters.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne199875%E2%80%9379-105) This allows for a number of surreal effects, including disappearances and reappearances, allowing people to appear to slide across the ground, and other effects.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne199875%E2%80%9379-105) Examples of pixilation include *[The Secret Adventures of Tom Thumb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_Adventures_of_Tom_Thumb "The Secret Adventures of Tom Thumb")* and *[Angry Kid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angry_Kid "Angry Kid")* shorts, and the [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award")-winning *[Neighbours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbours_\(1952_film\) "Neighbours (1952 film)")* by [Norman McLaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_McLaren "Norman McLaren").

### Computer

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=23 "Edit section: Computer")]

Main article: [Computer animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_animation "Computer animation")

**Computer animation** encompasses a variety of techniques, the unifying factor being that the animation is created digitally on a computer.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECulhane1990296-65)[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESerenko2007-106) 2D animation techniques tend to focus on image manipulation while 3D techniques usually build virtual worlds in which characters and objects move and interact.[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasson2007405-107) 3D animation can create images that seem real to the viewer.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESerenko2007482-108)

#### 2D

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=24 "Edit section: 2D")]

Main article: [2D computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2D_computer_graphics "2D computer graphics")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/03/Catenary_animation.gif)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Catenary_animation.gif)

A 2D animation of two circles joined by a chain

[2D animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2D_computer_graphics "2D computer graphics") figures are created or edited on the computer using 2D [bitmap graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raster_graphics "Raster graphics") and 2D [vector graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_graphics "Vector graphics").[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasson2007165-109) This includes automated computerized versions of traditional animation techniques, [interpolated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpolation "Interpolation") [morphing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphing "Morphing"),[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESito201332,_70,_132-110) [onion skinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onion_skinning "Onion skinning")[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPriebe200671%E2%80%9372-111) and interpolated rotoscoping. 2D animation has many applications, including After Effects Animation, [analog computer animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scanimate "Scanimate"), [Flash animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_animation "Flash animation"), and [PowerPoint animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PowerPoint_animation "PowerPoint animation"). [Cinemagraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinemagraph "Cinemagraph") are [still photographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_life_photography "Still life photography") in the form of an [animated GIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animated_GIF "Animated GIF") file of which part is animated.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhite2006392-112)

Final line [advection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advection "Advection") animation is a technique used in 2D animation,[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELoweSchnotz2008246%E2%80%9347-113) to give artists and animators more influence and control over the final product as everything is done within the same department.[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasson2007127%E2%80%9328-114) Speaking about using this approach in *[Paperman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paperman "Paperman")*, John Kahrs said that "Our animators can change things, actually erase away the CG underlayer if they want, and change the profile of the arm."[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeck2012-115)

When working with game animations, [skeletal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_animation "Skeletal animation") 2D animations are commonly created using tools like Spine, DragonBones, Blender COA Tools, Rive, and the built-in Unity editor. The primary benefit of this approach is the ability to reuse images, which reduces the amount of graphics stored in RAM. This principle of maximizing resource efficiency means that by reusing existing elements, you can enhance the visual appeal of animations without needing to create additional graphics.[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-116)

#### 3D

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=25 "Edit section: 3D")]

Main articles: [Computer animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_animation "Computer animation") and [3D computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_computer_graphics "3D computer graphics")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Caminandes-_Llama_Drama_-_Short_Movie.ogv/220px--Caminandes-_Llama_Drama_-_Short_Movie.ogv.jpg)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d0/Caminandes-_Llama_Drama_-_Short_Movie.ogv/Caminandes-_Llama_Drama_-_Short_Movie.ogv.480p.vp9.webm)

**Caminandes | Llama Drama**

3D animation is digitally modeled and manipulated by an animator. The 3D model maker usually starts by creating a 3D [polygon mesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygon_mesh "Polygon mesh") for the animator to manipulate.[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasson200788-117) A mesh typically includes many vertices that are connected by edges and faces, which give the visual appearance of form to a 3D object or 3D environment.[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasson200788-117) Sometimes, the mesh is given an internal digital skeletal structure called an [armature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armature_\(computer_animation\) "Armature (computer animation)") that can be used to control the mesh by weighting the vertices.[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESito2013208-118)[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasson200778%E2%80%9380-119) This process is called rigging and can be used in conjunction with [key frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_frame "Key frame") to create movement.[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESito2013285-120)

Other techniques can be applied, mathematical functions (e.g., gravity, particle simulations), simulated fur or hair, and effects, fire and [water simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_animation "Fluid animation").[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasson200796-121) These techniques fall under the category of 3D dynamics.[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELoweSchnotz200892-122)

##### Terms

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=26 "Edit section: Terms")]

- **[Cel shading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cel_shading "Cel shading")** is used to mimic traditional animation using computer software.[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-123) The shading looks stark, with less blending of colors. Examples include *[Skyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyland "Skyland")* (2007, France), *[The Iron Giant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Iron_Giant "The Iron Giant")* (1999, U.S.), *[Futurama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurama "Futurama")* (1999, U.S.) *[Appleseed Ex Machina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appleseed_Ex_Machina "Appleseed Ex Machina")* (2007, Japan), *[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_The_Wind_Waker "The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker")* (2002, Japan), *[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Breath_of_the_Wild "The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild")* (2017, Japan)
- **[Machinima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinima "Machinima")** – Films created by screen capturing in video games and virtual worlds. The term originated from the software introduction in the 1980s [demoscene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demoscene "Demoscene"), as well as the 1990s recordings of the [first-person shooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_shooter "First-person shooter") video game *[Quake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quake_\(video_game\) "Quake (video game)")*.
- **[Motion capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_capture "Motion capture")** is used when live-action actors wear special suits that allow computers to copy their movements into CG characters.[\[124\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESito2013207%E2%80%9308-124)[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasson2007204-125) Examples include *[Polar Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Polar_Express_\(film\) "The Polar Express (film)")* (2004, US), *[Beowulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beowulf_\(2007_film\) "Beowulf (2007 film)")* (2007, US), *[A Christmas Carol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Christmas_Carol_\(2009_film\) "A Christmas Carol (2009 film)")* (2009, US), *[The Adventures of Tintin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tintin_\(film\) "The Adventures of Tintin (film)")* (2011, US) *[kochadiiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochadaiiyaan "Kochadaiiyaan")* (2014, India)
- **[Computer animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_animation "Computer animation")** is used primarily for animation that attempts to resemble real life while having a stylized cartoonish appearance, using advanced rendering that mimics in detail skin, plants, water, fire, clouds, etc.[\[126\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEParent200719-126) Examples include *[Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_\(2009_film\) "Up (2009 film)")* (2009, US), *[How to Train Your Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_to_Train_Your_Dragon_\(2010_film\) "How to Train Your Dragon (2010 film)")* (2010, US)
- **[Physically based animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physically_based_animation "Physically based animation")** is animation using [computer simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_simulation "Computer simulation").[\[127\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-127)

### Mechanical

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=27 "Edit section: Mechanical")]

- **[Animatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animatronics "Animatronics")** is the use of [mechatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechatronics "Mechatronics") to create machines that seem animate rather than robotic.

  - **[Audio-Animatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio-Animatronics "Audio-Animatronics")** is a form of [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics "Robotics") animation, combined with 3-D animation, created by [Walt Disney Imagineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Imagineering "Walt Disney Imagineering") for shows and attractions at Disney theme parks move and make noise (generally a recorded speech or song).[\[128\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPilling1997249-128) They are fixed to whatever supports them. They can sit and stand, and they cannot walk. An Audio-Animatron is different from an [android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(robot\) "Android (robot)")-type robot in that it uses prerecorded movements and sounds, rather than responding to external stimuli. In 2009, Disney created an interactive version of the technology called Autonomatronics.[\[129\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO'Keefe2014-129)
  - **[Linear Animation Generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_alternator "Linear alternator")** is a form of animation by using static picture frames installed in a tunnel or a shaft. The animation illusion is created by putting the viewer in a linear motion, parallel to the installed picture frames.[\[130\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEParent200722%E2%80%9323-130)

- **[Chuckimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuckimation "Chuckimation")** is a type of animation created by the makers of the television series *[Action League Now!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_League_Now! "Action League Now!")* in which characters/props are thrown, or chucked from off camera or wiggled around to simulate talking by unseen hands.[\[131\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenyon1998-131)

- The **[Magic Lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_lantern "Magic lantern")** used mechanical slides to project moving images. [Christiaan Huygens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiaan_Huygens "Christiaan Huygens") was thought to have invented the Magic Lantern in the mid-1600s.[\[132\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-132)

### Other

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Animation\&action=edit\&section=28 "Edit section: Other")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/World_of_Color_overview.jpg/220px-World_of_Color_overview.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:World_of_Color_overview.jpg)

*[World of Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Color "World of Color")* hydrotechnics at [Disney California Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_California_Adventure "Disney California Adventure") creates the illusion of motion using 1,200 fountains with high-definition projections on mist screens.

- **[Hydrotechnics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_engineering "Hydraulic engineering")**: a technique that includes lights, water, fire, fog, and lasers, with high-definition projections on mist screens.[\[133\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-133)
- **[Drawn-on-film animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drawn-on-film_animation "Drawn-on-film animation")**: a technique where footage is produced by creating the images directly on [film stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_stock "Film stock"); for example, by [Norman McLaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_McLaren "Norman McLaren"),[\[134\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFaberWalters20041979-134) [Len Lye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Len_Lye "Len Lye") and [Stan Brakhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Brakhage "Stan Brakhage").
- **[Paint-on-glass animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paint-on-glass_animation "Paint-on-glass animation")**: a technique for making animated films by manipulating slow drying [oil paints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_paint "Oil paint") on sheets of glass,[\[135\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPilling1997222-135) for example by [Aleksandr Petrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Petrov_\(animator\) "Aleksandr Petrov (animator)").[\[136\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-136)
- **[Erasure animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charcoal "Charcoal")**: a technique using traditional 2D media, photographed over time as the artist manipulates the image. For example, [William Kentridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Kentridge "William Kentridge") is famous for his [charcoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charcoal "Charcoal") erasure films,[\[137\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTECarbone2010-137) and [Piotr Dumała](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Duma%C5%82a "Piotr Dumała") for his auteur technique of animating scratches on plaster.[\[138\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-138)
- **[Pinscreen animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinscreen_animation "Pinscreen animation")**: makes use of a screen filled with movable pins that can be moved in or out by pressing an object onto the screen.[\[139\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTENeupert2011-139) The screen is lit from the side so that the pins cast shadows. The technique has been used to create animated films with a range of textural effects difficult to achieve with traditional cel animation.[\[140\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPilling1997204-140)
- **[Sand animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_animation "Sand animation")**: sand is moved around on a back- or front-[lighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light "Light") piece of glass to create each frame for an animated film.[\[141\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown20037-141) This creates an interesting effect when animated because of the light [contrast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrast_\(vision\) "Contrast (vision)").[\[142\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFurniss199830%E2%80%9333-142)
- **[Flip book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flip_book "Flip book")**: a flip book (sometimes, especially in British English, called a flick book) is a book with a series of pictures that vary gradually from one page to the next, so that when the pages are turned rapidly, the pictures appear to animate by simulating motion or some other change.[\[143\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne199822%E2%80%9324-143)[\[144\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon19898%E2%80%9310-144) Flip books are often illustrated books for children,[\[145\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne1998xiv-145) they also are geared towards adults and employ a series of photographs rather than drawings. Flip books are not always separate books, they appear as an added feature in ordinary books or magazines, often in the page corners.[\[143\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTELaybourne199822%E2%80%9324-143) Software packages and websites are also available that convert digital video files into custom-made flip books.[\[146\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhite2006203-146)
- **[Character animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_animation "Character animation")**
- **[Multi-sketch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sketch "Multi-sketch") animation**
- **[Special effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_effect "Special effect") animation**
- **[2.5D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2.5D "2.5D") Animation:** A mix of 2D and 3D animation elements that emphasize the illusion of depth utilizing the pseudo-3D effect.[\[147\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-147) During the 1970s, the term "2.5D" started to gain recognition.[\[148\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-vfxapprentice.com-148) But its background comes from anime and manga during the 1920s where theatrical stage productions were popular.[\[149\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-149) Stage adaptations of well-liked anime series featured live performances by voice actors called 2.5D.[\[148\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-vfxapprentice.com-148)
