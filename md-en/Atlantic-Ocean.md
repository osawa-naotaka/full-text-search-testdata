The **Atlantic Ocean** is the second-largest of the world's five [oceanic divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_of_the_oceans "Borders of the oceans"), with an area of about 85,133,000 km2 (32,870,000 sq mi).[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Atlantic_Ocean_%E2%80%93_Britannica-2) It covers approximately 17% of [Earth's surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth#Surface "Earth") and about 24% of its water surface area. During the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery"), it was known for separating the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World "New World") of the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas") ([North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America") and [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_America "South America")) from the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World") of [Afro-Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Eurasia "Afro-Eurasia") ([Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"), [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia"), and [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe")).

Through its separation of Afro-Eurasia from the Americas, the Atlantic Ocean has played a central role in the development of human society, globalization, and the histories of many nations. While the [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_colonization_of_North_America "Norse colonization of North America") were the first known humans to cross the Atlantic, it was the [expedition of Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_of_Christopher_Columbus "Expedition of Christopher Columbus") in 1492 that proved to be the most consequential. Columbus' expedition ushered in an [age of exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery") and [colonization of the Americas by European powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization_of_the_Americas_by_European_powers "Colonization of the Americas by European powers"), most notably [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire"), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire"), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire "French colonial empire"), and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire"). From the 16th to 19th centuries, the Atlantic Ocean was the center of both [an eponymous slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade") and the [Columbian exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_exchange "Columbian exchange") while occasionally hosting naval battles. Such naval battles, as well as growing trade from regional American powers like the United States and [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil"), both increased in degree during the early 20th century, and while no major military conflicts have taken place in the Atlantic recently,\[*[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")*] the ocean remains a core component of trade around the world.

The Atlantic Ocean occupies an elongated, S-shaped basin extending longitudinally between Europe and Africa to the east, and the Americas to the west. As one component of the interconnected [World Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Ocean "World Ocean"), it is connected in the north to the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean "Arctic Ocean"), to the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean") in the southwest, the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean") in the southeast, and the [Southern Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ocean "Southern Ocean") in the south. Other definitions describe the Atlantic as extending southward to [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica"). The Atlantic Ocean is divided in two parts, the northern and southern Atlantic, by the [Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator "Equator").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-5)

## Toponymy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: Toponymy")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/1710_De_La_Feuille_Map_of_Africa_-_Geographicus_-_Africa-lafeuille-1710.jpg/220px-1710_De_La_Feuille_Map_of_Africa_-_Geographicus_-_Africa-lafeuille-1710.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1710_De_La_Feuille_Map_of_Africa_-_Geographicus_-_Africa-lafeuille-1710.jpg)

The [Aethiopian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aethiopian_Ocean "Aethiopian Ocean") depicted in a 1710 French map of [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa")

The oldest known mentions of an "Atlantic" sea come from [Stesichorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stesichorus "Stesichorus") around mid-sixth century BC (Sch. A. R. 1. 211):[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-MangasPl%C3%A1cido1998-6) *Atlantikôi pelágei* (Ancient Greek: Ἀτλαντικῷ πελάγει, 'the Atlantic sea', etym. 'Sea of [Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_\(mythology\) "Atlas (mythology)")') and in *[The Histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histories_\(Herodotus\) "Histories (Herodotus)")* of [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus") around 450 BC (Hdt. 1.202.4): *Atlantis thalassa* (Ancient Greek: Ἀτλαντὶς θάλασσα, 'Sea of Atlas' or 'the Atlantic sea'[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7)) where the name refers to "the sea beyond the [pillars of Heracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Heracles "Pillars of Heracles")" which is said to be part of the sea that surrounds all land.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-8) In these uses, the name refers to [Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_\(mythology\) "Atlas (mythology)"), the [Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_\(mythology\) "Titan (mythology)") in [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology "Greek mythology"), who supported the heavens and who later appeared as a frontispiece in medieval maps and also lent his name to modern [atlases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_\(geography\) "Atlas (geography)").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Oxford-Dict-9) On the other hand, to early [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") sailors and in ancient Greek mythological literature such as the *[Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad "Iliad")* and the *[Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey "Odyssey")*, this all-encompassing ocean was instead known as [Oceanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanus "Oceanus"), the gigantic river that encircled the world; in contrast to the enclosed seas well known to the Greeks: the Mediterranean and the Black Sea.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10) In contrast, the term "Atlantic" originally referred specifically to the [Atlas Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Mountains "Atlas Mountains") in Morocco and the sea off the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar "Strait of Gibraltar") and the West African coast.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Oxford-Dict-9)

The term "**[Aethiopian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aethiopian_Ocean "Aethiopian Ocean")**", derived from [Ancient Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aethiopia "Aethiopia"), was applied to the southern Atlantic as late as the mid-19th century.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-AmCyclo-1873-11) During the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery"), the Atlantic was also known to English cartographers as the **Great Western Ocean**.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Steele1986-12)

The Atlantic as it crashed against the shores of [Clare Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Island "Clare Island"), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland "Ireland") in 1981 (Note: upturned native [currach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currach "Currach") boat in the foreground)

**The pond** is a term often used by British and American speakers in reference to the northern Atlantic Ocean, as a form of [meiosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiosis_\(figure_of_speech\) "Meiosis (figure of speech)"), or ironic understatement. It is used mostly when referring to events or circumstances "on this side of the pond" or "on the other side of the pond" or "across the pond", rather than to discuss the ocean itself.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-13) The term dates to 1640, first appearing in print in a pamphlet released during the reign of [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_England "Charles I of England"), and reproduced in 1869 in [Nehemiah Wallington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nehemiah_Wallington "Nehemiah Wallington")'s *Historical Notices of Events Occurring Chiefly in The Reign of Charles I*, where "great Pond" is used in reference to the Atlantic Ocean by [Francis Windebank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Windebank "Francis Windebank"), Charles I's [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_\(England\) "Secretary of State (England)").[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-14)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-16)

## Extent and data

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Extent and data")]

For complete lists of marginal seas and borders of the Atlantic, see [List of seas § Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_seas#Atlantic_Ocean "List of seas"), and [Borders of the oceans § Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_of_the_oceans#Atlantic_Ocean "Borders of the oceans").

For the extent of the Southern Ocean, see [Southern Ocean § Definitions and use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ocean#Definitions_and_use "Southern Ocean").

The [International Hydrographic Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hydrographic_Organization "International Hydrographic Organization") (IHO) defined the limits of the oceans and seas in 1953,[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-IHO-1953-17) but some of these definitions have been revised since then and some are not recognized by various authorities, institutions, and countries, for example the [CIA World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_World_Factbook "CIA World Factbook"). Correspondingly, the extent and number of oceans and seas vary.

The Atlantic Ocean is bounded on the west by North and South America. It connects to the Arctic Ocean through the [Labrador Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador_Sea "Labrador Sea"), [Denmark Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_Strait "Denmark Strait"), [Greenland Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_Sea "Greenland Sea"), [Norwegian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Sea "Norwegian Sea") and [Barents Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea "Barents Sea") with the northern divider passing through [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland "Iceland") and [Svalbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svalbard "Svalbard"). To the east, the boundaries of the ocean proper are Europe and Africa: the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar "Strait of Gibraltar") (where it connects with the [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea "Mediterranean Sea") – one of its [marginal seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_sea "Marginal sea") – and, in turn, the [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea "Black Sea"), both of which also touch upon Asia).

In the southeast, the Atlantic merges into the Indian Ocean. The [20° East meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_meridian_east "20th meridian east"), running south from [Cape Agulhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Agulhas "Cape Agulhas") to [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica") defines its border. In the 1953 definition it extends south to Antarctica, while in later maps it is bounded at the [60° parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_parallel_south "60th parallel south") by the Southern Ocean.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-IHO-1953-17)

The Atlantic has irregular coasts indented by numerous bays, gulfs and seas. These include the [Baltic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea "Baltic Sea"), [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea "Black Sea"), [Caribbean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Sea "Caribbean Sea"), [Davis Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Strait "Davis Strait"), [Denmark Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_Strait "Denmark Strait"), part of the [Drake Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_Passage "Drake Passage"), [Gulf of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico "Gulf of Mexico"), [Labrador Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador_Sea "Labrador Sea"), [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea "Mediterranean Sea"), [North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea "North Sea"), [Norwegian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Sea "Norwegian Sea"), almost all of the [Scotia Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotia_Sea "Scotia Sea"), and other tributary water bodies.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-CIA-World-1) Including these marginal seas the coast line of the Atlantic measures 111,866 km (69,510 mi) compared to 135,663 km (84,297 mi) for the Pacific.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-CIA-World-1)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-18)

Including its marginal seas, the Atlantic covers an area of 106,460,000 km2 (41,100,000 sq mi) or 23.5% of the global ocean and has a volume of 310,410,900 km3 (74,471,500 cu mi) or 23.3% of the total volume of the Earth's oceans. Excluding its marginal seas, the Atlantic covers 81,760,000 km2 (31,570,000 sq mi) and has a volume of 305,811,900 km3 (73,368,200 cu mi). The North Atlantic covers 41,490,000 km2 (16,020,000 sq mi) (11.5%) and the South Atlantic 40,270,000 km2 (15,550,000 sq mi) (11.1%).[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-ETOPO1-3) The average depth is 3,646 m (11,962 ft) and the maximum depth, the [Milwaukee Deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Deep "Milwaukee Deep") in the [Puerto Rico Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_Trench "Puerto Rico Trench"), is 8,376 m (27,480 ft).[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-20)

## Bathymetry

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Bathymetry")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/Atlantic_bathymetry.jpg/220px-Atlantic_bathymetry.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Atlantic_bathymetry.jpg)

[False color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_color "False color") map of ocean depth in the Atlantic basin

The [bathymetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathymetry "Bathymetry") of the Atlantic is dominated by a [submarine mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-ocean_ridge "Mid-ocean ridge") called the [Mid-Atlantic Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_Ridge "Mid-Atlantic Ridge") (MAR). It runs from 87°N or 300 km (190 mi) south of the [North Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole "North Pole") to the subantarctic [Bouvet Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouvet_Island "Bouvet Island") at [54°S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_parallel_south "54th parallel south").[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-WHC-MAR-24) Expeditions to explore the bathymertry of the Atlantic include the [*Challenger* expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_expedition "Challenger expedition") and the [German *Meteor* expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Meteor_expedition "German Meteor expedition"); as of 2001[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit), [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University")'s [Lamont–Doherty Earth Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamont%E2%80%93Doherty_Earth_Observatory "Lamont–Doherty Earth Observatory") and the [United States Navy Hydrographic Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_Hydrographic_Office "United States Navy Hydrographic Office") conduct research on the ocean.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)

### Mid-Atlantic Ridge

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=5 "Edit section: Mid-Atlantic Ridge")]

Main article: [Mid-Atlantic Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_Ridge "Mid-Atlantic Ridge")

The MAR divides the Atlantic longitudinally into two halves, in each of which a series of basins are delimited by secondary, transverse ridges. The MAR reaches above 2,000 m (6,600 ft) along most of its length, but is interrupted by larger transform faults at two places: the [Romanche Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanche_Trench "Romanche Trench") near the Equator and the [Gibbs fracture zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_fracture_zone "Gibbs fracture zone") at [53°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_parallel_north "53rd parallel north"). The MAR is a barrier for bottom water, but at these two transform faults deep water currents can pass from one side to the other.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-LevGood-2003-26)

The MAR rises 2–3 km (1.2–1.9 mi) above the surrounding ocean floor and its [rift valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rift_valley "Rift valley") is the [divergent boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergent_boundary "Divergent boundary") between the [North American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_plate "North American plate") and [Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_plate "Eurasian plate") plates in the North Atlantic and the [South American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_plate "South American plate") and [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_plate "African plate") plates in the South Atlantic. The MAR produces [basaltic volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basaltic_lava "Basaltic lava") in [Eyjafjallajökull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyjafjallaj%C3%B6kull "Eyjafjallajökull"), Iceland, and [pillow lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillow_lava "Pillow lava") on the ocean floor.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-27) The depth of water at the apex of the ridge is less than 2,700 m (1,500 [fathoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fathom "Fathom"); 8,900 [ft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_\(unit\) "Foot (unit)")) in most places, while the bottom of the ridge is three times as deep.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-28)

The MAR is intersected by two perpendicular ridges: the [Azores–Gibraltar transform fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores%E2%80%93Gibraltar_transform_fault "Azores–Gibraltar transform fault"), the boundary between the [Nubian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_plate "Nubian plate") and [Eurasian plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_plate "Eurasian plate"), intersects the MAR at the [Azores triple junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores_triple_junction "Azores triple junction"), on either side of the Azores microplate, near the [40°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40th_parallel_north "40th parallel north").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-29) A much vaguer, nameless boundary, between the North American and South American plates, intersects the MAR near or just north of the [Fifteen-Twenty fracture zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifteen-Twenty_fracture_zone "Fifteen-Twenty fracture zone"), approximately at [16°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_parallel_north "16th parallel north").[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-30)

In the 1870s, the [*Challenger* expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_expedition "Challenger expedition") discovered parts of what is now known as the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, or:

> An elevated ridge rising to an average height of about 1,900 fathoms \[3,500 m; 11,400 ft] below the surface traverses the basins of the North and South Atlantic in a meridianal direction from Cape Farewell, probably its far south at least as Gough Island, following roughly the outlines of the coasts of the Old and the New Worlds.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-31)

The remainder of the ridge was discovered in the 1920s by the [German *Meteor* expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Meteor_expedition "German Meteor expedition") using echo-sounding equipment.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-32) The exploration of the MAR in the 1950s led to the general acceptance of [seafloor spreading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafloor_spreading "Seafloor spreading") and [plate tectonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics "Plate tectonics").[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-WHC-MAR-24)

Most of the MAR runs under water but where it reaches the surfaces it has produced volcanic islands. While nine of these have collectively been nominated a [World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site") for their geological value, four of them are considered of "Outstanding Universal Value" based on their cultural and natural criteria: [Þingvellir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Eingvellir_National_Park "Þingvellir National Park"), Iceland; [Landscape of the Pico Island Vineyard Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_of_the_Pico_Island_Vineyard_Culture "Landscape of the Pico Island Vineyard Culture"), Portugal; [Gough and Inaccessible Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gough_and_Inaccessible_Islands "Gough and Inaccessible Islands"), United Kingdom; and Brazilian Atlantic Islands: [Fernando de Noronha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_Noronha "Fernando de Noronha") and [Atol das Rocas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atol_das_Rocas "Atol das Rocas") Reserves, Brazil.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-WHC-MAR-24)

### Ocean floor

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=6 "Edit section: Ocean floor")]

For a list of fracture zones in the Atlantic, see [List of fracture zones § Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fracture_zones#Atlantic_Ocean "List of fracture zones").

Continental shelves in the Atlantic are wide off Newfoundland, southernmost South America, and northeastern Europe. In the western Atlantic [carbonate platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonate_platform "Carbonate platform") dominate large areas, for example, the [Blake Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Plateau "Blake Plateau") and [Bermuda Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bermuda_Rise\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Bermuda Rise (page does not exist)"). The Atlantic is surrounded by [passive margins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_margin "Passive margin") except at a few locations where [active margins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_margin "Active margin") form deep [trenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_trench "Oceanic trench"): the [Puerto Rico Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_Trench "Puerto Rico Trench") (8,376 m or 27,480 ft maximum depth) in the western Atlantic and [South Sandwich Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sandwich_Trench "South Sandwich Trench") (8,264 m or 27,113 ft) in the South Atlantic. There are numerous submarine canyons off northeastern North America, western Europe, and northwestern Africa. Some of these canyons extend along the continental rises and farther into the abyssal plains as deep-sea channels.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-LevGood-2003-26)

In 1922, a historic moment in cartography and oceanography occurred. The USS *Stewart* used a Navy Sonic Depth Finder to draw a continuous map across the bed of the Atlantic. This involved little guesswork because the idea of sonar is straightforward with pulses being sent from the vessel, which bounce off the ocean floor, then return to the vessel.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-the_Atlantic-33) The deep ocean floor is thought to be fairly flat with occasional deeps, [abyssal plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssal_plain "Abyssal plain"), [trenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_trench "Oceanic trench"), [seamounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamount "Seamount"), [basins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_basin "Oceanic basin"), [plateaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_plateau "Oceanic plateau"), [canyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_canyon "Submarine canyon"), and some [guyots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyot "Guyot"). Various shelves along the margins of the continents constitute about 11% of the bottom topography with few deep channels cut across the continental rise.

The mean depth between [60°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_parallel_north "60th parallel north") and [60°S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_parallel_south "60th parallel south") is 3,730 m (12,240 ft), or close to the average for the global ocean, with a modal depth between 4,000 and 5,000 m (13,000 and 16,000 ft).[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-LevGood-2003-26)

In the South Atlantic the [Walvis Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walvis_Ridge "Walvis Ridge") and [Rio Grande Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_Rise "Rio Grande Rise") form barriers to ocean currents. The [Laurentian Abyss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurentian_Abyss "Laurentian Abyss") is found off the eastern coast of Canada.

## Water characteristics

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=7 "Edit section: Water characteristics")]

[![Visualisation of the Gulf Stream stretching from the Gulf of Mexico to Western Europe](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Gulf_Stream_Sea_Surface_Currents_and_Temperatures_NASA_SVS.jpg/400px-Gulf_Stream_Sea_Surface_Currents_and_Temperatures_NASA_SVS.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gulf_Stream_Sea_Surface_Currents_and_Temperatures_NASA_SVS.jpg)

As the Gulf Stream meanders across the North Atlantic from the North American east coast to Western Europe its temperature drops by 20 °C (36 °F).

[![Map displaying a looping line with arrows indicating that water flows eastward in the far Southern Ocean, angling northeast of Australia, turning sough-after passing Alaska, then crossing the mid-Pacific to flow north of Australia, continuing west below Africa, then turning northwest until reaching eastern Canada, then angling east to southern Europe, then finally turning south just below Greenland and flowing down the Americas' eastern coast, and resuming its flow eastward to complete the circle](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Circulacion_termohalina.jpg/220px-Circulacion_termohalina.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Circulacion_termohalina.jpg)

Path of the [thermohaline circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermohaline_circulation "Thermohaline circulation"). Purple paths represent deep-water currents, while blue paths represent surface currents.

Surface water temperatures, which vary with latitude, current systems, and season and reflect the latitudinal distribution of solar energy, range from below −2 °C (28 °F) to over 30 °C (86 °F). Maximum temperatures occur north of the equator, and minimum values are found in the polar regions. In the middle latitudes, the area of maximum temperature variations, values may vary by 7–8 °C (13–14 °F).[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)

From October to June the surface is usually covered with sea ice in the [Labrador Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador_Sea "Labrador Sea"), [Denmark Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_Strait "Denmark Strait"), and Baltic Sea.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)\[*[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]

The [Coriolis effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coriolis_effect "Coriolis effect") circulates North Atlantic water in a clockwise direction, whereas South Atlantic water circulates counter-clockwise. The south [tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tide "Tide") in the Atlantic Ocean are semi-diurnal; that is, two high tides occur every 24 lunar hours. In latitudes above [40° North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40th_parallel_north "40th parallel north") some east–west oscillation, known as the [North Atlantic oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_oscillation "North Atlantic oscillation"), occurs.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)

### Salinity

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=8 "Edit section: Salinity")]

On average, the Atlantic is the saltiest major ocean; surface water [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity "Salinity") in the open ocean ranges from 33 to 37 parts per thousand (3.3–3.7%) by mass and varies with latitude and season. Evaporation, precipitation, river inflow and [sea ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_ice "Sea ice") melting influence surface salinity values. Although the lowest salinity values are just north of the equator (because of heavy tropical rainfall), in general, the lowest values are in the high latitudes and along coasts where large rivers enter. Maximum salinity values occur at about [25° north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_parallel_north "25th parallel north") and [south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_parallel_south "25th parallel south"), in [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical "Subtropical") regions with low rainfall and high evaporation.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)

The high surface salinity in the Atlantic, on which the Atlantic [thermohaline circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermohaline_circulation "Thermohaline circulation") is dependent, is maintained by two processes: the [Agulhas Leakage/Rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agulhas_Current#Agulhas_leakage_and_rings "Agulhas Current"), which brings salty Indian Ocean waters into the South Atlantic, and the "Atmospheric Bridge", which evaporates subtropical Atlantic waters and exports it to the Pacific.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-34)

The Atlantic Ocean consists of four major, upper [water masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_mass "Water mass") with distinct temperature and salinity. The Atlantic subarctic upper water in the northernmost North Atlantic is the source for subarctic intermediate water and North Atlantic intermediate water. North Atlantic central water can be divided into the eastern and western North Atlantic central water since the western part is strongly affected by the Gulf Stream and therefore the upper layer is closer to underlying fresher subpolar intermediate water. The eastern water is saltier because of its proximity to Mediterranean water. North Atlantic central water flows into South Atlantic central water at [15°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_parallel_north "15th parallel north").[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Emery-Atlantic-36)

There are five intermediate waters: four low-salinity waters formed at subpolar latitudes and one high-salinity formed through evaporation. Arctic intermediate water flows from the north to become the source for North Atlantic deep water, south of the Greenland-Scotland sill. These two intermediate waters have different salinity in the western and eastern basins. The wide range of salinities in the North Atlantic is caused by the asymmetry of the northern subtropical [gyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyre "Gyre") and a large number of contributions from a wide range of sources: Labrador Sea, Norwegian-Greenland Sea, Mediterranean, and South Atlantic Intermediate Water.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Emery-Atlantic-36)

The [North Atlantic deep water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Deep_Water "North Atlantic Deep Water") (NADW) is a complex of four water masses, two that form by deep convection in the open ocean – classical and upper Labrador sea water – and two that form from the inflow of dense water across the Greenland-Iceland-Scotland sill – Denmark Strait and Iceland-Scotland overflow water. Along its path across Earth the composition of the NADW is affected by other water masses, especially [Antarctic bottom water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Bottom_Water "Antarctic Bottom Water") and Mediterranean overflow water.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-37) The NADW is fed by a flow of warm shallow water into the northern North Atlantic which is responsible for the anomalous warm climate in Europe. Changes in the formation of NADW have been linked to global climate changes in the past. Since human-made substances were introduced into the environment, the path of the NADW can be traced throughout its course by measuring tritium and radiocarbon from [nuclear weapon tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_test "Nuclear weapon test") in the 1960s and [CFCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorofluorocarbon "Chlorofluorocarbon").[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-38)

World map of the five major ocean gyres

The clockwise warm-water [North Atlantic Gyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Gyre "North Atlantic Gyre") occupies the northern Atlantic, and the counter-clockwise warm-water [South Atlantic Gyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Gyre "South Atlantic Gyre") appears in the southern Atlantic.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)

In the North Atlantic, surface circulation is dominated by three inter-connected currents: the [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream "Gulf Stream") which flows north-east from the North American coast at [Cape Hatteras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Hatteras "Cape Hatteras"); the [North Atlantic Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Current "North Atlantic Current"), a branch of the Gulf Stream which flows northward from the [Grand Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Banks_of_Newfoundland "Grand Banks of Newfoundland"); and the [Subpolar Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Subpolar_Front\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Subpolar Front (page does not exist)"), an extension of the North Atlantic Current, a wide, vaguely defined region separating the subtropical gyre from the subpolar gyre. This system of currents transports warm water into the North Atlantic, without which temperatures in the North Atlantic and Europe would plunge dramatically.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-39)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/North_Atlantic_Circulation.gif/220px-North_Atlantic_Circulation.gif)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:North_Atlantic_Circulation.gif)

In the subpolar gyre of the North Atlantic warm subtropical waters are transformed into colder subpolar and polar waters. In the Labrador Sea this water flows back to the subtropical gyre.

North of the North Atlantic Gyre, the cyclonic [North Atlantic Subpolar Gyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=North_Atlantic_Subpolar_Gyre\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "North Atlantic Subpolar Gyre (page does not exist)") plays a key role in climate variability. It is governed by ocean currents from marginal seas and regional topography, rather than being steered by wind, both in the deep ocean and at sea level.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-40) The subpolar gyre forms an important part of the global [thermohaline circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermohaline_circulation "Thermohaline circulation"). Its eastern portion includes [eddying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddy_\(fluid_dynamics\) "Eddy (fluid dynamics)") branches of the [North Atlantic Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Current "North Atlantic Current") which transport warm, saline waters from the subtropics to the northeastern Atlantic. There this water is cooled during winter and forms return currents that merge along the eastern continental slope of Greenland where they form an intense (40–50 [Sv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sverdrup "Sverdrup")) current which flows around the continental margins of the [Labrador Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador_Sea "Labrador Sea"). A third of this water becomes part of the deep portion of the [North Atlantic Deep Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Deep_Water "North Atlantic Deep Water") (NADW). The NADW, in turn, feeds the [meridional overturning circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridional_overturning_circulation "Meridional overturning circulation") (MOC), the northward heat transport of which is threatened by anthropogenic climate change. Large variations in the subpolar gyre on a decade-century scale, associated with the [North Atlantic oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_oscillation "North Atlantic oscillation"), are especially pronounced in [Labrador Sea Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador_Sea_Water "Labrador Sea Water"), the upper layers of the MOC.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-41)

The South Atlantic is dominated by the anti-cyclonic southern subtropical gyre. The [South Atlantic Central Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=South_Atlantic_Central_Water\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "South Atlantic Central Water (page does not exist)") originates in this gyre, while [Antarctic Intermediate Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Intermediate_Water "Antarctic Intermediate Water") originates in the upper layers of the circumpolar region, near the [Drake Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_Passage "Drake Passage") and the Falkland Islands. Both these currents receive some contribution from the Indian Ocean. On the African east coast, the small cyclonic [Angola Gyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Angola_Gyre\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Angola Gyre (page does not exist)") lies embedded in the large subtropical gyre.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-42) The southern subtropical gyre is partly masked by a wind-induced [Ekman layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekman_layer "Ekman layer"). The residence time of the gyre is 4.4–8.5 years. [North Atlantic Deep Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Deep_Water "North Atlantic Deep Water") flows southward below the [thermocline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermocline "Thermocline") of the subtropical gyre.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-43)

### Sargasso Sea

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=11 "Edit section: Sargasso Sea")]

Main article: [Sargasso Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargasso_Sea "Sargasso Sea")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Sargasso.png/200px-Sargasso.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sargasso.png)

Approximate extent of the Sargasso Sea

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/HanaOZ.jpg/200px-HanaOZ.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:HanaOZ.jpg)

Sargassum fish (*Histrio histrio*)

The Sargasso Sea in the western North Atlantic can be defined as the area where two species of *[Sargassum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargassum "Sargassum")* (*S. fluitans* and *natans*) float, an area 4,000 km (2,500 mi) wide and encircled by the [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream "Gulf Stream"), [North Atlantic Drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Drift "North Atlantic Drift"), and [North Equatorial Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Equatorial_Current "North Equatorial Current"). This population of seaweed probably originated from Tertiary ancestors on the European shores of the former [Tethys Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tethys_Ocean "Tethys Ocean") and has, if so, maintained itself by [vegetative growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetative_reproduction "Vegetative reproduction"), floating in the ocean for millions of years.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-L%C3%BCn-p223-44)

Other species endemic to the Sargasso Sea include the [sargassum fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargassum_fish "Sargassum fish"), a predator with algae-like appendages which hovers motionless among the *Sargassum*. Fossils of similar fishes have been found in fossil bays of the former Tethys Ocean, in what is now the [Carpathian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains "Carpathian Mountains") region, that were similar to the Sargasso Sea. It is possible that the population in the Sargasso Sea migrated to the Atlantic as the Tethys closed at the end of the Miocene around 17 Ma.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-L%C3%BCn-p223-44) The origin of the Sargasso fauna and flora remained enigmatic for centuries. The fossils found in the Carpathians in the mid-20th century often called the "quasi-Sargasso assemblage", finally showed that this assemblage originated in the [Carpathian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Basin "Carpathian Basin") from where it migrated over [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily "Sicily") to the central Atlantic where it evolved into modern species of the Sargasso Sea.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-45)

The location of the spawning ground for European eels [remained unknown for decades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eel_life_history#Search_for_the_spawning_grounds "Eel life history"). In the early 19th century it was discovered that the southern Sargasso Sea is the spawning ground for both the [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_eel "European eel") and [American eel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_eel "American eel") and that the former migrate more than 5,000 km (3,100 mi) and the latter 2,000 km (1,200 mi). Ocean currents such as the Gulf Stream transport eel larvae from the Sargasso Sea to foraging areas in North America, Europe, and northern Africa.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-46) Recent but disputed research suggests that eels possibly use [Earth's magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_magnetic_field "Earth's magnetic field") to navigate through the ocean both as larvae and as adults.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-47)

## Climate

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=12 "Edit section: Climate")]

[![Map of Caribbean showing seven approximately parallel westward-pointing arrows that extend from east of the Virgin Islands to Cuba. The southern arrows bend northward just east of the Dominican Republic before straightening out again.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Atlantic_hurricane_graphic.png/220px-Atlantic_hurricane_graphic.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Atlantic_hurricane_graphic.png)

Waves in the [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds "Trade winds") in the Atlantic Ocean – areas of converging winds that move along the same track as the prevailing wind – create instabilities in the atmosphere that may lead to the formation of hurricanes.

The climate is influenced by the temperatures of the surface waters and water currents as well as winds. Because of the ocean's great capacity to store and release heat, maritime climates are more moderate and have less extreme seasonal variations than inland climates. [Precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\) "Precipitation (meteorology)") can be approximated from coastal weather data and air temperature from water temperatures.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)

The oceans are the major source of atmospheric moisture that is obtained through evaporation. Climatic zones vary with latitude; the warmest zones stretch across the Atlantic north of the equator. The coldest zones are in high latitudes, with the coldest regions corresponding to the areas covered by sea ice. Ocean currents influence the climate by transporting warm and cold waters to other regions. The winds that are cooled or warmed when blowing over these currents influence adjacent land areas.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)

The [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream "Gulf Stream") and its northern extension towards Europe, the [North Atlantic Drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Current "North Atlantic Current") is thought to have at least some influence on climate. For example, the Gulf Stream helps moderate winter temperatures along the coastline of southeastern North America, keeping it warmer in winter along the coast than inland areas. The Gulf Stream also keeps extreme temperatures from occurring on the Florida Peninsula. In the higher latitudes, the North Atlantic Drift, warms the atmosphere over the oceans, keeping the British Isles and northwestern Europe mild and cloudy, and not severely cold in winter, like other locations at the same high latitude. The cold water currents contribute to heavy fog off the coast of eastern Canada (the [Grand Banks of Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Banks_of_Newfoundland "Grand Banks of Newfoundland") area) and Africa's northwestern coast. In general, winds transport moisture and air over land areas.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)

### Natural hazards

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=13 "Edit section: Natural hazards")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Iceberg_A22A%2C_South_Atlantic_Ocean.jpg/220px-Iceberg_A22A%2C_South_Atlantic_Ocean.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Iceberg_A22A,_South_Atlantic_Ocean.jpg)

Iceberg A22A in the South Atlantic Ocean

Every winter, the [Icelandic Low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_Low "Icelandic Low") produces frequent storms. [Icebergs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceberg "Iceberg") are common from early February to the end of July across the shipping lanes near the [Grand Banks of Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Banks_of_Newfoundland "Grand Banks of Newfoundland"). The ice season is longer in the polar regions, but there is little shipping in those areas.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-48)

[Hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_hurricane "Atlantic hurricane") are a hazard in the western parts of the North Atlantic during the summer and autumn. Due to a consistently strong [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear "Wind shear") and a weak [Intertropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertropical_Convergence_Zone "Intertropical Convergence Zone"), [South Atlantic tropical cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_tropical_cyclone "South Atlantic tropical cyclone") are rare.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-49)

## Geology and plate tectonics

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=14 "Edit section: Geology and plate tectonics")]

The Atlantic Ocean is underlain mostly by dense [mafic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafic "Mafic") oceanic crust made up of [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt "Basalt") and [gabbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabbro "Gabbro") and overlain by fine clay, silt and siliceous ooze on the abyssal plain. The continental margins and continental shelf mark lower density, but greater thickness [felsic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felsic "Felsic") continental rock that is often much older than that of the seafloor. The oldest oceanic crust in the Atlantic is up to 145 million years and is situated off the west coast of Africa and the east coast of North America, or on either side of the South Atlantic.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-50)

In many places, the continental shelf and continental slope are covered in thick sedimentary layers. For instance, on the North American side of the ocean, large carbonate deposits formed in warm shallow waters such as Florida and the Bahamas, while coarse river outwash sands and silt are common in shallow shelf areas like the [Georges Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Bank "Georges Bank"). Coarse sand, boulders, and rocks were transported into some areas, such as off the coast of Nova Scotia or the [Gulf of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Maine "Gulf of Maine") during the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene "Pleistocene") ice ages.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-51)

### Central Atlantic

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=15 "Edit section: Central Atlantic")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Blakey_200Ma_-_COL.jpg/180px-Blakey_200Ma_-_COL.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Blakey_200Ma_-_COL.jpg)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Blakey_170Ma_-_COL.jpg/180px-Blakey_170Ma_-_COL.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Blakey_170Ma_-_COL.jpg)

Opening of the central Atlantic 200–170 Ma

The break-up of Pangaea began in the central Atlantic, between North America and Northwest Africa, where rift basins opened during the Late Triassic and Early Jurassic. This period also saw the first stages of the uplift of the Atlas Mountains. The exact timing is controversial with estimates ranging from 200 to 170 Ma.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-52)

The opening of the Atlantic Ocean coincided with the initial break-up of the supercontinent [Pangaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangaea "Pangaea"), both of which were initiated by the eruption of the [Central Atlantic Magmatic Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Atlantic_Magmatic_Province "Central Atlantic Magmatic Province") (CAMP), one of the most extensive and voluminous [large igneous provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_igneous_province "Large igneous province") in Earth's history associated with the [Triassic–Jurassic extinction event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic%E2%80%93Jurassic_extinction_event "Triassic–Jurassic extinction event"), one of Earth's major [extinction events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction_event "Extinction event").[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-53) Theoliitic [dikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dike_\(geology\) "Dike (geology)"), [sills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_sill "Aquatic sill"), and lava flows from the CAMP eruption at 200 Ma have been found in West Africa, eastern North America, and northern South America. The extent of the volcanism has been estimated to 4.5×106 km2 (1.7×106 sq mi) of which 2.5×106 km2 (9.7×105 sq mi) covered what is now northern and central Brazil.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-54)

The formation of the [Central American Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Isthmus "Central American Isthmus") closed the [Central American Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Seaway "Central American Seaway") at the end of the Pliocene 2.8 Ma ago. The formation of the isthmus resulted in the migration and extinction of many land-living animals, known as the [Great American Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_American_Interchange "Great American Interchange"), but the closure of the seaway resulted in a "Great American Schism" as it affected ocean currents, salinity, and temperatures in both the Atlantic and Pacific. Marine organisms on both sides of the isthmus became isolated and either diverged or went extinct.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-55)

### North Atlantic

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=16 "Edit section: North Atlantic")]

See also: [Opening of the North Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opening_of_the_North_Atlantic_Ocean "Opening of the North Atlantic Ocean") and [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO")

Geologically, the northern Atlantic is the area delimited to the south by two conjugate margins, Newfoundland and Iberia, and to the north by the Arctic [Eurasian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Basin "Eurasian Basin"). The opening of the northern Atlantic closely followed the margins of its predecessor, the [Iapetus Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iapetus_Ocean "Iapetus Ocean"), and spread from the central Atlantic in six stages: [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula "Iberian Peninsula")–[Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_\(island\) "Newfoundland (island)"), [Porcupine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcupine_Bank "Porcupine Bank")–North America, Eurasia–Greenland, Eurasia–North America. Active and inactive spreading systems in this area are marked by the interaction with the [Iceland hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_hotspot "Iceland hotspot").[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-56)

Seafloor spreading led to the extension of the crust and the formation of troughs and sedimentary basins. The Rockall Trough opened between 105 and 84 million years ago although the rift failed along with one leading into the [Bay of Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Biscay "Bay of Biscay").[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFittonLarsen199915-57)

Spreading began opening the [Labrador Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador_Sea "Labrador Sea") around 61 million years ago, continuing until 36 million years ago. Geologists distinguish two magmatic phases. One from 62 to 58 million years ago predates the separation of Greenland from northern Europe while the second from 56 to 52 million years ago happened as the separation occurred.

[Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_Plateau "Iceland Plateau") began to form 62 million years ago due to a particularly concentrated mantle plume. Large quantities of [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt "Basalt") erupted at this time period are found on Baffin Island, Greenland, the Faroe Islands, and Scotland, with ash falls in Western Europe acting as a stratigraphic marker.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFittonLarsen199910-58) The opening of the North Atlantic caused a significant uplift of continental crust along the coast. For instance, despite 7 km thick basalt, Gunnbjorn Field in East Greenland is the highest point on the island, elevated enough that it exposes older Mesozoic sedimentary rocks at its base, similar to old lava fields above sedimentary rocks in the uplifted Hebrides of western Scotland. [\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFittonLarsen199923%E2%80%9324-59)

The North Atlantic Ocean contains about 810 [seamounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamount "Seamount"), most of them situated along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-60) The [OSPAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSPAR_Convention "OSPAR Convention") database (Convention for the Protection of the Marine Environment of the North-East Atlantic) mentions 104 seamounts: 74 within national [exclusive economic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone "Exclusive economic zone"). Of these seamounts, 46 are located close to the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula "Iberian Peninsula").

### South Atlantic

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=17 "Edit section: South Atlantic")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Gondwana115Ma_hg.png/180px-Gondwana115Ma_hg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gondwana115Ma_hg.png)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Gondwana065Ma_hg.png/180px-Gondwana065Ma_hg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gondwana065Ma_hg.png)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Gondwana038ma_hg.png/180px-Gondwana038ma_hg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gondwana038ma_hg.png)

The opening of the South Atlantic

West Gondwana (South America and Africa) broke up in the Early Cretaceous to form the South Atlantic. The apparent fit between the coastlines of the two continents was noted on the first maps that included the South Atlantic and it was also the subject of the first computer-assisted plate tectonic reconstructions in 1965.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Eagles-intro-61)[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-62) This magnificent fit, however, has since then proven problematic and later reconstructions have introduced various deformation zones along the shorelines to accommodate the northward-propagating break-up.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Eagles-intro-61) Intra-continental rifts and deformations have also been introduced to subdivide both continental plates into sub-plates.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seton-2012-p217-63)

Geologically, the South Atlantic can be divided into four segments: equatorial segment, from 10°N to the Romanche fracture zone (RFZ); central segment, from RFZ to Florianopolis fracture zone (FFZ, north of Walvis Ridge and Rio Grande Rise); southern segment, from FFZ to the Agulhas–Falkland fracture zone (AFFZ); and Falkland segment, south of AFFZ.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Torsvik2009-p1316-64)

In the southern segment the Early Cretaceous (133–130 Ma) intensive [magmatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magmatism "Magmatism") of the [Paraná–Etendeka Large Igneous Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_and_Etendeka_traps "Paraná and Etendeka traps") produced by the [Tristan hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_hotspot "Tristan hotspot") resulted in an estimated volume of 1.5×106 to 2.0×106 km3 (3.6×105 to 4.8×105 cu mi). It covered an area of 1.2×106 to 1.6×106 km2 (4.6×105 to 6.2×105 sq mi) in Brazil, Paraguay, and Uruguay and 0.8×105 km2 (3.1×104 sq mi) in Africa. [Dyke swarms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyke_swarm "Dyke swarm") in Brazil, Angola, eastern Paraguay, and Namibia, however, suggest the LIP originally covered a much larger area and also indicate failed rifts in all these areas. Associated offshore basaltic flows reach as far south as the Falkland Islands and South Africa. Traces of magmatism in both offshore and onshore basins in the central and southern segments have been dated to 147–49 Ma with two peaks between 143 and 121 Ma and 90–60 Ma.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Torsvik2009-p1316-64)

In the Falkland segment rifting began with dextral movements between the Patagonia and Colorado sub-plates between the Early Jurassic (190 Ma) and the Early Cretaceous (126.7 Ma). Around 150 Ma sea-floor spreading propagated northward into the southern segment. No later than 130 Ma rifting had reached the Walvis Ridge–Rio Grande Rise.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seton-2012-p217-63)

In the central segment, rifting started to break Africa in two by opening the [Benue Trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benue_Trough "Benue Trough") around 118 Ma. Rifting in the central segment, however, coincided with the [Cretaceous Normal Superchron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetic_reversal#Superchrons "Geomagnetic reversal") (also known as the Cretaceous quiet period), a 40 Ma period without magnetic reversals, which makes it difficult to date sea-floor spreading in this segment.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seton-2012-p217-63)

The equatorial segment is the last phase of the break-up, but, because it is located on the Equator, magnetic anomalies cannot be used for dating. Various estimates date the propagation of seafloor spreading in this segment and consequent opening of the Equatorial Atlantic Gateway (EAG) to the period 120–96 Ma.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-GiorgioniEtAl2015-65)[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-LateAptianPalaeoceanography-66) This final stage, nevertheless, coincided with or resulted in the end of continental extension in Africa.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Seton-2012-p217-63)

About 50 Ma the opening of the [Drake Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_Passage "Drake Passage") resulted from a change in the motions and separation rate of the South American and Antarctic plates. First, small ocean basins opened and a shallow gateway appeared during the Middle Eocene. 34–30 Ma a deeper seaway developed, followed by an [Eocene–Oligocene climatic deterioration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene%E2%80%93Oligocene_extinction_event "Eocene–Oligocene extinction event") and the growth of the [Antarctic ice sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_ice_sheet "Antarctic ice sheet").[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-67)

### Closure of the Atlantic

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=18 "Edit section: Closure of the Atlantic")]

See also: [Pangaea Proxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangaea_Proxima "Pangaea Proxima")

An embryonic subduction margin is potentially developing west of Gibraltar. The [Gibraltar Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Arc "Gibraltar Arc") in the western Mediterranean is migrating westward into the central Atlantic where it joins the converging African and Eurasian plates. Together these three tectonic forces are slowly developing into a new subduction system in the eastern Atlantic Basin. Meanwhile, the [Scotia Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotia_Arc "Scotia Arc") and [Caribbean plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_plate "Caribbean plate") in the western Atlantic Basin are eastward-propagating subduction systems that might, together with the Gibraltar system, represent the beginning of the closure of the Atlantic Ocean and the final stage of the Atlantic [Wilson cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson_cycle "Wilson cycle").[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-68)

## History

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=19 "Edit section: History")]

Main category: [History of the Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:History_of_the_Atlantic_Ocean "Category:History of the Atlantic Ocean")

### Human origin

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=20 "Edit section: Human origin")]

[Humans evolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_evolution "Human evolution") in Africa; around 7 mya; then developing stone tools around 2.6 mya; to finally evolve as [modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomically_modern_human "Anatomically modern human") around 200 kya. The earliest evidence for the complex behavior associated with this [behavioral modernity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_modernity "Behavioral modernity") has been found in the Greater [Cape Floristic Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Floristic_Region "Cape Floristic Region") (GCFR) along the coast of South Africa. During the latest glacial stages, the now-submerged plains of the [Agulhas Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agulhas_Bank "Agulhas Bank") were exposed above sea level, extending the South African coastline farther south by hundreds of kilometers. A small population of modern humans – probably fewer than a thousand reproducing individuals – survived glacial maxima by exploring the high diversity offered by these Palaeo-Agulhas plains. The GCFR is delimited to the north by the [Cape Fold Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fold_Belt "Cape Fold Belt") and the limited space south of it resulted in the development of social networks out of which complex Stone Age technologies emerged.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-69) Human history thus begins on the coasts of South Africa where the Atlantic [Benguela Upwelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguela_Current "Benguela Current") and the Indian Ocean [Agulhas Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agulhas_Current "Agulhas Current") meet to produce an intertidal zone on which shellfish, fur seal, fish and sea birds provided the necessary protein sources.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-70) The African origin of this modern behaviour is evidenced by 70,000 years-old engravings from [Blombos Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blombos_Cave "Blombos Cave"), South Africa.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-71)

### Old World

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=21 "Edit section: Old World")]

[Mitochondrial DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_DNA "Mitochondrial DNA") (mtDNA) studies indicate that 80–60,000 years ago a major demographic expansion within Africa, derived from a single, small population, coincided with the emergence of behavioral complexity and the rapid [MIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_isotope_stage "Marine isotope stage") 5–4 environmental changes. This group of people not only expanded over the whole of Africa, but also started to disperse [out of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans "Recent African origin of modern humans") into Asia, Europe, and Australasia around 65,000 years ago and quickly replaced the archaic humans in these regions.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-72) During the [Last Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum "Last Glacial Maximum") (LGM) 20,000 years ago humans had to abandon their initial settlements along the European North Atlantic coast and retreat to the Mediterranean. Following rapid climate changes at the end of the LGM this region was repopulated by [Magdalenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalenian "Magdalenian") culture. Other hunter-gatherers followed in waves interrupted by large-scale hazards such as the [Laacher See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laacher_See "Laacher See") volcanic eruption, the inundation of [Doggerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doggerland "Doggerland") (now the [North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea "North Sea")), and the formation of the [Baltic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea "Baltic Sea").[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-73) The European coasts of the North Atlantic were permanently populated about 9–8.5 thousand years ago.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-74)

This human dispersal left abundant traces along the coasts of the Atlantic Ocean. 50 kya-old, deeply stratified [shell middens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_midden "Shell midden") found in [Ysterfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ysterfontein "Ysterfontein") on the western coast of South Africa are associated with the Middle Stone Age (MSA). The MSA population was small and dispersed and the rate of their reproduction and exploitation was less intense than those of later generations. While their middens resemble 12–11 kya-old Late Stone Age (LSA) middens found on every inhabited continent, the 50–45 kya-old [Enkapune Ya Muto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enkapune_Ya_Muto "Enkapune Ya Muto") in Kenya probably represents the oldest traces of the first modern humans to disperse out of Africa.[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-75)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Meilgaard-boplads.jpg/220px-Meilgaard-boplads.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Meilgaard-boplads.jpg)

Excavation of the [Ertebølle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erteb%C3%B8lle_culture "Ertebølle culture") middens in 1880

The same development can be seen in Europe. In [La Riera Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=La_Riera_Cave\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "La Riera Cave (page does not exist)") (23–13 kya) in Asturias, Spain, only some 26,600 molluscs were deposited over 10 kya. In contrast, 8–7 kya-old shell middens in Portugal, Denmark, and Brazil generated thousands of tons of debris and artefacts. The [Ertebølle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erteb%C3%B8lle_culture "Ertebølle culture") middens in Denmark, for example, accumulated 2,000 m3 (71,000 cu ft) of shell deposits representing some 50 million molluscs over only a thousand years. This intensification in the exploitation of marine resources has been described as accompanied by new technologies – such as boats, harpoons, and fish hooks  – because many caves found in the Mediterranean and on the European Atlantic coast have increased quantities of marine shells in their upper levels and reduced quantities in their lower. The earliest exploitation, however, took place on the now submerged shelves, and most settlements now excavated were then located several kilometers from these shelves. The reduced quantities of shells in the lower levels can represent the few shells that were exported inland.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-76)

### New World

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=22 "Edit section: New World")]

See also: [Brendan of Clonfert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_the_Navigator#Tale_of_reaching_North_America "Brendan the Navigator")

During the LGM the [Laurentide Ice Sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurentide_Ice_Sheet "Laurentide Ice Sheet") covered most of northern North America while [Beringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beringia "Beringia") connected Siberia to Alaska. In 1973, late American geoscientist [Paul S. Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Schultz_Martin "Paul Schultz Martin") proposed a "blitzkrieg" colonization of the Americas by which [Clovis hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovis_culture "Clovis culture") migrated into North America around 13,000 years ago in a single wave through an ice-free corridor in the ice sheet and "spread southward explosively, briefly attaining a density sufficiently large to overkill much of their prey."[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-77) Others later proposed a "three-wave" migration over the [Bering Land Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Land_Bridge "Bering Land Bridge").[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-78) These hypotheses remained the long-held view regarding the [settlement of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_migration_and_settlement_of_the_Americas_from_Asia "Prehistoric migration and settlement of the Americas from Asia"), a view challenged by more recent archaeological discoveries: the oldest archaeological sites in the Americas have been found in South America; sites in northeast Siberia report virtually no human presence there during the LGM; and most Clovis artefacts have been found in eastern North America along the Atlantic coast.[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-79) Furthermore, colonisation models based on mtDNA, [yDNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YDNA "YDNA"), and [atDNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AtDNA "AtDNA") data respectively support neither the "blitzkrieg" nor the "three-wave" hypotheses but they also deliver mutually ambiguous results. Contradictory data from archaeology and genetics will most likely deliver future hypotheses that will, eventually, confirm each other.[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-80) A proposed route across the Pacific to South America could explain early South American finds and another hypothesis proposes a northern path, through the Canadian Arctic and down the North American Atlantic coast.[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-81) Early settlements across the Atlantic have been suggested by alternative theories, ranging from purely hypothetical to mostly disputed, including the [Solutrean hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solutrean_hypothesis "Solutrean hypothesis") and some of the [Pre-Columbian trans-oceanic contact theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_trans-oceanic_contact_theories "Pre-Columbian trans-oceanic contact theories").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Norse-world.png/220px-Norse-world.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Norse-world.png)

Based on the medieval *[Íslendingasögur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagas_of_Icelanders "Sagas of Icelanders")* sagas, including the *[Grœnlendinga saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_saga "Greenland saga")*, this interpretative map of the "Norse World" shows that Norse knowledge of the Americas and the Atlantic remained limited.

The [Norse settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_colonization_of_North_America "Norse colonization of North America") of the [Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroe_Islands "Faroe Islands") and [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland "Iceland") began during the 9th and 10th centuries. A settlement on [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland "Greenland") was established before 1000 CE, but contact with it was lost in 1409 and it was finally abandoned during the early [Little Ice Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Ice_Age "Little Ice Age"). This setback was caused by a range of factors: an unsustainable economy resulted in erosion and denudation, while conflicts with the local [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit "Inuit") resulted in the failure to adapt their Arctic technologies; a colder climate resulted in starvation, and the colony got economically marginalized as the [Great Plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plague "Great Plague") harvested its victims on Iceland in the 15th century.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-82) Iceland was initially settled 865–930 CE following a warm period when winter temperatures hovered around 2 °C (36 °F) which made farming favorable at high latitudes. This did not last, however, and temperatures quickly dropped; at 1080 CE summer temperatures had reached a maximum of 5 °C (41 °F). The *[Landnámabók](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landn%C3%A1mab%C3%B3k "Landnámabók")* (*Book of Settlement*) records disastrous famines during the first century of settlement – "men ate foxes and ravens" and "the old and helpless were killed and thrown over cliffs" – and by the early 1200s hay had to be abandoned for short-season crops such as [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley "Barley").[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-83)

### Atlantic World

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=23 "Edit section: Atlantic World")]

Main articles: [Atlantic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_world "Atlantic world") and [Atlantic history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_history "Atlantic history")

See also: [List of notable crossings of the Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_notable_crossings_of_the_Atlantic_Ocean "List of notable crossings of the Atlantic Ocean") and [Battle of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic "Battle of the Atlantic")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Map_of_Portuguese_Carreira_da_India.gif/220px-Map_of_Portuguese_Carreira_da_India.gif)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Map_of_Portuguese_Carreira_da_India.gif)

The Atlantic [Gyres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyre "Gyre") influenced the [Portuguese discoveries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_discoveries "Portuguese discoveries") and trading port routes, here shown in the India Run ("*Carreira da Índia*"), which would be developed in subsequent years.

[Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus") [reached the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyages_of_Christopher_Columbus "Voyages of Christopher Columbus") in 1492, sailing under the Spanish flag.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Chamb-184-84) Six years later [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama "Vasco da Gama") reached India under the Portuguese flag, by navigating south around the [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope "Cape of Good Hope"), thus proving that the Atlantic and Indian Oceans are connected. In 1500, in his voyage to India following Vasco da Gama, [Pedro Álvares Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_%C3%81lvares_Cabral "Pedro Álvares Cabral") reached Brazil, taken by the currents of the [South Atlantic Gyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Gyre "South Atlantic Gyre"). Following these explorations, Spain and Portugal quickly [conquered and colonized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization_of_the_Americas "Colonization of the Americas") large territories in the New World and forced the Amerindian population into slavery in order to exploit the vast quantities of silver and gold they found. Spain and Portugal monopolized this trade in order to keep other European nations out, but conflicting interests nevertheless led to a series of Spanish-Portuguese wars. A peace treaty mediated by the Pope divided the conquered territories into Spanish and Portuguese sectors while keeping other colonial powers away. England, France, and the Dutch Republic enviously watched the Spanish and Portuguese wealth grow and allied themselves with [pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_the_Atlantic_World "Piracy in the Atlantic World") such as [Henry Mainwaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Mainwaring "Henry Mainwaring") and [Alexandre Exquemelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Exquemelin "Alexandre Exquemelin"). They could explore the convoys leaving the Americas because prevailing winds and currents made the transport of heavy metals slow and predictable.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Chamb-184-84)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Atlantic_slavetrade_estimates.png/220px-Atlantic_slavetrade_estimates.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Atlantic_slavetrade_estimates.png)

Embarked and disembarked slaves in the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade") 1525–1863 (first and last slave voyages)

In the colonies of the Americas, depredation, [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox "Smallpox") and other diseases, and [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery "Slavery") quickly reduced the [indigenous population of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Indigenous peoples of the Americas") to the extent that the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade") had to be introduced to replace them – a trade that became the norm and an integral part of the colonization. Between the 15th century and 1888, when [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Brazil "Slavery in Brazil") became the last part of the Americas to end the slave trade, an estimated ten million Africans were exported as slaves, most of them destined for agricultural labour. The slave trade was officially abolished in the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_British_Isles "Slavery in the British Isles") and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States "Slavery in the United States") in 1808, and slavery itself was abolished in the British Empire in 1838 and in the United States in 1865 after the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War").[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-85)[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-86)

From Columbus to the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") trans-Atlantic trade, including colonialism and slavery, became crucial for Western Europe. For European countries with direct access to the Atlantic (including Britain, France, the Netherlands, Portugal, and Spain) 1500–1800 was a period of sustained growth during which these countries grew richer than those in Eastern Europe and Asia. Colonialism evolved as part of the trans-Atlantic trade, but this trade also strengthened the position of merchant groups at the expense of monarchs. Growth was more rapid in non-absolutist countries, such as Britain and the Netherlands, and more limited in [absolutist monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarchy "Absolute monarchy"), such as Portugal, Spain, and France, where profit mostly or exclusively benefited the monarchy and its allies.[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Acem-p546-87)

Trans-Atlantic trade also resulted in increasing urbanization: in European countries facing the Atlantic, urbanization grew from 8% in 1300, 10.1% in 1500, to 24.5% in 1850; in other European countries from 10% in 1300, 11.4% in 1500, to 17% in 1850. Likewise, GDP doubled in Atlantic countries but rose by only 30% in the rest of Europe. By the end of the 17th century, the volume of the Trans-Atlantic trade had surpassed that of the Mediterranean trade.[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Acem-p546-87)

## Economy

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=24 "Edit section: Economy")]

The Atlantic has contributed significantly to the development and economy of surrounding countries. Besides major transatlantic transportation and communication routes, the Atlantic offers abundant petroleum deposits in the [sedimentary rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock "Sedimentary rock") of the continental shelves.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Kanstadsamlingen_-_NMF010005-01347.jpg/220px-Kanstadsamlingen_-_NMF010005-01347.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kanstadsamlingen_-_NMF010005-01347.jpg)

[Cod fishery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cod_fisheries "Cod fisheries") in Norway

The Atlantic harbors petroleum and gas fields, fish, [marine mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_mammal "Marine mammal") ([seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinniped "Pinniped") and whales), [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand "Sand") and [gravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel "Gravel") aggregates, [placer deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placer_deposit "Placer deposit"), [polymetallic nodules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymetallic_nodules "Polymetallic nodules"), and precious stones.[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-88) Gold deposits are a mile or two underwater on the ocean floor, however, the deposits are also encased in rock that must be mined through. Currently, there is no cost-effective way to mine or extract gold from the ocean to make a profit.[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-89)

Various international treaties attempt to reduce pollution caused by environmental threats such as oil spills, [marine debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_debris "Marine debris"), and the [incineration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incineration "Incineration") of toxic wastes at sea.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25)

### Fisheries

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=25 "Edit section: Fisheries")]

See also: [Ocean fisheries § Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_fisheries#Atlantic_Ocean "Ocean fisheries"), and [Fishing down the food web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_down_the_food_web "Fishing down the food web")

The [shelves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf "Continental shelf") of the Atlantic hosts one of the world's richest [fishing resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_fisheries "Wild fisheries"). The most productive areas include the [Grand Banks of Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Banks_of_Newfoundland "Grand Banks of Newfoundland"), the [Scotian Shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotian_Shelf "Scotian Shelf"), [Georges Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Bank "Georges Bank") off [Cape Cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod "Cape Cod"), the [Bahama Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahama_Banks "Bahama Banks"), the waters around Iceland, the [Irish Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea "Irish Sea"), the [Bay of Fundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Fundy "Bay of Fundy"), the [Dogger Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogger_Bank "Dogger Bank") of the North Sea, and the Falkland Banks.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-USN-2001-25) Fisheries have, however, undergone significant changes since the 1950s and global catches can now be divided into three groups of which only two are observed in the Atlantic: fisheries in the eastern-central and southwest Atlantic oscillate around a globally stable value, the rest of the Atlantic is in overall decline following historical peaks. The third group, "continuously increasing trend since 1950", is only found in the Indian Ocean and western Pacific.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-NOA-2016-p39-90)

Banks of the northwest Atlantic

- Northwest Atlantic

  In the Northwest Atlantic landings have decreased from 4.2 million tons in the early 1970s to 1.9 million tons in 2013. During the 21st century, some species have shown weak signs of recovery, including [Greenland halibut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_halibut "Greenland halibut"), [yellowtail flounder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowtail_flounder "Yellowtail flounder"), [Atlantic halibut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_halibut "Atlantic halibut"), [haddock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haddock "Haddock"), [spiny dogfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiny_dogfish "Spiny dogfish"), while other stocks shown no such signs, including cod, [witch flounder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witch_\(righteye_flounder\) "Witch (righteye flounder)"), and redfish. Stocks of invertebrates, in contrast, remain at record levels of abundance. 31% of stocks are overfished in the northwest Atlantic.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-NOA-2016-p39-90)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Time_series_for_collapse_of_Atlantic_northwest_cod.png/220px-Time_series_for_collapse_of_Atlantic_northwest_cod.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Time_series_for_collapse_of_Atlantic_northwest_cod.png)

Capture of Atlantic northwest cod in million tons

In 1497, [John Cabot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cabot "John Cabot") became the first [Western European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European "Western European") since the [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings "Vikings") to explore mainland North America and one of his major discoveries was the abundant resources of [Atlantic cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_cod "Atlantic cod") off [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland "Newfoundland"). Referred to as "Newfoundland Currency" this discovery yielded some 200 million tons of fish over five centuries. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, new fisheries started to exploit [haddock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haddock "Haddock"), [mackerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackerel "Mackerel"), and [lobster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobster "Lobster"). From the 1950s to the 1970s, the introduction of European and Asian distant-water fleets in the area dramatically increased the fishing capacity and the number of exploited species. It also expanded the exploited areas from near-shore to the open sea and to great depths to include deep-water species such as [redfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastes "Sebastes"), [Greenland halibut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_halibut "Greenland halibut"), witch flounder, and [grenadiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenadiers_\(fish\) "Grenadiers (fish)"). [Overfishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfishing "Overfishing") in the area was recognized as early as the 1960s but, because this was occurring on [international waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_waters "International waters"), it took until the late 1970s before any attempts to regulate was made. In the early 1990s, this finally resulted in the [collapse of the Atlantic northwest cod fishery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_the_Atlantic_northwest_cod_fishery "Collapse of the Atlantic northwest cod fishery"). The population of a number of deep-sea fishes also collapsed in the process, including [American plaice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_plaice "American plaice"), redfish, and Greenland halibut, together with flounder and grenadier.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-92)

- Eastern central-Atlantic

  In the eastern central-Atlantic small [pelagic fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelagic_fish "Pelagic fish") constitute about 50% of landings with sardine reaching 0.6–1.0 million tons per year. Pelagic fish stocks are considered fully fished or overfished, with sardines south of [Cape Bojador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Bojador "Cape Bojador") the notable exception. Almost half of the stocks are fished at biologically unsustainable levels. Total catches have been fluctuating since the 1970s; reaching 3.9 million tons in 2013 or slightly less than the peak production in 2010.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-NOA-2016-p39-90)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Bahamas.A2001138.1550.250m.jpg/170px-Bahamas.A2001138.1550.250m.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bahamas.A2001138.1550.250m.jpg)

[Bahama Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahama_Banks "Bahama Banks")

- Western central-Atlantic

  In the western central-Atlantic, catches have been decreasing since 2000 and reached 1.3 million tons in 2013. The most important species in the area, [Gulf menhaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_menhaden "Gulf menhaden"), reached a million tons in the mid-1980s but only half a million tons in 2013 and is now considered fully fished. [Round sardinella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_sardinella "Round sardinella") was an important species in the 1990s but is now considered overfished. [Groupers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouper "Grouper") and [snappers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutjanidae "Lutjanidae") are overfished and [northern brown shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_brown_shrimp "Northern brown shrimp") and [American cupped oyster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_oyster "Eastern oyster") are considered fully fished approaching overfished. 44% of stocks are being fished at unsustainable levels.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-NOA-2016-p39-90)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Agulhas_Bank_NOAA_ETOPO1.jpg/220px-Agulhas_Bank_NOAA_ETOPO1.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Agulhas_Bank_NOAA_ETOPO1.jpg)

[Agulhas Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agulhas_Bank "Agulhas Bank")

- Southeast Atlantic

  In the southeast Atlantic catches have decreased from 3.3 million tons in the early 1970s to 1.3 million tons in 2013. [Horse mackerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_mackerel "Horse mackerel") and [hake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hake "Hake") are the most important species, together representing almost half of the landings. Off South Africa and Namibia [deep-water hake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merluccius_paradoxus "Merluccius paradoxus") and [shallow-water Cape hake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merluccius_capensis "Merluccius capensis") have recovered to sustainable levels since regulations were introduced in 2006 and the states of [southern African pilchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_pilchard "Southern African pilchard") and [anchovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_anchovy "Southern African anchovy") have improved to fully fished in 2013.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-NOA-2016-p39-90)

* Southwest Atlantic

  In the southwest Atlantic, a peak was reached in the mid-1980s and catches now fluctuate between 1.7 and 2.6 million tons. The most important species, the [Argentine shortfin squid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illex_argentinus "Illex argentinus"), which reached half a million tons in 2013 or half the peak value, is considered fully fished to overfished. Another important species was the [Brazilian sardinella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_sardinella "Brazilian sardinella"), with a production of 100,000 tons in 2013 it is now considered overfished. Half the stocks in this area are being fished at unsustainable levels: [Whitehead's round herring](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Whitehead%27s_round_herring\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Whitehead's round herring (page does not exist)") has not yet reached fully fished but [Cunene horse mackerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunene_horse_mackerel "Cunene horse mackerel") is overfished. The sea snail [perlemoen abalone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haliotis_midae "Haliotis midae") is targeted by [illegal fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_fishing "Illegal fishing") and remains overfished.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-NOA-2016-p39-90)

### Waste and pollution

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=28 "Edit section: Waste and pollution")]

[Marine pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_pollution "Marine pollution") is a generic term for the entry into the ocean of potentially hazardous chemicals or particles. The biggest culprits are rivers and with them many agriculture [fertilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilizer "Fertilizer") chemicals as well as livestock and human waste. The excess of oxygen-depleting chemicals leads to [hypoxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoxia_\(environmental\) "Hypoxia (environmental)") and the creation of a [dead zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_zone_\(ecology\) "Dead zone (ecology)").[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-94)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Nesocichla_eremita_-Inaccessible_Island%2C_British_overseas_territory-8_%283%29.jpg/220px-Nesocichla_eremita_-Inaccessible_Island%2C_British_overseas_territory-8_%283%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nesocichla_eremita_-Inaccessible_Island,_British_overseas_territory-8_\(3\).jpg)

[Marine debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_debris "Marine debris") strewn over the beaches of the South Atlantic [Inaccessible Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inaccessible_Island "Inaccessible Island")

[Marine debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_debris "Marine debris"), which is also known as marine litter, describes human-created waste floating in a body of water. Oceanic debris tends to accumulate at the center of [gyres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_gyres "Oceanic gyres") and coastlines, frequently washing aground where it is known as beach litter. The [North Atlantic garbage patch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_garbage_patch "North Atlantic garbage patch") is estimated to be hundreds of kilometers across in size.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-95)

Other pollution concerns include agricultural and municipal waste. Municipal pollution comes from the eastern United States, southern Brazil, and eastern Argentina; [oil pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_pollution "Oil pollution") in the [Caribbean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Sea "Caribbean Sea"), [Gulf of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico "Gulf of Mexico"), [Lake Maracaibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maracaibo "Lake Maracaibo"), [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea "Mediterranean Sea"), and [North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea "North Sea"); and industrial waste and municipal sewage pollution in the Baltic Sea, North Sea, and Mediterranean Sea.

A USAF [C-124](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-124_Globemaster_II "C-124 Globemaster II") aircraft from [Dover Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_Air_Force_Base "Dover Air Force Base"), Delaware was carrying three [nuclear bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_bombs "Nuclear bombs") over the Atlantic Ocean when it experienced a loss of power. For their own safety, the crew jettisoned two nuclear bombs, which were never recovered.[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-96)

### Climate change

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=29 "Edit section: Climate change")]

See also: [Shutdown of thermohaline circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shutdown_of_thermohaline_circulation "Shutdown of thermohaline circulation")

North Atlantic hurricane activity has increased over past decades because of increased [sea surface temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_surface_temperature "Sea surface temperature") (SST) at tropical latitudes, changes that can be attributed to either the natural [Atlantic Multidecadal Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Multidecadal_Oscillation "Atlantic Multidecadal Oscillation") (AMO) or to [anthropogenic climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropogenic_climate_change "Anthropogenic climate change").[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Mann-97) A 2005 report indicated that the Atlantic [meridional overturning circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridional_overturning_circulation "Meridional overturning circulation") (AMOC) slowed down by 30% between 1957 and 2004.[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-98) In 2024, the research highlighted a significant weakening of the AMOC by approximately 12% over the past two decades.[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-99) If the AMO were responsible for SST variability, the AMOC would have increased in strength, which is apparently not the case. Furthermore, it is clear from statistical analyses of annual tropical cyclones that these changes do not display multidecadal cyclicity.[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Mann-97) Therefore, these changes in SST must be caused by human activities.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-100)

The ocean [mixed layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_layer "Mixed layer") plays an important role in heat storage over seasonal and decadal time scales, whereas deeper layers are affected over millennia and have a heat capacity about 50 times that of the mixed layer. This heat uptake provides a time-lag for climate change but it also results in thermal expansion of the oceans which contributes to [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise "Sea level rise"). 21st-century global warming will probably result in an [equilibrium sea-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_level "Equilibrium level") rise five times greater than today, whilst melting of glaciers, including that of the Greenland ice sheet, expected to have virtually no effect during the 21st century, will likely result in a sea-level rise of 3–6 metres (9.8–19.7 ft) over a millennium.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-101)

## Theories of natural delimitation between the Atlantic and Pacific oceans

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Atlantic_Ocean\&action=edit\&section=30 "Edit section: Theories of natural delimitation between the Atlantic and Pacific oceans")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Natural_delimitation_between_the_Pacific_and_South_Atlantic_oceans_by_the_Shackleton_fracture_zone.svg/300px-Natural_delimitation_between_the_Pacific_and_South_Atlantic_oceans_by_the_Shackleton_fracture_zone.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Natural_delimitation_between_the_Pacific_and_South_Atlantic_oceans_by_the_Shackleton_fracture_zone.svg)

Map showing the proposal presented by the thesis entitled "[Natural delimitation between the Pacific and South Atlantic oceans by the Shackleton Fracture Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_delimitation_between_the_Pacific_and_South_Atlantic_oceans_by_the_Shackleton_Fracture_Zone "Natural delimitation between the Pacific and South Atlantic oceans by the Shackleton Fracture Zone")"

Scientific researchers have proposed delimiting the boundary between the Atlantic and Pacific oceans by two different natural boundaries, [by the Shackleton fracture zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_delimitation_between_the_Pacific_and_South_Atlantic_oceans_by_the_Shackleton_Fracture_Zone "Natural delimitation between the Pacific and South Atlantic oceans by the Shackleton Fracture Zone")[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-102) and [by the Scotia Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_delimitation_between_the_Pacific_and_South_Atlantic_oceans_by_the_Scotia_Arc "Natural delimitation between the Pacific and South Atlantic oceans by the Scotia Arc")[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-103)[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-104)[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-105) the former being more current than the latter.
