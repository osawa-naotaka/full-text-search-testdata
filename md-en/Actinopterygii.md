**Actinopterygii** ([/ˌæktɪnɒptəˈrɪdʒiaɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language "Ancient Greek language") **[ἀκτίς](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%80%CE%BA%CF%84%CE%AF%CF%82#Ancient_Greek "wikt:ἀκτίς")*&#x20;(aktis)* 'having rays' and **[πτέρυξ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CF%80%CF%84%CE%AD%CF%81%CF%85%CE%BE#Ancient_Greek "wikt:πτέρυξ")*&#x20;(ptérux)* 'wing, fins'), members of which are known as **ray-finned fish** or **actinopterygians**, is a [class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_\(biology\) "Class (biology)") of [bony fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteichthyes "Osteichthyes")[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-2) that comprise over 50% of living [vertebrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate "Vertebrate") species.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-NelsonFoW-3) They are so called because of their lightly built [fins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_fin "Fish fin") made of webbings of [skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin "Skin") supported by radially extended thin bony [spines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spine_\(zoology\) "Spine (zoology)") called *[lepidotrichia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepidotrichia "Lepidotrichia")*, as opposed to the bulkier, fleshy lobed fins of the [sister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_taxon "Sister taxon") class [Sarcopterygii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcopterygii "Sarcopterygii") (lobe-finned fish). Resembling [folding fans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folding_fan "Folding fan"), the actinopterygian fins can easily change shape and [wetted area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetted_area "Wetted area"), providing superior [thrust-to-weight ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust-to-weight_ratio "Thrust-to-weight ratio") per movement compared to sarcopterygian and [chondrichthyian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chondrichthyian "Chondrichthyian") fins. The fin rays attach directly to the proximal or basal skeletal elements, the radials, which represent the [articulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articulation_\(anatomy\) "Articulation (anatomy)") between these fins and the internal skeleton (e.g., pelvic and pectoral girdles).

The vast majority of actinopterygians are [teleosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleost "Teleost"). By [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species") count, they dominate the [subphylum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subphylum "Subphylum") [Vertebrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrata "Vertebrata"), and constitute nearly 99% of the over 30,000 [extant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extant_taxon "Extant taxon") species of [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish "Fish").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-4) They are the most abundant [nektonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nektonic "Nektonic") [aquatic animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_animal "Aquatic animal") and are ubiquitous throughout [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater "Freshwater") and [marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean "Ocean") environments from the [deep sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_sea "Deep sea") to [subterranean waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subterranean_river "Subterranean river") to the highest [mountain streams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_stream "Mountain stream"). Extant species can range in size from *[Paedocypris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paedocypris "Paedocypris")*, at 8 mm (0.3 in); to the massive [ocean sunfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_sunfish "Ocean sunfish"), at 2,300 kg (5,070 lb); and to the giant [oarfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oarfish "Oarfish"), at 11 m (36 ft). The largest ever known ray-finned fish, the extinct *[Leedsichthys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leedsichthys "Leedsichthys")* from the [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic "Jurassic"), has been estimated to have grown to 16.5 m (54 ft).

Ray-finned fishes occur in many variant forms. The main features of typical ray-finned fish are shown in the adjacent diagram.

The [swim bladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swim_bladder "Swim bladder") is a more derived structure and used for [buoyancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buoyancy "Buoyancy").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Funk-5) Except from the [bichirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bichir "Bichir"), which just like the [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung "Lung") of [lobe-finned fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobe-finned_fish "Lobe-finned fish") have retained the ancestral condition of ventral budding from the [foregut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foregut "Foregut"), the swim bladder in ray-finned fishes derives from a dorsal bud above the foregut.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-6)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Funk-5) In early forms the swim bladder could still be used for breathing, a trait still present in [Holostei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holostei "Holostei") ([bowfins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowfin "Bowfin") and [gars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gar "Gar")).[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7) In some fish like the [arapaima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arapaima "Arapaima"), the swim bladder has been modified for breathing air again,[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-8) and in other lineages it have been completely lost.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-9)

The teleosts have urinary and reproductive tracts that are fully separated, while the Chondrostei have common urogenital ducts, and partially connected ducts are found in Cladistia and Holostei.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-10)

Ray-finned fishes have many different types of [scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_scale "Fish scale"); but all [teleosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleost "Teleost") have [leptoid scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptoid_scale "Leptoid scale"). The outer part of these scales fan out with bony ridges, while the inner part is crossed with fibrous connective tissue. Leptoid scales are thinner and more transparent than other types of scales, and lack the hardened [enamel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tooth_enamel "Tooth enamel")- or [dentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dentine "Dentine")-like layers found in the scales of many other fish. Unlike [ganoid scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganoid_scale "Ganoid scale"), which are found in non-teleost actinopterygians, new scales are added in concentric layers as the fish grows.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-11)

Teleosts and chondrosteans (sturgeons and paddlefish) also differ from the bichirs and holosteans (bowfin and gars) in having gone through a whole-genome duplication ([paleopolyploidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleopolyploidy "Paleopolyploidy")). The WGD is estimated to have happened about 320 million years ago in the teleosts, which on average has retained about 17% of the gene duplicates, and around 180 (124–225) million years ago in the chondrosteans. It has since happened again in some teleost lineages, like Salmonidae (80–100 million years ago) and several times independently within the [Cyprinidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprinidae "Cyprinidae") (in goldfish and common carp as recently as 14 million years ago). ## Reproduction

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Actinopterygii\&action=edit\&section=3 "Edit section: Reproduction")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f3/Gasterosteus_aculeatus_1879.jpg/220px-Gasterosteus_aculeatus_1879.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gasterosteus_aculeatus_1879.jpg)

[Three-spined stickleback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-spined_stickleback "Three-spined stickleback") (*Gasterosteus aculeatus*) males (red belly) build nests and compete to attract females to lay eggs in them. Males then defend and fan the eggs. Painting by [Alexander Francis Lydon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Francis_Lydon "Alexander Francis Lydon"), 1879

In nearly all ray-finned fish, the sexes are separate, and in most species the females spawn eggs that are fertilized externally, typically with the male inseminating the eggs after they are laid. Development then proceeds with a free-swimming larval stage.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-17) However other patterns of [ontogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontogeny "Ontogeny") exist, with one of the commonest being [sequential hermaphroditism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequential_hermaphroditism "Sequential hermaphroditism"). In most cases this involves [protogyny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protogyny "Protogyny"), fish starting life as females and converting to males at some stage, triggered by some internal or external factor. [Protandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protandry "Protandry"), where a fish converts from male to female, is much less common than protogyny.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-18)

Most families use [external](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_fertilization "External fertilization") rather than [internal fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_fertilization "Internal fertilization").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Pitcher-19) Of the [oviparous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oviparity "Oviparity") teleosts, most (79%) do not provide parental care.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Reynolds-20) [Viviparity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viviparity "Viviparity"), [ovoviviparity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovoviviparity "Ovoviviparity"), or some form of parental care for eggs, whether by the male, the female, or both parents is seen in a significant fraction (21%) of the 422 teleost families; no care is likely the ancestral condition.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Reynolds-20) The oldest case of viviparity in ray-finned fish is found in [Middle Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Triassic "Middle Triassic") species of †*[Saurichthys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurichthys "Saurichthys")*.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-21) Viviparity is relatively rare and is found in about 6% of living teleost species; male care is far more common than female care.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Reynolds-20)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Clutton-Brock-22) Male territoriality ["preadapts"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exaptation "Exaptation") a species for evolving male parental care.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Werren-23)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Baylis-24)

There are a few examples of fish that self-fertilise. The [mangrove rivulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove_rivulus "Mangrove rivulus") is an amphibious, simultaneous hermaphrodite, producing both eggs and spawn and having internal fertilisation. This mode of reproduction may be related to the fish's habit of spending long periods out of water in the mangrove forests it inhabits. Males are occasionally produced at temperatures below 19 °C (66 °F) and can fertilise eggs that are then spawned by the female. This maintains genetic variability in a species that is otherwise highly inbred.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Wootton-25)

## Classification and fossil record

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Actinopterygii\&action=edit\&section=4 "Edit section: Classification and fossil record")]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Evolution_of_ray-finned_fish.png/500px-Evolution_of_ray-finned_fish.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Evolution_of_ray-finned_fish.png)

See also: [Evolution of fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_fish "Evolution of fish")

Actinopterygii is divided into the subclasses [Cladistia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cladistia "Cladistia"),[Chondrostei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chondrostei "Chondrostei") and [Neopterygii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neopterygii "Neopterygii"). The [Neopterygii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neopterygii "Neopterygii"), in turn, is divided into the infraclasses [Holostei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holostei "Holostei") and [Teleostei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleostei "Teleostei"). During the [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic "Mesozoic") ([Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic "Triassic"), [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic "Jurassic"), [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous "Cretaceous")) and [Cenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic "Cenozoic") the teleosts in particular [diversified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_fish#Post_Devonian "Evolution of fish") widely. As a result, 96% of living fish species are teleosts (40% of all fish species belong to the teleost subgroup [Acanthomorpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acanthomorpha "Acanthomorpha")), while all other groups of actinopterygians represent depauperate lineages.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Sallan_2014-26)

The classification of ray-finned fishes can be summarized as follows:

- Cladistia, which include bichirs and reedfish

- Actinopteri, which include:

  - Chondrostei, which include Acipenseriformes (paddlefishes and sturgeons)

  - Neopterygii, which include:

    - Teleostei (most living fishes)

    - Holostei, which include:

      - Lepisosteiformes (gars)
      - Amiiformes (bowfin)

The [cladogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cladogram "Cladogram") below shows the main [clades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade "Clade") of living actinopterygians and their evolutionary relationships to other [extant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extant_taxon "Extant taxon") groups of [fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishes "Fishes") and the four-limbed vertebrates ([tetrapods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrapods "Tetrapods")).[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PNAS-27)[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-TOL-28) The latter include mostly terrestrial [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species") but also groups that became [secondarily aquatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondarily_aquatic_tetrapods "Secondarily aquatic tetrapods") (e.g. [whales and dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetacea "Cetacea")). Tetrapods [evolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_tetrapods "Evolution of tetrapods") from a group of [bony fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bony_fish "Bony fish") during the [Devonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonian "Devonian") [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_\(geology\) "Period (geology)").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-laurin\&reisz1995-29) Approximate [divergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergent_evolution "Divergent evolution") dates for the different actinopterygian clades (in [millions of years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myr "Myr"), mya) are from Near et al., 2012.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PNAS-27)

The polypterids (bichirs and reedfish) are the [sister lineage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_group "Sister group") of all other actinopterygians, the Acipenseriformes (sturgeons and paddlefishes) are the sister lineage of Neopterygii, and Holostei (bowfin and gars) are the sister lineage of teleosts. The [Elopomorpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elopomorpha "Elopomorpha") ([eels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eel "Eel") and [tarpons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarpon "Tarpon")) appear to be the most [basal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_\(phylogenetics\) "Basal (phylogenetics)") teleosts.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PNAS-27)

The earliest known [fossil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil "Fossil") actinopterygian is *[Andreolepis hedei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreolepis_hedei "Andreolepis hedei")*, dating back 420 million years ([Late Silurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Silurian "Late Silurian")), remains of which have been found in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia"), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden"), and [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia "Estonia").[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-30) Crown group actinopterygians most likely originated near the Devonian-Carboniferous boundary.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-HendersonDunneFaseyGiles2022-31) The earliest fossil relatives of modern teleosts are from the [Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic "Triassic") [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_\(geology\) "Period (geology)") (*[Prohalecites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohalecites "Prohalecites")*, *[Pholidophorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pholidophorus "Pholidophorus")*),[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Arratia_2015-32)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Romano_et_al_2016-33) although it is suspected that teleosts originated already during the [Paleozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleozoic "Paleozoic") [Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era_\(geology\) "Era (geology)").[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-PNAS-27)
