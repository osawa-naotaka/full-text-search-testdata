**Agnostida** are an [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_\(biology\) "Order (biology)") of extinct [arthropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod "Arthropod") which have classically been seen as a group of highly modified [trilobites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilobite "Trilobite"), though some recent research has doubted this placement. Regardless, they appear to be close relatives as part of the [Artiopoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artiopoda "Artiopoda").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-:0-1) They are present in the [Lower Cambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Cambrian "Lower Cambrian") fossil record along with [trilobites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilobites "Trilobites") from the [Redlichiida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redlichiida "Redlichiida"), [Corynexochida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corynexochida "Corynexochida"), and [Ptychopariida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptychopariida "Ptychopariida") orders, and were highly diverse throughout the [Cambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian "Cambrian"). Agnostidan diversity severely declined during the Cambrian-[Ordovician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordovician "Ordovician") transition, and the last agnostidans went [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct "Extinct") in the Late Ordovician.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-2)

## Systematics

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Agnostida\&action=edit\&section=1 "Edit section: Systematics")]

The Agnostida are divided into two suborders — [Agnostina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnostina "Agnostina") and [Eodiscina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eodiscina "Eodiscina") — which are then subdivided into a number of [families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_\(biology\) "Family (biology)"). As a group, agnostids are isopygous, meaning their [pygidium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygidium "Pygidium") is similar in size and shape to their [cephalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalon_\(arthropod_anatomy\) "Cephalon (arthropod anatomy)"). Most agnostid species were eyeless.

The systematic position of the order Agnostida within the class Trilobita remains uncertain, and there has been continuing debate whether they are trilobites or a [stem group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_group "Stem group"). The challenge to the status has focused on Agnostina partly due to the [juveniles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juvenile_\(organism\) "Juvenile (organism)") of one genus have been found with legs differing dramatically from those of adult trilobites,[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-3) suggesting they are not members of the [lamellipedian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamellipedia "Lamellipedia") [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade "Clade"), of which trilobites are a part. Instead, the limbs of agnostids closely resemble those of stem group crustaceans, although they lack the [proximal endite](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Proximal_endite\&action=edit\&redlink=1 "Proximal endite (page does not exist)"), which defines that group. The study suggested that they were likely the [sister taxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_taxon "Sister taxon") to the crustacean stem lineage, and, as such, part of the clade, [Crustaceomorpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustaceomorpha "Crustaceomorpha").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-4) Other researchers have suggested, based on a [cladistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cladistic "Cladistic") analyses of [dorsal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsum_\(anatomy\) "Dorsum (anatomy)") [exoskeletal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoskeleton "Exoskeleton") features, that Eodiscina and Agnostida are closely united, and the Eodiscina descended from the trilobite order [Ptychopariida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptychopariida "Ptychopariida").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-5) A 2019 study of adult specimens with preserved soft tissue from the [Burgess Shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgess_Shale "Burgess Shale") found that agnostidans shared morphological similarities to trilobites and other related [artiopodans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artiopodans "Artiopodans") like [nektaspids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nektaspida "Nektaspida"), and their placement as stem-crustaceans was unsupported. The study recovered agnostidans as the sister group to other trilobites within the Artiopoda.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-:0-1)

## Ecology

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Agnostida\&action=edit\&section=2 "Edit section: Ecology")]

Scientists have long debated whether the agnostids lived a [pelagic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelagic "Pelagic") or a [benthic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benthic "Benthic") lifestyle. Their lack of eyes, a morphology not well-suited for swimming, and their fossils found in association with other benthic trilobites suggest a benthic (bottom-dwelling) mode of life. They are likely to have lived on areas of the ocean floor which received little or no light and fed on [detritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detritus "Detritus") which descended from upper layers of the sea to the bottom. Their wide geographic dispersion in the [fossil record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_record "Fossil record") is uncharacteristic of benthic animals, suggesting a pelagic existence. The thoracic segment appears to form a hinge between the head and pygidium allowing for a bivalved [ostracodan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostracod "Ostracod")-type lifestyle. The orientation of the thoracic appendages appears ill-suited for benthic living. Recent work suggests that some agnostids were benthic predators, engaging in cannibalism and possibly pack-hunting behavior.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-6)

They are sometimes preserved within the voids of other organisms, for instance within empty [hyolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyolith "Hyolith") conchs,[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-7) within [sponges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge "Sponge"), [worm tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selkirkia "Selkirkia") and under the carapaces of [bivalved arthropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidneyia "Sidneyia"),[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Chatterton_2003-8) presumably in order to hide from predators or strong storm currents; or maybe whilst scavenging for food.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Chatterton_2003-8) In the case of the tapering worm tubes *[Selkirkia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selkirkia "Selkirkia")*, trilobites are always found with their heads directed towards the opening of the tube, suggesting that they reversed in; the absence of any moulted carapaces suggests that moulting was not their primary reason for seeking shelter.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#cite_note-Chatterton_2003-8)
